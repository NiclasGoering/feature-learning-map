- RG record: sense (c), neural-network field theory with a Wilsonian reading: the infinite-width Gaussian action $S_*=S_\infty$ (Eq. (2), (9)) is called a fixed point and the finite-width terms $\Delta S_N$ (Eqs. (4), (5)) perturbations. Nothing is integrated out or coarse-grained: no RG transformation or scale $\ell$ is constructed, and Sec. 4 lists coarse-graining in function space (eigenfunctions, data geometry, layers) as unsolved. What "flows" is a generic coupling set $g_i$ with $dg_i/d\ell=\beta_i$ (Eq. (10)), linearised to $y_ig_i$ (Eqs. (11), (34)) with the schematic $\beta=yg-ag^2$ (Eq. (35), Fig. 7); the only network-specific width laws are $\kappa_4[f_N]=\kappa_4[z]/N$ (Eq. (24), independent features) and the assumed $g_i(N)\sim N^{-\alpha_i}$ (Eq. (36)), and no $\beta_i$, $y_i$ or $\alpha_i$ is computed for any network. Maturity: interpretation and analogy (Sec. 2 calls the framework purely conceptual), with standard results restated as the only derived content (Eqs. (19), (24), (25), (29)-(31)); at most first order in $1/N$, no loop or non-perturbative calculation.
- Read: the full arXiv HTML v1 (Secs. 1-5, all eight figures, 30-page PDF, no appendix); equation numbers checked against the PDF and agree. Derived: Eqs. (24), (25) (cumulants of a normalised sum of independent terms, textbook) and Eqs. (29)-(31) (ReLU sensitivity, the known edge-of-chaos result of the cited refs [9, 10]). Stated without derivation: the operator expansion Eqs. (5)/(21) (no $\lambda_k^{(N)}$ is given for any architecture), Eq. (8) ($\alpha_n$ unspecified), Eq. (19) (called "expected"), Eqs. (36)-(38). Analogy or interpretation: the relevant/marginal/irrelevant classification (Eqs. (9)-(15), (32)-(35)), criticality as a "controlled" regime, overparameterisation as functional simplicity, generalisation and overfitting as suppression or growth of couplings, architectural universality classes (Secs. 2, 4). The paper itself says the framework is purely conceptual yet not merely metaphorical (Sec. 2) and not a loose analogy (Sec. 4); this entry records only what is computed versus assumed.
- Inconsistencies and gaps: (i) width $N$ and the RG scale $\ell$ are never related (Eq. (32) writes $g_i(N)$, Eq. (33) flows in $\ell$), so the "flow" is not tied to width, depth or any coarse-graining; (ii) the abstract says the critical structure of finite networks "is given by finite-width effects", but no result shows it, and the edge-of-chaos part (Eqs. (29)-(31)) is infinite-width, depth-wise signal propagation, linked to finite-width operators only by assertion; (iii) the leading cumulant $\propto 1/N$ (Eq. (24)) is suppressed with width, so it is irrelevant in the paper's own language, and no relevant or marginal finite-width operator is exhibited.
- Figures: Figs. 7 and 8 are schematic by the paper's own statement (Eq. (35) with hand-picked $y$, $a$; Fig. 7a shows exponential flows for $y=-0.6, 0, 0.6$). In Fig. 3 the points lie exactly on the reference line and the legends read $\mathcal Q_4(N)=\mathcal Q_4(1)/N$ and $|\gamma_2(N)|=|\gamma_2(1)|/N$, so it looks like a plot of the law rather than sampled networks; the paper does not say which. Figs. 1, 2, 4 are Monte Carlo for a random-feature ReLU network, Eq. (16); the law of $a_j$, $w_j$, the number of draws, the input set (about 24 inputs from the Fig. 2 axes) and the input used in Fig. 4 are not stated. `N` range 20 to 2560 is read off the log axes. The Q-Q panel of Fig. 4 and Fig. 2 are not recorded as results.
- `learning_rule: bayes` because the object is the function-space prior at initialisation (as for other NNGP-prior entries); no posterior or training is computed. `moves_mean: unknown` throughout because no predictor is computed. `contribution: theory`: the numerics are small checks of the central-limit scalings, not tests of the Wilsonian claim.
