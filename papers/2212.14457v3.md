one_line: "Exact zero-noise posterior and evidence of deep linear networks via Meijer-G functions; with data-agnostic priors, large effective depth $LP/N$ yields the evidence-maximising posterior."
summary: "Computes the zero-noise (interpolating) Bayesian posterior of deep linear networks with scalar output and iid Gaussian weight priors, for arbitrary full-rank data, depth, widths and $P\\le N_0$. The predictive posterior and model evidence are exact Meijer-G functions; the posterior mean is always the minimum-norm interpolant $\\theta_*$, and depth acts only on the variance along input directions orthogonal to the training data. Laplace-method expansions with $P/N_0\\to\\alpha_0\\in(0,1)$ treat fixed depth with $P/N\\to\\alpha$, depth proportional to width, and $L,P,N\\to\\infty$ at fixed effective posterior depth $\\lambda_{\\rm post}=LP/N$. Evidence-maximising priors give a Gaussian posterior with orthogonal-direction scale $\\nu=\\|\\theta_*\\|^2/\\alpha_0$; at finite depth this needs a data-dependent prior variance, whereas at $\\sigma^2=1$ it is reached when $L/N$ is fixed or as $\\lambda_{\\rm post}\\to\\infty$, and evidence grows with $\\lambda_{\\rm post}$. Corollaries give a $1/N$ variance-limited scaling law and double descent in $\\alpha_0$."

- Equation numbers in Sec. 3–4 differ between the arXiv HTML rendering and the PDF of v3 (e.g. the data assumption $\|\theta_{*,N_0}\|\to\|\theta_*\|$ is (3.5) in the PDF and (3.7) in the HTML). Theorem, corollary and section numbers agree, so all locations cite those; anchors are HTML ids.
- Effective depth: $\lambda_{\rm prior}=\sum_\ell 1/N_\ell$ ($=L/N$ at equal widths, Eq. (2.7)) and $\lambda_{\rm post}=\sum_\ell P/N_\ell$ ($=LP/N$, Eq. (2.8)). Notation: $\mu_*=\langle\theta_*,x\rangle$, $\Sigma_\perp=\langle x_i^\perp,x_j^\perp\rangle/(N_0-P)$, $\nu=\|\theta_*\|^2/\alpha_0$.
- All asymptotic results take $P/N_0\to\alpha_0\in(0,1)$ and assume $\|\theta_{*,N_0}\|$ converges. Proofs of Thm 3.2 cases (b), (c) are sketched as "salient differences" from case (a); the proof of Cor 4.12 is omitted.
- The paper states that the limits of input dimension, $P$, $N$ and $L$ do not commute (Sec. 1.1) but gives no commutation table. Its comparisons: at $\sigma^2=1$, fixed $L$ with $N\to\infty$ gives the factor $(1+z_*/\alpha)^{-1}$, which tends to 1 as $L\to\infty$; $L/N\to\lambda_{\rm prior}$ gives the optimal posterior directly, with evidence larger by $e^{O(N)}$ (Sec. 3.3.2). Cor 4.12 writes the exponent constant as $c\le0$, while Sec. 3.3.2 says the fixed-depth evidence ratio vanishes like $\exp[-O(N)]$; the entry follows Sec. 3.3.2.
- Thm 3.3 (evidence maximisation iff posterior $\mathcal N(\mu_*,\nu\Sigma_\perp)$) is stated at fixed widths with $P,N_0\to\infty$; its proof notes that in that limit every $\sigma^2$ gives the same leading-order posterior.
- R1 here means standard (NNGP) scaling at zero noise, not μP exact conditioning (R4). Depth lists `one-hidden-layer` as well as `deep` because Thm 3.1 holds for any $L\ge1$ and Thm 3.4 for any fixed $L\ge0$; r2 and r3 ($L\to\infty$) are `deep` only.
- Grade B throughout, as for 2012.04030: $\mu_*$ and $\nu$ need the minimum-norm interpolant, i.e. $Y^\top(X^\top X)^{-1}Y$ from the empirical Gram matrix. Given $\nu$, $z_*$ and $t_*$ solve scalar equations. Closure is `exact` because the posterior is computed exactly, with no closure approximation.
- No numerics: the three figures are schematics (interpolant geometry, Mellin–Barnes contours).
