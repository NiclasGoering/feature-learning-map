one_line: "Finite Bayesian networks have a random top-layer kernel that data can shape toward the output kernel, a flexibility lost at large width."
summary: "The paper argues that infinite-width Bayesian networks cannot learn representations, since their kernel is fixed. It introduces infinite networks with finite bottlenecks and, for deep linear networks, shows that the variance of the prior kernel grows with depth and falls as one over the width. Under MAP inference and posterior sampling the top-layer kernel moves from the input kernel toward the output kernel, but in Bayesian networks a large width keeps it close to the prior. A toy model shows an optimal finite width, and a ResNet-34 trained on CIFAR-10 has a kernel far from its infinite-width limit and close to the label kernel."

- Read the full HTML v3 text; Appendices A-D (derivations, convolutional recursions, natural gradients) were read only for headings. The paper's formal results are for deep linear networks. Its nonlinear claims are the ResNet experiment and the infinite-with-bottleneck construction (Sec. 2), which is recorded in the summary only.
- `width_limit` lists both groups: the prior kernel variance is a large-width expansion (Eq. 19, valid for width much greater than depth), and the toy model and bottleneck constructions are exact finite-width statements.
- The posterior results in Sec. 4 are approximate analytic expressions for linear networks (the paper calls them "similar expressions", not theorems), so they are `phys`. Eqs. (23)-(27) are printed without full conditions in the HTML; the width-versus-outputs dependence is read from the text.
- `moves_mean` is `yes` for r1 only in the sense that finite width changes the predictor; the paper does not isolate the mean from the covariance.
