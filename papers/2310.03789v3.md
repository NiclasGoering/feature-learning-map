one_line: "Gaussian-mixture adaptive-kernel theory of Langevin-equilibrium two-layer networks maps grokking, for cubic and modular-addition teachers, to a first-order transition through a mixed phase."
summary: "Treating the equilibrium of Langevin training as a Bayesian posterior, the paper extends the adaptive-kernel approach from a Gaussian to a Gaussian-mixture law for each neuron's first-layer weights. In joint limits of width (mean-field readout scaling), input dimension and data, the per-neuron action reduces to one or two order parameters solved by saddle point. For an erf student learning $H_1+\\epsilon H_3$ and a quadratic student learning addition modulo a prime, raising an effective interaction $u$ (more data, less noise or smaller width) drives a first-order transition through a mixed phase in which a finite fraction of neurons align with the teacher. Langevin ensembles of the erf model reproduce the predicted weight distributions, and the authors conjecture that the mechanism lets finite networks learn the cubic component with $n\\propto d$ rather than $n\\propto d^3$ samples."

- Read v3 (5 May 2024) in full from the arXiv HTML and checked it against the PDF. The venue comes from the PDF header ("Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2024"). arXiv has no journal-ref.
- In v3, appendix equation numbers restart at (1) in App. A in both the PDF and the HTML, so App. A.4 also has an "Eq. (13)". The r1 location therefore names Sec. 3.1. App. B continues from (20) to (42), so Eq. (38) is unambiguous.
- Regime R2 / large-width-1/N follows the adaptive-kernel line this paper extends. The paper itself does not expand in 1/N: it sends width, input dimension and data to infinity together under mean-field-type readout scaling. In both scaling tables the per-weight readout variance falls like $N^{-3/2}$, so outputs vanish at init.
- The two models use different scalings. Teacher–student keeps the noise $\sigma^2$ at O(1) (with $\alpha=\beta$). Modular addition has $\sigma^2\propto N^{-1/2}$, which the yes/no likelihood dial cannot express, so r2 sets only temperature: to-zero. The labels are noiseless; $\sigma^2$ is the Langevin/likelihood noise, so label_noise is none.
- Grade A for r1 and r2: the paper derives the Gaussian-mixture form by saddle point and states it is exact in the scaling limit (Claim III). The theory curves are compared with the histograms, not fitted to them. r2 depends on a chosen sextic weight decay $\Gamma$, hence prior_family: other.
- The sampler is set to ULA because the paper cites the unadjusted Langevin algorithm and HMC only as examples. Main runs: learning rate $2\cdot10^{-3}$, 360000 epochs. App. D runs: $7\cdot10^6$ epochs, learning rate not stated. Approximations "other" means the equivalent-kernel continuum limit plus replacing the orthogonal weight norm by its mean.
- r4 adds learning_rule gd because Figs. 5–6 study Langevin training as dynamics (delayed generalization in time).
