summary: "The paper adds sparsity, meaning uninformative positions between informative features, to the random hierarchy model of data, which makes the label invariant to discrete versions of diffeomorphisms. Empirically, the training-set size needed to reach a fixed test error scales as $(s_0+1)^L n_c m^L$ for locally connected networks and as $(s_0+1)^2 n_c m^L$ for CNNs. Hidden representations become insensitive to synonym exchange and to diffeomorphisms at the same training-set size, which the authors link to the observed correlation between test error and diffeomorphism sensitivity on CIFAR10. A heuristic argument and a one-step GD computation support the scaling."

- Read the full HTML v2, including App. C (one-step GD) and the figure captions of Apps. B, D, E, F; the appendix figures themselves were not inspected.
- r1 and r2 are empirical scaling laws read off figures; the constants $C_0$, $C_1$ are not determined. The sample-complexity formulas are fits, so grade `A` means closed form in the data parameters, not a derived prediction.
- Vocabulary gaps: no `target` or `data_class` value for hierarchical (context-free grammar) classification tasks, hence `other`; `method` is `other` since the paper's arguments are heuristic and empirical.
- The paper trains on cross-entropy with a CNN/LCN family; step size, epochs and optimiser details are in App. D and were not recorded, so `gd_step_size` is `not-stated`. Width in experiments is finite; App. C takes the width $H\to\infty$ with a special initialisation.
- Label `synonymic-invariance` is reused from the file introduced by 2307.02129v5 (open PR); this paper's own explanation is the heuristic in Sec. 6 and App. C. `moves_mean` is `unknown` because there is no mean predictor in the sense of the Bayesian theories.
