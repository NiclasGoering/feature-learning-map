one_line: "Introduces the Bayesian representation learning limit, in which deep GP posteriors are Gaussian with covariances from an interpretable KL objective, and deep kernel machines."
summary: "Standard infinite-width limits of deep Gaussian processes make the posterior over Gram matrices a point mass at the prior kernel recursion, so no representation is learned. The paper scales the likelihood with width by copying the labels $N$ times, a limit that keeps learning. The Gram matrices then maximise the deep kernel machine objective: the log-likelihood minus layerwise KL divergences to the prior kernels. The exact posterior over features is zero-mean Gaussian with those Gram matrices as covariances. Langevin-sampled wide DGPs match the theory, and a sparse deep kernel machine scales linearly in the number of data points; a BNN extension is in the appendix."

- Read the main text of v6 in full from the HTML version; the appendices (A BNN extension, D weak convergence, E posterior derivation, K unimodality, M sparse DKM) were checked by heading only.
- The paper's focus is deep Gaussian processes, so architecture is `other`; the BNN extension in App. A is variational (non-Gaussian posteriors) and is not given its own result.
- Regime tag R3 is used: the limit rescales the likelihood with width at fixed $P$, equal to the μP-Bayesian regime of the later Lauditi et al. paper, although this paper never uses the name. Parametrisation is `other` for the same reason.
- Mixing `high` is a judgement: Langevin with step $10^{-3}$ and 10 chains, and the paper notes sampling can be very slow because of small prior eigenvalues; no diagnostics are reported.
- Uniqueness of the global maximum of the DKM objective is assumed in the point-mass claim; the paper proves it only for linear models (App. K) and checks it numerically otherwise.
