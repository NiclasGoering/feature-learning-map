summary: "The paper builds a family of input laws on the hypercube whose labels are the parity of $k$ hidden bits, half the mass being uniform and half having all $k$ bits equal. Theorem 1 shows that any linear predictor over a fixed bounded embedding needs dimension or norm exponential in $k$. Theorem 2 shows that gradient descent with weight decay on a two-layer ReLU6 network, from a symmetric random initialisation, reaches small error once the width is a polynomial in $k$. After the first step a subset of first-layer neurons becomes proportional to the sum of the support bits. A MNIST digit-sum parity experiment shows the same gap between a ReLU network and linear models."

- Read the whole HTML text including Sec. 5 proof sketch; the technical lemmas in the appendix were skimmed.
- The input law $\mathcal D_A$ is a mixture: half uniform, half with all $k$ support bits equal. It is built so that gradient descent can find the support, so the result is not about the uniform parity distribution.
- Theorem 2 uses population gradient descent with weight decay and a ReLU6 activation; I recorded the activation as `relu`. The initialisation is ternary and symmetric, entered as `other`.
- The width $q$ of Thm 2 is polynomial in $k$ and must also be small compared with $\sqrt n$, so I did not set a width limit beyond high-dim.
- The MNIST experiment uses the digit-sum parity of $k$ digits; the paper gives accuracy only as curves in Fig. 1, so the 80 percent figure is as quoted in the text.
