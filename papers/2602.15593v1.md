RNNs and DNNs differ only by a masking of temporal correlations. For endpoint-supervised tasks the two share kernels below a critical learning-signal strength, while above it only the RNN develops temporal coherence, in a second-order transition. For sequential tasks, weight sharing propagates label information across unsupervised timesteps, so RNNs generalise from fewer supervised steps while DNNs revert to the prior mean."

- No HTML version exists, so the PDF v1 was read (Secs. 1-4 in full, Apps. A.3-A.5 skimmed). Equation and figure numbers are those of the PDF; anchors are `none`.
- Training is Langevin dynamics with weight decay; the paper treats the stationary law as a Bayesian posterior, so the rule is `bayes`. Noise is $K=\kappa/N$ and the likelihood is scaled with width (μP). The paper's $\kappa$ is the intensive label noise, not a kernel or the map's $\kappa$; in the map's symbols the energy weight is $1/v$ and the ridge $\kappa/v$. The $O(1)$ term $\frac12\ln|v\Phi^-+\kappa|$ is dropped explicitly (text after Eq. 25), as in other μP kernel theories.
- The closed-form transition (App. A.5) is at $T=4$, $P=1$ and $\kappa=0$, with $\lambda_c=8$ and $d^{*2}=\frac45u^2w^3(\lambda-8)$ (Eqs. 44-45); its Landau expansion is Eq. (41). r4 is a perturbative expansion in the label kernel $Y$ (App. A.4.3), hence the `perturbative` solver. Mixing is `high`: SGLD at tiny temperature, equilibrium judged by agreement with the kernel theory; no autocorrelation or R-hat is reported.
- New mechanism `temporal-kernel-coherence` (Secs. 3.2, 3.3). It is a competition between a diagonal and an off-diagonal minimum of the kernel objective, which is why T5 is used, but the transition is second order, not a first-order switch between saddles.
- Grades: r1 is C because the nonlinear single-site law needs numerical averaging (App. A.6); r2 is A because the $T=4$ linear case has closed forms; r4 is B since the kernels come from the saddle-point equations and a given task. The analytic transition is derived for linear activation only; Fig. A.6 shows it persists for erf.
