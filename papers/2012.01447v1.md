summary: "The paper equates the information-bottleneck (IB) notion of relevance with the renormalisation-group (RG) relevance of field theory. For a short-range system on a cylinder, with a cell $V$ to compress and a distant region $E$ as relevance variable, the IB equations reduce at large buffer to an encoder that depends on $V$ only through the matrix element of the lowest-dimension primary operator. The first IB phase transition then sits at $\\beta_{c,1}^{-1}=\\epsilon^2$, set by the scaling dimension, and is checked numerically on the critical 2D Ising model. The encoder carries the data's symmetries when its alphabet is large enough. Real-space mutual-information RG is presented as the $\\beta\\to\\infty$ limit of IB at fixed alphabet size."

- RG record: sense (d), learning as RG on data (information-theoretic coarse-graining); no neural network is trained or analysed, and deep-learning estimators such as deep variational IB and RSMI-NE are only cited. The spins of a cylinder cell $V$ are compressed into $H$ by the IB encoder $P(h|v)$, Eq. (1), keeping only information about an environment $E$ behind a buffer $L_B$ that discards short-range correlations. Nothing flows in the Wilsonian sense; the object is the optimal encoder as the trade-off $\beta$ rises: Eqs. (3)-(4) (HTML App. B Eq. (22), PDF B9) reduce it to the lowest-dimension primary operator, and Eq. (5) (HTML (26), PDF C4) gives $\beta_{c,1}^{-1}=\epsilon^2=e^{-4\pi\Delta_1L_B/L}$, first order in $\epsilon=(\lambda_1/\lambda_0)^{L_B}$ and valid for $L_B\gg L$, with a cubic Landau-type solution in $t=\beta-\beta_{c,1}$ (HTML (28), (33); PDF C6, C11). Only the first IB transition is solved (no higher order); maturity is a physics-level derivation ("we prove" in the text) plus one small numerical check on the critical 2D Ising model, and the continuum Gaussian-field claim is only stated.
