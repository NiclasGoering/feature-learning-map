one_line: "Data augmentation in SGLD softens the likelihood, as tempering with $T>1$ does, so cold posteriors express honest aleatoric uncertainty; a noisy Dirichlet model avoids tempering."
summary: "The paper argues that Bayesian classification lacks a way to state beliefs about aleatoric uncertainty, and that this explains much of the cold posterior effect. The softmax likelihood equals a Dirichlet observation model, in which tempering the likelihood by $1/T$ adds $1/T$ pseudo-counts to the observed class, and a cold posterior equals a tempered likelihood with a prior narrowed by $T$. The limiting law of SGLD with data augmentation is shown to be a likelihood tempered at temperature $K>1$ on the augmented data, so augmentation softens the likelihood and makes cold posteriors natural. A noisy Dirichlet model with a controlled noise parameter matches or beats tempering on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet, with optimal settings that track label noise and augmentation strength."

- Read the main text and the experiment section; appendices (variational derivation, prior-scale experiments) were skimmed.
- Regime tag R1 and `finite-proportional` are the closest schema group for a Bayes paper at finite width and data; the paper does not analyse proportional scaling. They are chosen only because Bayes entries need a regime tag.
- Eq. (16) is a direct calculation of the expected stochastic gradient (drift only; gradient noise and step-size bias ignored), not a labelled theorem; r2 is therefore `phys`.
- r1 (Eq. 10) is likelihood-only tempering (Eq. 9), although Sec. 1 calls it a cold posterior; the cold posterior of Eq. (2) is r5 (Thm 1 and App. C.1-C.2.1).
- Eq. (20), the tempered-likelihood mean in linear regression, is printed as $(\tfrac1T X^\top X+\alpha^{-1}T\sigma^2I)^{-1}X^\top y$; the correct mean is $(X^\top X+\alpha^{-1}T\sigma^2I)^{-1}X^\top y$. Eq. (21) is correct.
- App. H does not say whether the runs of Figs. 5 and 11 use data augmentation; Fig. 11 keeps the step size fixed while C.2 says matching a cold posterior needs it scaled by $\sqrt T$.
- The venue is not stated in v1 (the paper is often cited as NeurIPS 2022); left as preprint.
