It separates the error into propagation of chaos, proved at stationarity, and sampling of the finite-particle stationary law with off-the-shelf log-concave samplers. For pairwise interactions it obtains a sharp $\\tilde O(dk^2/N^2)$ chaos rate under weak interaction, and improved gradient-call complexities for several samplers. For general functionals, including mean-field two-layer networks with noisy gradient descent, it shows a log-Sobolev inequality uniform in $N$ whose constant is doubly exponential in the problem parameters."

- Read Secs. 1-4 of v4 from the HTML (introduction, assumptions, propagation of chaos statements, Table 1, Examples 10-13) and the statement of Cor. 25; the proofs in Apps. A-E were not read. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- The paper is sampling and optimisation theory for McKean-Vlasov dynamics; two-layer networks appear only as the motivating application (Sec. 4.2). `learning_rule: bayes` follows the rule for stationary laws of mean-field Langevin dynamics. Regime tag R3 is taken for the mean-field limit at fixed data; r1 and r2 are about generic pairwise interactions, so their architecture is overridden to `other`.
- The complexity bounds in r2 and r4 are in the metric $\frac{\sqrt\alpha}{\sigma}W_2$ and $\sqrt{\mathsf{KL}}$ respectively, with $\kappa$ the condition number. Rates in the paper's Table 1 differ per assumption set; only two entries are quoted.
- r3's constant is the paper's own caveat (Sec. 4.2 "Limitations"): doubly exponential, so the guarantee is vacuous in practice; Chewi et al. (2409.10440) later improve it to a single exponential.
