one_line: "Layer-wise GD on a three-layer network learns the nonlinear feature $\\mathbb{K}f^*$, giving $\\tilde O(d^2)$ samples for quadratic-feature targets and a depth separation from two-layer networks."
summary: "A three-layer network with a fixed random first layer is trained in two stages: one gradient step on the middle layer, then regression on the readout. The first step makes the network a function of the learned feature $\\mathbb{K}f^*$, the target passed through the random-feature kernel operator, so the sample complexity depends on the low-frequency part of the target and not on the inverse kernel. For single-index targets this feature is linear and $\\tilde\\Theta(d)$ samples suffice. For $g^*(x^\\top Ax)$ the feature is quadratic and $\\tilde\\Theta(d^2)$ samples suffice. The paper also constructs a target that this procedure learns but that two-layer networks need superpolynomial width or weights to approximate."

- Read the HTML of v3 (April 2025 revision, which sharpens Thm 1 to $\|\mathbb{K}\|_{op}\|\mathbb{K}f^*\|^{-2}$ and the corollaries to $\tilde\Theta(d)$ and $\tilde\Theta(d^2)$). The task brief named other authors; arXiv lists Nichani, Damian, Lee. Venue NeurIPS 2023 is from memory of the publication, not printed on the arXiv page, so check it.
- Training is layer-wise with sample splitting: first layer $V$ fixed at random, one GD step on $W$ from zero, biases redrawn, then GD with weight decay on the readout $a$ (Algorithm 1). `learned_layers`, `parametrisation`, `prior_*` and `p_vs_n` are therefore `other`; the readout scale is $1/m_1$ with $\pm1$ initial weights and $W=0$. The stage-1 step is $\eta_1=(m_1/m_2)\bar\eta$.
- Theorem 1 holds for sub-Gaussian inputs and polynomial-moment targets, hence `input: other`, `target: arbitrary`. `activation` is ReLU for $\sigma_1$ and generic (polynomial growth) for $\sigma_2$. Thm 3 and Cor 1 are in one result because they share assumptions; the anchor for Cor 1 is `Thmcorollary1`. The $(\kappa/\sqrt d)^{1/3}$ error floor is an artefact of the proof for general $A$.
- Mechanism: `target-kernel-image` is new; the paper explains it in Sec. 3.1 (Eq. (5)-(12)). It is not a spike in the weights; it is a function-space description of the single feature the middle layer extracts. Fig. 2 (SGD with momentum, $\mu$P, all layers trained, two vs three layers) is not made a result because it is only a simulation; Fig. 1 uses the closed-form kernel at $m_2\to\infty$.
- Open problem op-09 is only touched loosely (an optimisation-based depth separation for two vs three layers under GD, not a Bayesian μP statement). The lower bound in r4 is for any two-layer net, not only GD-trained ones; the GD-time conclusion is argued in the remarks after Thm 4.
