summary: "The paper defines silent alignment: the tangent kernel changes its eigenstructure early, while small, and afterwards changes only in overall scale. It shows that in that case the trained function is kernel regression with the final data-dependent kernel, with error of order $\\sigma^L$ in the initialisation scale. For two-layer linear networks on whitened data it derives two phases, a rank-one alignment at $t\\sim1/s$ and a fitting phase at $t\\sim\\frac1s\\log(s/\\sigma^2)$. For deep linear networks it gives a rank-one kernel correction that aligns more with depth. Experiments on ReLU, tanh and ResNet models show the effect for whitened data and its weakening for anisotropic data."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 6 and the figure captions; the appendices (proofs, deep-network details, ResNet experiments) were skimmed only. The analytic work is for linear networks; the nonlinear and ResNet claims are empirical, as the paper states.
- The setting is gradient flow with small initialisation $\sigma$ and whitened inputs. The paper also says the first, two-layer analysis has "focused on transparency over rigor"; hence `phys` for r2 and r3.
- The statement of the Section 2 result assumes the kernel model of Eq. (3); the paper does not show that real networks satisfy it exactly. r1 is therefore a theorem about the model, not about a network.
- The partially-whitened experiment uses Adam with learning rate $5\times10^{-3}$ (Fig. 4), not gradient flow.
- No open problem is listed: the paper compares gradient descent with kernel regression on its final kernel and has no Bayesian posterior.
