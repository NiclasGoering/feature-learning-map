summary: "The paper extends the univariate result of Savarese et al. to inputs in $\\mathbb R^d$. The minimal Euclidean weight norm needed to realise $f$ with a one-hidden-layer ReLU network of unbounded width is characterised through the $\\mathcal R$-norm, the $L^1$ norm of the Radon transform of $(-\\Delta)^{(d+1)/2}f$, up to a term for the unregularised linear unit. All Sobolev functions in $W^{d+1,1}$ have finite cost, radial bumps and a pyramid are computed explicitly, and the $\\mathcal R$-norm is shown not to be an RKHS norm. The pyramid has infinite two-layer cost but a finite three-layer net, giving a qualitative depth separation."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 5 and the statements of the appendix results; proofs in Apps. G to N were not checked.
- No learning rule is in the paper. As for 1902.05040, `learning_rule: [gd]` is the closest bin (global minimiser of a weight-norm-regularised loss), and `width_limit: inf-meanfield-fixedP` is the closest infinite-width bin.
- The $\mathcal R$-norm formulas are stated for odd $d$ in Prop. 1; for even $d$ the same holds with pseudo-differential operators involving a Hilbert transform (Sec. 3). The `statement` of r1 therefore quotes the odd-$d$ form. Conjecture 1 (piecewise linear $f$ has finite $\mathcal R$-norm iff exactly a finite-width net) is not recorded as a result.
- r4 uses a depth override because Example 5 compares two and three layers; the paper-level `depth` lists both for the same reason.
- The paper's own remark that quantitative depth separation for the pyramid is left open is not recorded.
