summary: "The paper analyses mean-field Langevin dynamics, the continuous-time limit of noisy gradient descent on an infinite-width two-layer network, in the practical setting of finitely many particles, discrete time and stochastic gradients. A framework for uniform-in-time propagation of chaos controls the three errors under a log-Sobolev inequality for the proximal Gibbs measure. The objective gap decays exponentially up to terms vanishing with step size and gradient variance and an $O(1/N)$ particle error. The authors specialise it to plain stochastic gradients and to SVRG, and to ordinary Langevin dynamics, where the complexities improve on earlier work. Applications include mean-field networks and MMD minimisation."

- Read Secs. 1-4 of v1 from the HTML (setting, assumptions, Thms 1-4, Table 1) and the conclusion; the appendices with the proofs and the examples (MMD minimisation, standard Langevin specialisation) were not read. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`. The paper has no experiments.
- The dynamics are studied as an optimiser (noisy gradient descent on a mean-field two-layer network), so `learning_rule: gd`. The stationary-measure LSI of r1 serves the convergence argument and is not treated as a Bayes result. The width limit is the mean-field one ($N\to\infty$ particles at fixed data), and no regime tag applies to a GD-only entry.
- `E_*` in r3 and r4 is the paper's complexity factor defined in Table 1, built from the smoothness constants and $\alpha$; only the leading terms of the bounds are quoted.
- Assumptions 1-3 (smooth convex loss, strongly convex regulariser, LSI) are used by all results; Assumption 4 gives the refined rates quoted as "extra smoothness".
