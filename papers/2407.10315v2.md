one_line: "Replica theory of continual learning in deep ReLU networks: task-similarity order parameters predict forgetting, and multi-head networks show a transition to catastrophic anterograde interference."
summary: "Sequential learning of tasks is cast as Gibbs sampling with a penalty on weight change, and analysed with the replica method for deep, wide ReLU networks. With a shared readout, two scalar order parameters (relevant-feature and rule similarity) determine forgetting, and depth lowers short-term forgetting. With task-specific readouts, forgetting is mild but a similarity parameter sets a critical load below which the network keeps old tasks perfectly and fails on the new one. The predictions are checked on target-distractor sequences and permuted and split benchmark datasets with gradient-descent simulations. An intermediate regularisation strength balances the two tasks."

- Read the HTML of v2 (26 Jan 2025) for the main text Sec. 1 to 5 and the headings and selected parts of the SI (Sec. 6, 7, 9.9 and the GD-simulation protocol, SI 13). The long replica derivations in SI 8 and 9 were not checked.
- This is a Gibbs (equilibrium) formulation with a quadratic penalty on weight change between tasks, so `learning_rule: bayes`, but the numerical validation uses gradient descent with a penalty; the paper does not describe a posterior sampler. Hence `sampler: gd-simulation`, `mixing: none`, and r4 is marked as a Bayes-versus-GD comparison. Temperature is not discussed in the main text beyond $\beta\to\infty$ schematics.
- The single-head theory is exact only as $N\to\infty$, $\alpha\to0$; the multi-head theory keeps $\alpha$ finite, and the phase transition appears for $\alpha>1$. The learning-curve figures also exist in a "fixed-representation" regime for $\alpha<1$.
- New mechanism `readout-norm-divergence`: Fig. 5 caption and Sec. 4.3/SI 9.9 attribute the divergence of the new-task error to the readout norm $\|a_2\|$ diverging while $\|W_2-W_1\|$ stays minimal. The phase boundary $\alpha=1$ is task independent; the boundary $\alpha_c$ uses $\gamma_{\rm sim}$ under assumptions stated in SI 9.9.1.
- Tag `first-order-transition` is a judgement call: the paper reports an abrupt change at $\alpha_c$ but calls it a phase transition without naming its order.
