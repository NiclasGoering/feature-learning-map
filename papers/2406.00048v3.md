summary: "The paper studies next-token prediction on sequences from the random hierarchy model, a tree-structured probabilistic context-free grammar. It computes the decay of token-token correlations with distance, which falls in steps set by the tree. A finite training set only resolves correlations above the sampling noise $(v^2P)^{-1/2}$, which defines an effective context window $t^\\ast(P)$. Correlations are argued to let deep networks represent hidden variables level by level, giving sample complexities $P_\\ell$, loss steps and a power-law scaling of the loss. Transformers and CNNs trained by gradient descent agree qualitatively, and a conjecture on $t^\\ast(P)$ is tested on Shakespeare and WikiText."

- Read the full v3 HTML main text and the Appendix A and G experiment descriptions; the combinatorial derivation of the correlations (App. D) and the sampling-noise proof (App. E) were not checked line by line. Venue from the arXiv comment ("NeurIPS 2024").
- The paper is about data structure and sample complexity for gradient-trained networks, with no Bayesian posterior; it is recorded as GD, no regime tag, Bayes dials `not-applicable`. `finite-exact` and `p_vs_n: fixed` are the closest slots for experiments at fixed width; the theory itself is a statement about data statistics. `method: other`, `data_class: other` and `target: other` are vocabulary gaps (combinatorial averages over RHM realisations; next-token prediction on a grammar).
- Sample complexities $P_\ell$ (r1) and the scaling law (r2) come from a heuristic argument; the Limitations paragraph says there is no proof connecting the strategy of Sec. 4.1 to the sample complexity of gradient-trained networks. Hence `phys`. The label `synonymic-invariance` is reused for the hidden-variable representation; the paper itself shows the mechanism only for CNNs (Fig. 3).
- Architecture detail: CNNs use ReLU in the maximal-update parametrisation with SGD (App. A.1); the RHM transformers are attention-only stacks trained with Adam (App. A.2). Both are in the defaults; r5 overrides to BERT-like transformers on text. App. G reports that CNNs reach the third step at fewer samples than $P_3$ (weight sharing), an architecture dependence the theory does not capture.
- $\tilde C^{(\ell)}$ in r3 is the paper's large-$v$ form in Eq. (8); the printed expression was read from the HTML text, so the checker should confirm the exponent of $v$.
