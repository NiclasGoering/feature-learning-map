summary: "The paper considers the stationary measure of the interacting particle approximation of mean-field Langevin dynamics, for an energy that is linearly convex plus a strongly convex term. Following Kook et al., it builds a Lipschitz transport map from the standard Gaussian via the reverse heat flow of Kim and Milman. A propagation-of-chaos bound for heterogeneous environments, extended from Nitanda, lets the Lipschitz constant depend on the problem parameters through a single exponential, improving the doubly exponential bound of Kook et al. The map implies a log-Sobolev inequality uniform in $N$ and Gaussian isoperimetry. The setting covers mean-field two-layer networks, including ReLU."

- Read Secs. 1-2 of v1 (assumptions, Thms 2-3 and the consequences) from the HTML and skimmed the proof outline of Sec. 3; the detailed proofs were not checked. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Pure functional-inequality theory. Two-layer networks enter through Example 1; `learning_rule: bayes` follows the rule for stationary laws of mean-field Langevin dynamics, and regime tag R3 is taken for the mean-field limit at fixed data. r1 and r3 are for a general linearly convex energy, so their architecture is overridden to `other`.
- The bound is for every $N\ge1$ and tied to a perturbative form $F=F_0+\int V\,d\nu$ with $V$ $\lambda$-strongly convex. The paper notes a concurrent result (Wang 2024) with comparable singly exponential scaling under weaker assumptions but without the transport-map consequences.
- The constants are exponential in the problem parameters, so the guarantee remains weak for strongly interacting or large-norm networks. This is relevant to open problem op-03.
- Full-text check (v1 pdf, Secs. 1-5): r4 added (Thms 7-8), the static statement about the finite-$N$ law that the transport argument rests on; it holds for all $N\ge1$ and compares itself with Kook Thm 5 and Nitanda Thm 1 (Sec. 4). r1 corrected: Assumption 2 bounds $\nabla_1\nabla_2\delta^2F_0$, not $\delta^2F_0$. r2 corrected: the loss must also be $\beta_\ell$-smooth, and $h$ Lipschitz in all trainable parameters (ReLU via $h=\mathrm{ReLU}(\langle\theta,x\rangle)$, Example 1); r2, r4 typed T3.
- Conventions: Eq. (1.4) is $\propto\exp(-\frac{2N}{\sigma^2}F)$ with $F_0=\frac1n\sum_i L$ averaged over data (Example 1), so $T=\sigma^2/2$ and a quadratic $V=\frac\lambda2\|x\|^2$ gives a prior $\mathcal N(0,\frac{\sigma^2}{2\lambda}I)$ with variance proportional to $T$.
