Following Kook et al., it builds a Lipschitz transport map from the standard Gaussian via the reverse heat flow of Kim and Milman. A propagation-of-chaos bound for heterogeneous environments, extended from Nitanda, lets the Lipschitz constant depend on the problem parameters through a single exponential, improving the doubly exponential bound of Kook et al. - Read Secs. 1-2 of v1 (assumptions, Thms 2-3 and the consequences) from the HTML and skimmed the proof outline of Sec. 3; the detailed proofs were not checked. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Pure functional-inequality theory. Two-layer networks enter through Example 1; `learning_rule: bayes` follows the rule for stationary laws of mean-field Langevin dynamics, and regime tag R3 is taken for the mean-field limit at fixed data. r1 and r3 are for a general linearly convex energy, so their architecture is overridden to `other`.
- The bound is for every $N\ge1$ and tied to a perturbative form $F=F_0+\int V\,d\nu$ with $V$ $\lambda$-strongly convex. The paper notes a concurrent result (Wang 2024) with comparable singly exponential scaling under weaker assumptions but without the transport-map consequences.
- The constants are exponential in the problem parameters, so the guarantee remains weak for strongly interacting or large-norm networks. This is relevant to open problem op-03.
