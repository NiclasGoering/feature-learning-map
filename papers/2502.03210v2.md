one_line: "A Legendre-transform theory of the Bayesian posterior on output discrepancies links kernel rescaling and directional kernel adaptation across mean-field to standard scaling."
summary: "The paper writes the Bayesian posterior of a one-hidden-layer network as a minimisation problem for the mean output discrepancies, so that the order parameter has dimension $P$ instead of one. A saddle-point (tree-level) approximation is enough in mean-field scaling, while standard scaling needs one-loop fluctuation corrections, and the same equations cover the continuum between them. For linear networks the mean predictor of this adaptive kernel equals that of a rescaled NNGP kernel in the proportional limit. Directional adaptation survives in the output covariance and, for non-linear networks, in the mean, where it changes the sample complexity relative to the NNGP and to rescaling theories."

- Read the abstract and Secs. 1–7 of the HTML of v2; the appendices (cumulant expansion, App. A.2.3 non-linear teacher-student calculation, numerics) were not read in detail, so r5 rests on the statement in Sec. 6 and the pointer to Fig. 5 in App. A.2.3. `venue` is `preprint`.
- Result r1, r4 and r5 use the mean-field tree level at small $P$ (80 training points, $N=100$–$200$); r3 states the equivalence to rescaling for $N\propto P\to\infty$, which is why the R3′ tag and the `inf-meanfield-propP` group are used there. The paper says the one-loop theory applies to the whole range $\gamma\in[1,2]$ but tests mainly linear networks.
- Dataset details differ across figures: an Ising-type binary task (`boolean-cube` inputs, labels $\pm1$) in Figs. 1–3, a linear teacher with Gaussian inputs in Fig. 4, MNIST in the appendix. The mean-field noise scales as $\kappa=\kappa_0N^{1-\gamma}$, so the likelihood is rescaled with width.
- The paper itself notes that the mean output of the linear network is equivalent to a rescaled NNGP kernel, which is why r1 and r3 both appear with different mechanism labels: r1 is the adapted kernel, r3 its effective scalar form for the mean.
- Equation numbers follow the HTML. The relation to Seroussi et al. is the paper's own statement ("recovering results by Seroussi et al. (2023)").
