one_line: "A Legendre-transform theory of the Bayesian posterior on output discrepancies links kernel rescaling and directional kernel adaptation across mean-field to standard scaling."
summary: "The paper writes the Bayesian posterior of a one-hidden-layer network as a minimisation problem for the mean output discrepancies, so that the order parameter has dimension $P$ instead of one. A saddle-point (tree-level) approximation is enough in mean-field scaling, while standard scaling needs one-loop fluctuation corrections, and the same equations cover the continuum between them. For linear networks the mean predictor of this adaptive kernel equals that of a rescaled NNGP kernel, exactly at tree level and at one loop in the proportional limit. Directional adaptation survives in the output covariance and, for non-linear networks, in the mean, where it changes the sample complexity relative to the NNGP and to rescaling theories."

- Read the abstract and Secs. 1–7 of the HTML of v2; the entry check then read App. A.2–A.5, B, C.3 and D in the pdftotext of v2. `venue`: the arXiv comment says accepted at ICML 2025.
- r5: Eqs. (73)–(74) carry mean-field factors ($g_w/N$, $\kappa_0/N$, $g_w/2N^2$), but the Fig. 5 caption gives $\gamma=1$ with $N=1000$, $D=30$. The overrides keep the theory as written and record the numerics in `empirics`.
- Sampling is full-batch Langevin GD with weight decay (Eq. 167) at $T=2\kappa_0\in[0.8,4]$, 10000 steps, independent seeds; no autocorrelation check. Neither `low` nor `high` mixing fits exactly; `high` is kept.
- Result r1, r4 and r5 use the mean-field tree level at small $P$ (80 training points, $N=100$–$200$); r3 states the equivalence to rescaling for $N\propto P\to\infty$, but the tree-level equivalence (Eq. 23) is exact at any $P$; only the one-loop form needs $N\propto P$. The R3′ tag and the paper-level `inf-meanfield-propP` rest on the Discussion: the one-loop theory covers $\gamma\in[1,2]$. The paper says the one-loop theory applies to the whole range $\gamma\in[1,2]$ but tests mainly linear networks.
- Dataset details differ across figures: an Ising-type binary task (`boolean-cube` inputs, labels $\pm1$) in Figs. 1–3 and 11, a linear teacher with Gaussian inputs in Figs. 4 and 12, $H_1+\epsilon H_3$ of a projection in Fig. 5, MNIST in Figs. 6–8. Labels are noiseless; the Gaussian $\xi$ is the likelihood (regularisation) noise. The mean-field noise scales as $\kappa=\kappa_0N^{1-\gamma}$, so the likelihood is rescaled with width.
- The paper itself notes that the mean output of the linear network is equivalent to a rescaled NNGP kernel, which is why r1 and r3 both appear with different mechanism labels: r1 is the adapted kernel, r3 its effective scalar form for the mean.
- Equation numbers follow the HTML. The relation to Seroussi et al. is the paper's own statement ("recovering results by Seroussi et al. (2023)").
