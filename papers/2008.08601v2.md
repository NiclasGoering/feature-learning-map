summary: "The paper identifies wide networks that become Gaussian processes with a free scalar field theory, whose $n$-point functions are Wick sums of kernels. Large but finite width is a non-Gaussian process with effective action $S_{GP}+\\int(\\lambda f^4+\\kappa f^6)$, treated at tree level with interacting Feynman diagrams; connected 4- and 6-point functions scale as $1/N$ and $1/N^2$. For randomly initialised single-hidden-layer Erf, ReLU and Gaussian-kernel networks, a quartic coupling fitted to the 4-point function predicts the 6-point function and unseen 4-point inputs. A cutoff on input-space integrals gives a Wilsonian flow of the couplings: the ReLU-net quartic coupling obeys $\\beta(\\lambda)=-(d_{in}+4)\\lambda$, and the sextic coupling is irrelevant. Trained networks are not studied."

- RG record: (a) Sense (c): neural-network field theory with a Wilsonian cutoff RG, where the GP limit is a free field and finite width turns on interactions (Tables 1 and 3, Eq. (3.12)). (b) Nothing in width or depth is coarse-grained: the cutoff $\Lambda$ only restricts the input-space vertex integrals $\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d^{d_{in}}x$ (Eq. (4.5)) at fixed $N$, so large-norm inputs are dropped. (c) What flows is the quartic coupling $\lambda(\Lambda)$, with $\beta=dg/d\log\Lambda$ from $dG^{(n)}/d\Lambda=0$ (Eqs. (4.6)–(4.7)); the general equations (4.13)–(4.14), (4.20)–(4.21) are not solved, and the only explicit flow is ReLU-net with $\kappa$ dropped, $\beta(\lambda)=-(d_{in}+4)\lambda$ (Eq. (4.27), integrated in Eq. (4.28), tested in Fig. 7), at tree level and first order in $\lambda\sim1/N$ with no loops and no self-energy (Eq. (3.14), step 4.5). (d) It does not go beyond first order ($\kappa\sim1/N^2$ and $\lambda^2$ are dropped), and its maturity is a framework plus derivation plus numerics on randomly initialised single-layer nets, with trained networks only discussed.

- Judgement calls. `bayes` and R2 / `large-width-1/N` because the object is the prior ensemble at large finite $N$ expanded in $\lambda\sim1/N$, although there is no posterior, likelihood or training. Closure `one-loop` is the nearest label, but the paper works at tree level only. Grade D because $\lambda$ is fitted to measured $G^{(4)}$ before $G^{(6)}$ and unseen $G^{(4)}$ are predicted. In r3 the paper says the $1/N$ results also hold for deep fully-connected networks, but the derivation shown (Eqs. (2.39)–(2.50)) is written for one hidden layer; the deep case was not checked.
- Inconsistencies in the paper. The Introduction says $\beta$-functions are computed for 4-point couplings "in some of our examples", but only ReLU-net has one (Eq. (4.27)); Gauss-net needs none (Sec. 4.3) and Erf-net has none. Sec. 2.4 says the $1/N$ falloff will be proved in Sec. 2.5, which computes $n=4,6$ and conjectures $2k$ (Eq. (2.54)). The Sec. 3.6 text says the local $\lambda_2$ term helps all three nets, but Table 5 shows Gauss M1 to M2 only 0.0145 to 0.0144 test MAPE. The text gives $\Lambda=10^5$ for the Erf-net $\lambda_m$ plot (Sec. 3.5), while the Fig. 6 panel title reads $\Lambda=100$. Erf-net connected $G^{(6)}$ is below noise at all $N$ (Fig. 3), so $1/N^2$ is not tested there.
