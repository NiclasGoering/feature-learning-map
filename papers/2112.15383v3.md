summary: "The paper treats the equilibrium of noisy gradient descent with weight decay as the Bayesian posterior of finite-width CNNs and FCNs. Decoupling layers and neurons and replacing each neuron's law by the KL-closest Gaussian gives closed equations of state, in which feature learning is an $O(1/N)$ shift of the inverse pre-kernel built from the training discrepancy. This shift is kept to all orders in an emergent feature-learning scale $\\chi\\propto n^2\\,\\mathrm{MSE}/C$ rather than expanded perturbatively. For a two-layer erf CNN with a linear teacher the equations reduce to one scalar equation, and the input-weight covariance grows only along the teacher direction. Langevin-equilibrated FCNs and CNNs agree with the theory far from the GP limit."

- Version read: arXiv v3 (22 Sep 2022), HTML and PDF; published as Nature Communications 14, 908 (2023), not compared with v3. Main-text equation, section and figure numbers agree between HTML and PDF. In the Supplementary Information, HTML equation numbers run one to two ahead of the PDF from Eq. (81) on (the scalar equation for $\alpha$ is PDF Eq. (109) but HTML Eq. (111)); Eq. (63) cited in r4 is identical in both.
- Learning rule: the object is the equilibrium law of GD plus weight decay plus white noise at small learning rate, which the paper identifies with the Bayesian posterior, so `bayes`. The regime tag R2 / `large-width-1/N` reflects the $O(1/N)$ per-neuron tilt; the paper stresses that it is not plain $1/N$ perturbation theory, because it keeps all orders in $\chi$. `p_vs_n: proportional` describes the experiments ($n$ comparable to width or channels: $n=1600$ with $C=320$–$640$, $n=1024$ with $N\le1024$), not a formal limit.
- Closure `gaussian`: the variational Gaussian approximation (KL-closest centred Gaussian per neuron, with an extra mean for ReLU, Sec. 9) is derived, not fitted. It is supported by a diagram argument (Sec. 8), by an $S\gg1$ argument for the two-layer CNN (Sec. 7), and by Gaussianity checks (Figs. 1a, 2, 4, 6). So grade D does not apply. The Fig. 5 comparison for the 3-layer CNN feeds empirical kernels into the EoS, so it is a consistency check and is not listed as a result.
- `method: other` is the mean-field (Hartree–Fock-like) decoupling plus the variational Gaussian approximation. `spectral-eigenlearning` in r2 is the equivalent-kernel approximation, with a leading $1/n$ correction ($q_{\rm train}$, which uses a numerically estimated $\bar C_n$). In r2, train-set predictions are fully analytic, while test-set predictions use numerical GP inference with the analytic $\Sigma$.
- Mixing set to `high`: unadjusted noisy GD at $\sigma^2=10^{-3}$–$5\cdot10^{-3}$ ($0.1$ for the two-layer CNN). Equilibrium was declared from train-loss stationarity, agreement across seeds and insensitivity to a further learning-rate cut. Input-layer autocorrelation times reached about $10^6$ epochs and were handled by averaging over many seeds.
- Two inconsistencies in the paper. The Fig. 1 caption gives $d=128$ for the FCN, while the Fig. 7 caption says the Fig. 1(b) data use $d=64$. The 3-layer CNN sizes are swapped between Sec. 2.4 ($S_0=50$, $S_1=30$) and Methods ($S_1=50$, $S_0=30$).
