one_line: "Proves the replica-symmetric free entropy of random generalized linear models, giving Bayes-optimal errors, sharp phase transitions and the regions where AMP is optimal."
summary: "The paper studies teacher-student generalized linear models (the perceptron and relatives) with an i.i.d. random data matrix, with samples and dimension growing at fixed ratio. It proves, by the adaptive interpolation method, that the free entropy equals the replica-symmetric formula, which yields the Bayes-optimal estimation and generalization errors. State evolution of the AMP algorithm shows when AMP attains these errors and locates the hard phase between the information-theoretic and algorithmic thresholds. Examples include the binary perceptron, sign-less and ReLU channels and the symmetric door, with comparisons to logistic regression and a small trained network; the setting has no hidden layer, so no feature learning is studied."

- The setting is a single-layer GLM, not a network with learned features, so all mechanisms are `not-analysed` (T0), except r4 where the first-order hard phase maps onto `mixed-phase` (T5). `architecture: other` is used because the vocabulary has no GLM or perceptron entry.
- r4 is marked `thm` because the underlying formulas are proved. The numerical thresholds (1.249, 1.493, 1.566, 1/2 via Eq. (11)) come from evaluating them. The paper itself says the numerics are not bounded rigorously.
- Hypotheses h1–h5: Thm 1 requires $\Delta>0$ (a small Gaussian regularising noise) unless the output is discrete; the data matrix is only assumed to have independent, zero-mean, unit-variance entries, and `isotropic-gaussian` is the nearest vocabulary value. Numerics use Gaussian matrices.
- The anchors follow the HTML numbering of v3: the main-part theorems are `Thmtheorem1/2`, Figs. 1, 2 are `S3.F1`, `S3.F2`, Eq. (10) is in section `S2.SS4`.
