summary: "The paper argues that uniformly random labelings, the setting of classical no-free-lunch theorems, are almost all incompressible, whereas real datasets are compressible. It proves a Kolmogorov-style no-free-lunch bound: on uniformly random labels every classifier of bounded description length has cross entropy near that of random guessing. Compression-based PAC-Bayes bounds are computed for convolutional networks on tabular data. GPT-3 assigns exponentially higher probability to simpler sequences, and randomly initialised GPT-2 models also favour low-complexity ones. Cross-validation over many models and a flexible model with a simplicity penalty are argued to avoid overfitting across sample sizes."

- Read the main text of the HTML version, including the statement of Thm 3.1 and Eqs. (1)-(3). The proof (App. C), the appendix experiments and the model-selection results of Sec. 5 (cross-validation bound, GoogLeNet/ViT mixture, polynomial regression) were not recorded as results.
- This is a position paper. `learning_rule: [gd]` follows the experiments (trained MLPs, CNNs and pre-trained language models); the bounds themselves hold for any learner. r5 is about untrained random initialisation; it stays under `gd` because the paper never treats it as a posterior. No regime tag is given because no width or sample scaling is analysed.
- Thm 3.1 contains an unspecified constant $c$ depending on the programming language, and $K(p)$ is uncomputable, so no number is predicted (grade `none`). The "Prediction" in r3 uses the compression method of Lotfi et al. 2022 as an upper bound.
- In r4 and r5 "complexity" is the length of the shortest expression tree (or repetition pattern) in a toy language, an upper bound on Kolmogorov complexity up to a constant.
