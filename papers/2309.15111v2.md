summary: "The paper trains both layers of a polynomial-width two-layer ReLU network with minibatch SGD on the logistic loss, on XOR labels $-x_1x_2$ of Boolean inputs. It proves population loss $o(1)$ after $\\tilde O(d)$ samples, without layer-wise training or modified learning rates. In phase one the network is small, neurons evolve almost independently, and the component of a neuron along the signal grows exponentially from its random overlap of order $1/\\sqrt d$. In phase two the signal components dominate and are balanced across the four clusters, while simultaneous training of the second layer amplifies the few neurons that found signal. A short proof shows $\\tilde\\Theta(d)$ is needed for rotationally invariant algorithms."

- Read the HTML version in full except the lemma proofs in Appendices B to E. Theorem 3.1 is stated for Boolean data only; the paper says Gaussian inputs are left open.
- The paper reports no experiments.
- The text is inconsistent on the initialisation scale: Sec. 2.2 says $\|w_j\|=\theta$, Thm 3.1 says "initialization scale $\theta/\sqrt p$". The entry records no numeric scale.
- The constant $C$ is unspecified, so grade is none. The weights are called "mean-field" because the output has a $1/p$ factor and gradients are scaled by $p$ (footnote 3).
- The proof of the lower bound in Appendix F is introduced as a sketch but is written as a full proof of Prop F.1; I marked it thm.
- Open problem op-05 mentions the rotation-equivariance lower bound of Glasgow 2024; this is Prop F.1.
