summary: "The paper studies gradient flow of a wide two-layer network on a Gaussian single-index target, with second-layer weights learned a factor $\\varepsilon$ faster than first-layer weights. The population flow reduces to a $d$-independent mean-field system that is singularly perturbed for small $\\varepsilon$. The authors propose a canonical learning order: the risk shows plateaus at $\\tfrac12\\sum_{k\\ge l}\\varphi_k^2$ as the network fits Hermite components of the link in increasing degree, on time-scales growing as $\\varepsilon^{-1/2l}$. They prove parts of the scenario, support the rest by matched asymptotics and numerics, and show projected online SGD reaches small risk with $O(d)$ samples."

- Read the HTML version through Sec. 4, the statements of Thms 1 to 3 and the description of numerics. The long proofs (Secs. 6, 7, appendices) were not checked.
- The canonical learning order (r1) is a proposed scenario: the abstract says it rests on new rigorous results, non-rigorous derivations and numerics, hence `phys`. Thm 3 is conditional on the scenario holding up to level $L$.
- Width and scaling are my reading: the order of limits is $d\to\infty$, $m\to\infty$ (mean-field, $a_i/m=\Theta(1/m)$), then small $\varepsilon$; the sample size is $n\asymp d$. I put it in both `inf-meanfield-fixedP` and `high-dim`.
- Learning-rate ratio $\varepsilon$ means the second layer is learned much faster than the first. Weights $u_i$ are projected onto the unit sphere, and activations lacking Hermite coefficients make the scenario fail (App. E.1).
- I reused `staircase-of-directions` although the stages here are Hermite degrees in a single direction rather than new directions; please check whether a separate label is wanted.
