summary: "The paper defines the leap complexity of a target that depends on a few latent coordinates: the largest number of new coordinates added when its monomials are ordered best. It conjectures that online-SGD on isotropically initialised networks needs $\\tilde\\Theta(d^{\\max(\\mathrm{Leap}-1,1)})$ steps and shows that kernels need $d^{\\deg}$ samples and CSQ algorithms $d^{\\mathrm{Leap}}$ (Boolean) queries. It proves the conjecture for nested Hermite monomials on Gaussian data, for a two-layer network trained layerwise with projected first-layer SGD: the first layer picks up the support coordinates one monomial at a time, a saddle-to-saddle dynamics. Experiments on the hypercube with vanilla SGD show the plateaus."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 4, the statements of the theorems and the appendix section headings; the long proofs (App. C to F) were not read.
- The proved results need changes to vanilla SGD (layerwise training, a projection of first-layer weights, frozen biases, second-layer ridge) and the support is taken to be a subset of coordinates of $x$. Sec. 3 says these are not needed in practice and Fig. 1 uses vanilla joint training. The saddle-to-saddle claim is proved only for increasing leaps (footnote in Sec. 1.2).
- Thm 2 gives the total time for the largest leap; the faster plateaus for smaller leaps follow only with varied step sizes (App. D.2.1).
- Prop 2 for Gaussian data gives $d^{\mathrm{Leap}/2}$ only; the paper conjectures $d^{\mathrm{Leap}}$.
- `op-06` is listed because the paper gives gradient-descent sample complexities on staircase targets, which op-06 names as the comparison for a Bayesian analogue. The venue is `preprint` because the arXiv record gives no journal reference.
