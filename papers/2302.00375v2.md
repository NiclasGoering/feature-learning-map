summary: "The paper computes the Bayes-optimal test error for learning a deep random network of extensive width from Gaussian inputs, with $n$, $d$ and all widths growing proportionally. A replica computation, resting on a conjectured deep Gaussian-equivalence property, shows that this error equals that of an equivalent target linear in the input plus effective noise. Ridge and kernel regression with explicitly given optimal regularisation reach the Bayes-optimal error, while finite-width random features do not. A Gibbs sampler confirms the prediction for one hidden layer. Numerically, at $n\\sim d^2$ trained networks learn the target almost perfectly while kernel and ridge regression capture only low-degree approximations."

- Title: the arXiv listing (and ICML proceedings) title is used; the v2 PDF/HTML header reads "Optimal Learning of Deep Random Networks of Extensive-width". Venue from the arXiv journal-ref: Proceedings of the 40th ICML, PMLR 202:6468–6521.
- Status: the Bayes errors (r1) and the ERM curves (r2) are replica / heuristic results that rely on Conjectures 3.1–3.4; the paper itself says it conjectures the Bayes-error characterisation. They are recorded as `phys`, and the deep Bayes GEP is listed separately as r4 (`conjecture`), with numerical support in App. A.5–A.6 (covariances at layers 4 and 7, output Gaussianity up to $L=9$).
- Depth: the formulas hold for any $L\ge1$ (App. B for $L=1$, App. C for $L\ge2$), hence both depth slugs; the Gibbs-sampler check (Fig. 1) is for $L=1$ only.
- Regime tag R6: here $n\propto d$ while the target has $O(d^2)$ parameters, so $n$ is not proportional to the parameter count; R6 is used as the Bayes-optimal teacher–student tag. $n$ is also proportional to the widths, but the paper computes no kernel renormalisation.
- Beyond $n\propto d$: the paper states that the Gaussian-equivalence properties no longer apply, argues that the Bayes error should vanish at $n\sim d^2$ because the target has $O(d^2)$ parameters, shows only numerics (r3), and names a learning-curve theory for superlinear sample sizes as an open challenge (Conclusion). This is why op-04 is listed (touched, not answered).
- r3: the trained networks' activation, initialisation and parametrisation are not stated. Fig. 2 uses full-batch GD (learning rate $8\cdot10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.1); Figs. 4 and 5 use Adam (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, 2000 epochs), so `gd_time` is `not-stated` (no time scaling given).
- Parametrisation `ntk`: the forward pass has explicit $1/\sqrt{k_{\ell-1}}$ factors with $O(1)$-variance weights; for the posterior this is equivalent to SP with matched variances.
- Sampler `other`: Fig. 1 uses the Gibbs sampler of Piccioli, Troiani and Zdeborová (arXiv 2306.02729); no convergence diagnostics are reported beyond 15000 thermalisation steps.
