summary: "The paper shows that grokking on modular arithmetic is not specific to neural networks or gradient descent. Kernel machines combined with Recursive Feature Machines, which iterate kernel regression and the average gradient outer product (AGOP), reach perfect test accuracy after a delay with zero training loss throughout. The AGOP feature matrices become block-circulant, and two progress measures, circulant deviation and AGOP alignment, improve gradually before test loss moves. Two-layer quadratic networks show the same features. A theorem shows that a quadratic kernel with circulant Mahalanobis matrices equals the Fourier multiplication algorithm."

- Read the HTML of v3 through Sec. 6 and skimmed the headings of the appendices; Appendix G (proof of Thm 5.1) not checked.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a forced choice: the main method (RFM) uses kernel regression and AGOP, not gradient descent or Bayes inference, and the paper's point is that it is "not tied to gradient-based optimization". Only r3 (networks trained with AdamW) is gradient descent. Please check this placement.
- Width: kernel machines are called infinite-width networks by the paper (Sec. 6), hence `N: to-infinity` for r1, r2, r4; the network in r3 has a finite hidden layer (width not recorded here).
- Thm 5.1 is shown for subtraction; the paper says addition is identical after replacing $C^\ell$ by $C^\ell R$, and that multiplication and division follow after discrete-logarithm reordering.
- Emergence with respect to training-set size is reported as gradual in test loss but sharp in accuracy (Appendix Fig. 6); no result is recorded for it.
