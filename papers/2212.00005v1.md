- Read the whole paper (about 8 pages). The proofs are short; I checked the structure of the proof of Thm 1, and Thm 2 repeats it with the last layer's pre-activation bounded.
- The target is fixed to the output $\hat y(W^*)$ with $W^*=0$ (all-0.5 outputs), so the loss is minimised at zero weights. The "learning" is therefore a relaxation to a trivial minimum, and $J_t\to0$ follows from an upper bound on $J_t$ that uses only $L\to0$. I recorded the claim as the paper states it.
- $J_t$ is defined implicitly by requiring the output partition function to equal the input one at all times (Eq. (14)), and $g(t)$ is chosen by hand ($g(t)=(1-e^{-t})(\frac1N\log Z-\log2)$), so the correspondence to RG is by construction of the definition.
- Training is simulated annealing (Langevin diffusion with decaying noise, Lemma 1 from Holley–Stroock); it is studied as a dynamics, hence `learning_rule: [gd]`. The paper says SGD would work given a convergence result of the form of Lemma 1, which it does not supply.
- Prior and input law are placed under `other`/`not-stated` because the input is the 1D Ising Boltzmann law, not covered by the vocabulary.
