one_line: "Noisy-gradient training of finite networks is Bayesian inference on a weakly non-Gaussian process; first-order Edgeworth perturbation theory gives the 1/N correction to NNGP predictions."
summary: "Full-batch gradient descent with weight decay and injected noise is read as Langevin dynamics whose equilibrium is the posterior of a Bayesian network with noise variance $\\sigma^2=T/2$; assuming ergodicity, the time-averaged output is a posterior mean over functions. As $N\\to\\infty$ this is the NNGP, not the NTK. At large finite $N$ the output prior is expanded in an Edgeworth series up to its fourth cumulant, which gives first-order $1/N$ corrections to the posterior mean and variance and an explicit equivalent-kernel form for large training sets. The corrections predict Langevin-trained two-layer networks much better than the NNGP, and weight sharing in the fourth cumulant is offered as the reason finite CNNs beat their NNGP on CIFAR-10."

- RG record: (a) Sense (c), neural-network field theory without any RG: the paper builds a function-space field theory for the Langevin-trained network with action $S=S_{\rm GP}+S_{\rm Data}+S_U/N$ (Eqs. (7), (8), (26)), and the words renormalisation, RG, cutoff and coarse-graining never occur. (b) Nothing is integrated out or coarse-grained: the weights are integrated exactly in the prior (Eq. (3)), and the only truncation is the Edgeworth series in the output cumulants, kept to the fourth cumulant $U$ (Eqs. (7)–(9)). (c) No coupling is renormalised: the bare $K$ and $U$ enter first-order perturbation theory in $U/N$, giving the $O(1/N)$ posterior mean and variance of Eqs. (10)–(12), and the equivalent-kernel results Eqs. (13)–(14) keep the bare ridge $\sigma^2/n$; the only recursion is the layer-to-layer map of $(K,U)$ in Eqs. (39)–(42) (PDF: C2–C5), written for any depth but evaluated only for two layers, Eqs. (44), (46). (d) The paper stops at first order (error $O(N^{-2})$ in Eq. (10)), with no resummation, flow equation or fixed point; its maturity is derivation (Apps. A, B, K) plus numerics (Figs. 1–3), with ergodicity assumed and tested numerically.
- Read the full text of the arXiv v3 HTML (30 Sep 2021), including Apps. A–K, and checked the figures in the PDF. Published as Phys. Rev. E 104, 064301 (2021) (DOI in the arXiv metadata); the journal version was not compared with v3. Main-text Eqs. (1)–(14) carry the same numbers in HTML and PDF, but the appendix equations run continuously in the HTML (Eqs. (15)–(85)) and by appendix letter in the PDF (A1–K4); for example the layer recursion Eq. (41) is (C4) in the PDF. Equation numbers in the entry are the HTML ones.
- Effective ridge and equivalent kernel: Eq. (13) is the standard equivalent kernel with the bare ridge $\sigma^2/n$ ($\sigma^2=T/2$, Sec. II.1), and Eq. (14) is built on it through the discrepancy operator $\tilde\delta=(\sigma^2/n)\tilde K^{-1}$ (App. K). The paper says higher terms in $1/n$, "as in Cohen et al. [8]", could extend the equivalent kernel to smaller $n$ and does not include them, so no ridge is renormalised here. 2405.06008 renormalises that ridge, $\sigma_c^2=\sigma^2+c$, by integrating out unlearnable kernel modes; it cites this paper only (its ref. [50]) for the Hermite-polynomial Edgeworth expansion, its Eqs. (39)–(41), applied to the feature distribution (expansion in $1/d$), not to the network output prior. 2106.04110 keeps the same bare equivalent-kernel ridge and replaces first-order perturbation theory by a saddle-point target shift, which it presents as non-perturbative in contrast to the leading-order corrections of this paper.
- Diagrammatics: App. B uses Wick contractions of the fourth Hermite functional and states that bubble diagrams are omitted; the paper never labels terms as tree or loop. The cubic and linear terms in $y$ of Eq. (11) come from the $\bar f\bar f\bar f\Sigma_*$ and $\bar f\Sigma\Sigma_*$ contractions of Eq. (28). The recursion in Eqs. (39)–(41) is of the same kind as the cumulant recursions of 2204.01058, which this paper does not cite.
- Judgement calls: `learning_rule` is `bayes` (Langevin run to equilibrium), but r1 is set to `[bayes, gd]` with `compared_other_rule: "yes"` only because the paper contrasts its NNGP kernel with the NTK of gradient flow (Secs. I–II); it runs no gradient-flow baseline, so the lead may prefer `bayes` and `"no"`. Closure is `one-loop` (first-order Edgeworth perturbation theory) for the finite-width results and `exact` for the $N\to\infty$ GP. FL type T2 with label `not-analysed` follows from the order and data dependence of the correction, not from a mechanism the paper names; Sec. V leaves open when the correction improves on the GP. Mixing is `low`: $T=2\sigma^2=O(1)$, with the autocorrelation analysis of Fig. 2(b) and App. F.
- Caveats in the paper: the Fig. 2(c) caption mentions FWC predictions for quadratic and ReLU activations, but only the quadratic FWC series is plotted and discussed (the ReLU $U$ is derived in App. D). Fig. 1 evaluates $|\bar f_U|$ from Eq. (11) on random training sets and not Eq. (14) directly; the $n$-scaling of the second term of Eq. (14) is stated from "numerical results suggest". In Fig. 3 ($n=10$) the DNN MSE$/{\rm Var}(y)$ is about 0.987–0.998 against about 1.004 for the NNGP (read from the plot), so both are near chance and the gain is small; the caption quotes slope $-2.0$, and the $n=1000$ run (Fig. 7) reaches $-1.77$. App. G.2 gives $\sigma^2=0.2$ for the CNN, while App. H quotes an effective $\sigma^2=0.1$ at "the same" noise level. No finite-width correction is computed for the CNN.
