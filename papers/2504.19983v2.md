The paper proves that the $p$-th teacher direction is recovered at a time set by its second-layer weight $a_p$ and the student's initial overlap, so that learning is a sequence of sharp transitions, and that $m=\\tilde\\Theta(P_*)$ neurons suffice for the top $P_*$ directions. For $a_p\\asymp p^{-\\beta}$ these transitions add up to power-law loss decay in steps, samples and width."

- Read the HTML of v2 through Sec. 3.1 (setting, Thm 2.1, Prop 2.2, Cor 2.3, Sec. 2.4, idealised dynamics) and the headings of the proofs (App. B-E); the proofs themselves were not checked.
- The student is 2-homogeneous, $f=\sum_k\|v_k\|^2\sigma(\bar v_k\cdot x)$, with both layers trained at once on the MSE loss; `parametrisation: other` and `prior_family: spherical` (neurons start uniform on a sphere of radius $d^{-C}$) record this.
- Cor. 2.3 is stated with $2I=k_*$ the information exponent; the paper calls the choice of $\\eta$ from $n$ and $m$ an "unstable" discretisation.
- Fig. 2(b) is gradient descent on the population loss (fixed step size), not online SGD, hence the override on r4.
- Only even links with information exponent above 2 are covered; the paper says this removes the wrong-hemisphere case.
