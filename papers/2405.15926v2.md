summary: "The paper studies a deep multi-head self-attention network, linear in its value weights, with fixed query and key weights and a Gibbs posterior over the rest. In the thermodynamic limit $N,P\\to\\infty$ with $P/N$ finite, back-propagating kernel renormalisation gives the mean predictor as kernel regression. The kernel is a sum of kernels between pairs of attention paths, weighted by an order-parameter matrix that minimises an action and aligns the kernel with the labels. This matrix equals the overlaps of the effective weights along paths. Sampling experiments on a hidden-Markov-chain task and one-shot image classification confirm the theory, and it guides pruning of heads in a trained network."

- Read the main text of v2 (Secs. 1-5) from the HTML and the sampling and temperature details of Apps. E-G; the derivation (Part II, BPKR) and the extra experiments (App. H) were not checked. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Mechanism label reuse: `patch-scale-renormalised` (T1, "per-patch kernel renormalisation") is used for the matrix $U$ over attention-path pairs, as the closest existing label (a per-branch matrix with cross entries, as for patch pairs in Aiudi et al.). This is a judgement call, since the paper's object is a path-path matrix over $H^L$ branches and not patches.
- The result statements are labelled Result 1 and Result 2 in the paper, not theorems; the derivation is a saddle-point (physics) argument, hence `phys`. Grade B because $K$ needs the empirical attentioned inputs and $U$ is found by minimising an action numerically (Adam, App. E).
- Query and key weights are fixed (designed for the Markov task, taken from a gradient-trained network for the one-shot task), so the theory does not cover their learning. `learned_layers: other` records this. In r4 the gradient-trained comparison network learns all weights.
- Mixing is `low`: NUTS at $\mathcal{T}=0.01$ with 10 chains for $N\le100$; for $N\ge1000$ no samples were taken. The theory is compared with samples down to $N=10$, below the number of independent entries of $U$ (136).
