summary: "The paper studies learning $\\sigma(w^\\star\\cdot x)$ on Gaussian data when the link has information exponent $k^\\star\\ge3$, where online SGD on the correlation loss needs $d^{k^\\star-1}$ samples. Replacing the loss by a spherical average over a perturbation of size $\\lambda$ raises the signal-to-noise ratio of the stochastic gradient while the weight vector is near the equator, with $\\lambda$ up to $d^{1/4}$. A first stage with smoothing and a second stage without it give $n\\gtrsim d^{k^\\star/2}+d/\\epsilon$ samples, which matches the correlational statistical query lower bound that the paper also proves. The paper links this to tensor PCA and conjectures the same for tuned minibatch SGD."

- Read Sections 1 to 7 of the HTML version in full; the appendices (proof of Thm 1 in App. B, experimental details in App. E) were not read.
- Venue: neither the arXiv record nor the first page names one, so `preprint`.
- Mechanism label `not-analysed` is used throughout: the analysis is of the gradient signal-to-noise ratio and the growth of the overlap $\alpha$ of a single weight vector, not of a change in features. A label for escape from the equator exists in another open ingest pull request; copying it here makes `validate.py` fail, so it is not used.
- The model is a single neuron $\sigma(w\cdot x)$ on the sphere (well specified), so `depth: one-hidden-layer` and `architecture: other` are the closest allowed values. Theorem 1 treats $k^\star\ge3$ only; for $k^\star=1,2$ unsmoothed online SGD already reaches $d$ samples.
- Section 7.2 (ERM on the smoothed landscape) and 7.3 (minibatch SGD as implicit smoothing) are stated as remarks and a conjecture, not proved, and are not recorded as results.
- Theorem 1 is for online SGD with the learning-rate schedule written in the statement; the heuristic of Section 5 assumes an optimal step size.
