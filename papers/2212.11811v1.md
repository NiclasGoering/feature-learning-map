summary: "The paper reviews the correspondence that maps an ensemble of one-hidden-layer networks to a field theory: infinite width is a free Gaussian-process theory and finite-width corrections are interactions. For a translation-invariant kernel it measures effective quartic and sextic couplings of a local interaction ansatz by sampling networks. It then identifies the weight standard deviation $\\sigma_W$ with the cut-off of a Wetterich-equation renormalisation group, so that changing $\\sigma_W$ moves the couplings along an RG flow. At zeroth order in momentum the flow is pure power-law scaling in $\\sigma_W$, stated without displayed derivation and tested for $N=2$ to $1000$ at $d_{\\rm in}=1$. It is a condensed report of an earlier, longer paper by the same authors, with preliminary numerics and no training."

- RG record: sense (c), neural-network field theory and its RG; a condensed report of the active RG of 2108.01403 for a one-hidden-layer network, with $d_{\rm in}=1$ in all numerics. Coarse-grained: output-field momentum modes above a cut-off $\Lambda$ that the text after Eq. (4.1) identifies with the weight standard deviation $\sigma_W$, through the Wetterich equation (4.2) with Litim regulator (4.5) and the two-point function pinned to the network kernel. Flows: the zero-momentum effective couplings $u_4$ (and $u_6$) of the local ansatz (3.5)–(3.6), with $\sigma_W$ as flow parameter, after dropping the momentum dependence of $\Gamma^{(4)}$ (zeroth order in $q$); the result is pure dimensional scaling, $\sigma_W\,\mathrm{d}u_4/\mathrm{d}\sigma_W=(4-d_{\rm in})u_4$, Eq. (4.6), with no loop term, so nothing beyond this truncation is computed (the equation is non-perturbative in $1/N$ at the level of (4.2) only). Maturity: the step from (4.2) to (4.6) is not displayed, and the $u_6$ law has no check; the only test is Fig. 2 ($N=2,10,100,1000$, $d_{\rm in}=1$), which the paper calls preliminary.
- Read the whole paper (arXiv HTML v1, cross-checked against the 10-page PDF; equation numbers are section-wise, "(4.6)", in both). Derived versus reported, by equation. Reported from refs. [41], [44], [46], [47] without derivation: (2.1)–(2.7), the Gaussian-process limit (2.3), Table 1, the kernel (3.2), the activation (3.1), the sampling estimators (3.3)–(3.4), and the claim after (2.7) that $S_{\rm int}$ can reproduce the expectation values "with arbitrary precision"; (2.8)–(2.9) are statements of that correspondence. Phenomenological assumption: the local ansatz (3.5), checked against samples, and the definition (3.6) of $u_4$. Stated by the authors as their own derivation, but with the computation not shown: (4.1) (Fourier transform of $K_W$, then a low-momentum approximation to $Z_0^{-1}/(\Lambda^2+p^2)$), the regulator constraint $\Gamma^{(2)}_\Lambda+r_\Lambda=\Lambda^2\exp(p^2/\Lambda^2)$ in the text after (4.4), the flow (4.2), and the scaling law (4.6) with its $u_6$ analogue. (4.2) has only the $\Gamma^{(4)}$ term; why no $\Gamma^{(3)}$ terms appear is not said. The only numerical tests are Fig. 1 (sampling check of the $1/N$ ordering) and Fig. 2 (exponent of (4.6)).
- New relative to 2108.01403 (compared with its arXiv text: Sec. 4, Appendix A and the conclusion read, the rest skimmed). No new analytic result: (4.2)–(4.6) here correspond to its Wetterich equation (3.3), constraint (4.3), regulator (4.7) and scaling results (4.11)–(4.14), while its passive RG (Sec. 3), locality and power counting (Sec. 2.2) and proofs (Appendix B) are absent here. The explicit identification $\Lambda^2=2\sigma_W^2/d_{\rm in}$ (its (4.4)) is not repeated here. Fig. 2 shows four widths where its Figs. 8–9 show ten ($N=2,3,4,5,10,20,50,100,500,1000$). The caption of Fig. 2 gives $\sigma_b=0$, whereas its Appendix A, (A.2), takes $\sigma_b=1$. The $N=10$ panel here has the same numbers as its Fig. 8(e), while the $N=2,100,1000$ panels differ ($\log_{10}|u_{4,0}|=1.660, 0.349, -0.828$ here against $1.61, 0.05, -0.97$ there); whether the runs were repeated is not stated. The outlook here (2PI formalism [48] for non-translation-invariant kernels such as ReLU, more hidden layers, an $N$ expansion, large $d_{\rm in}$) is not in the conclusion of 2108.01403, which names tensor models for non-local interactions instead.
- Inconsistencies in the paper. (i) For $d_{\rm in}=1$, Eq. (4.6) gives $\log u_4=+3\log\sigma_W+\mathrm{cst}$, but every "theory" line in Fig. 2 has slope $-3.00$ and the data fall with $\sigma_W$; the same pairing of a positive exponent with a falling plot is in 2108.01403, (4.14) against its Figs. 8–9. The paper does not comment, so I recorded (4.6) as printed in r1 and the plotted slopes in r2. (ii) Sec. 3 says all numerical tests use $\sigma_b=1$, $n_{\rm bags}=20$, $\sigma_W=1$, but Fig. 2 uses $\sigma_b=0$, $n_{\rm bags}=30$ and $\sigma_W$ from 1 to 20. (iii) "$m_{2n}=O(1/N)$ for $n>1$" is stated for all $n$ but only $m_2$ and $m_4$ are plotted, and the $u_6$ claim in Sec. 3 has no figure. (iv) The amplitudes $\log_{10}|u_{4,0}|$ in Fig. 2 fall from 1.660 ($N=2$) to $-0.828$ ($N=1000$); the paper does not discuss their $N$ dependence.
- Judgement calls. `contribution: theory-experiments` rather than `review`, because the paper presents (4.6) and Fig. 2 as its own main result, although I found them to be a condensed version of the longer paper.
