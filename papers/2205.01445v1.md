summary: "A two-layer network with Gaussian inputs takes one full-batch gradient step on its first-layer weights, after which a ridge readout is fitted on fresh data, with $n,d,N\\to\\infty$ proportionally. The first gradient is close to a rank-one matrix built from $X^\\top y$, so the trained weights acquire a spike aligned with the teacher's linear component. With step size $\\eta=\\Theta(1)$ the trained features obey Gaussian equivalence and improve on the initial random features by an explicit amount, but cannot beat the best linear predictor. With $\\eta=\\Theta(\\sqrt N)$ an upper bound on the ridge risk falls below the kernel lower bound for erf and tanh single-index teachers. Simulations with $d$ up to 2048 match the asymptotic formulas."

- Training protocol: the first layer gets one full-batch GD step with the readout $a$ frozen at its Gaussian initialisation; the readout is then fitted by ridge regression on an independent fresh sample of the same size. `learned_layers: [all]` records that both layers are learned, but by two different procedures, not by joint GD.
- Small step ($\eta=\Theta(1)$): the trained model does not simply reproduce the random-features risk. Thm 7 gives a strict improvement $\delta>0$ over the initial random-features ridge whenever $\mu_1\mu_1^*\eta\neq0$. Fact 6 then bounds the risk below by $\|P_{>1}f^*\|^2_{L^2}$, so the model stays in the "linear regime". Thm 5 and Fact 6 hold after any fixed number of steps; Thm 7 is for one step. Prop 8 (formal version Thm 31, Eq. (C.120)): as $\psi_1\to\infty$, $\delta$ has a closed form that increases with $\eta$.
- Large step ($\eta=\Theta(\sqrt N)$): only an upper bound is proved (Thm 11), for $n/d>\psi_1^*$, bounded $\sigma$ and ridge $n^{\varepsilon-1}<\lambda/N<n^{-\varepsilon}$. The constant 10 is not optimised and $C$ is not given, hence grade none. The separation from kernels is proved only for erf/erf and tanh/tanh (Cor 12); for SoftPlus, $\tau^*\approx0.03$ and simulations show non-vanishing risk (App. A.1, D.2).
- r1 (Thm 3) needs only Assumption 1: any Lipschitz teacher with $\|f^*\|_{L^2}=\Theta(1)$, hence the target override. Sec. 4 results add Assumption 2: single-index, $\mu_0^*=0$, $\mu_1^*\neq0$. The activation needs bounded first three derivatives, $\mathbb{E}\sigma(z)=0$ and $\mu_1\neq0$; it must also be odd for Thms 5 and 7 and bounded for Thm 11. Label noise is sub-Gaussian with variance $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ (`other`).
- Conjecture 4 (Gaussian-equivalent spike of the trained CK matrix and its overlap with labels) has numerical support only (Fig. 4).
- Parametrisation: the paper says its initialisation "resembles" the mean-field parameterisation and that $\eta=\Theta(\sqrt N)$ "coincides with" μP, hence the r3 override.
- arXiv HTML numbers Appendices A–D as A1–A4 (e.g. Thm 30 sits in section A3; its anchor is Thmtheo30).
- open_problems is empty: op-04 and op-07 are stated for μP posteriors, and this GD-only paper gives at most a dynamical analogue (a μP-like step with $n\propto N$).
