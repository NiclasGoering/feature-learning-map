one_line: "KL divergence rises to an asymptote with decimation steps in 1D and 2D Ising and with depth in tanh MLPs, and does not detect criticality."
summary: "The paper computes the relative entropy (KL divergence) between a reference distribution and the system after n coarse-graining steps, to compare real-space decimation RG with depth in a feedforward network. For the 1D Ising chain the normalised quantity is obtained exactly and proved monotonic, while for the 2D Ising model a truncated decimation recursion is used that breaks down above the critical coupling. For a tanh MLP with Gaussian initialisation (width 784 or 1024, depth 30 or 15, MNIST and CIFAR-10), the KL divergence between the input layer and layer m is evaluated by Monte Carlo before and after training. All three systems show a rise to a parameter-dependent asymptote that is blind to the order-to-chaos transition, and the authors present the identification of layers with RG steps as a structural analogy."

- RG record: sense (d), learning as coarse-graining measured by relative entropy, with a (b)-type comparison to the order-to-chaos criticality of signal propagation (Eqs. (56)–(63), reviewed from earlier work, not new). In the Ising models half the spins are summed out per decimation step; in the network nothing is integrated out, and layer $m$ is a deterministic function of layer 0 replaced by a Gaussian. What flows is the relative entropy $S(p\|p^{(n)})$ and its normalised form $s^{(n)}$ (Eqs. (7), (9), (21), (23), (52)) along decimation steps, with the couplings iterated by Eqs. (13)–(14) (exact in 1D) or Eqs. (40), (45) (truncated in 2D), and in the network $D(p(\mathbf z^0)\|q(\mathbf z^m))$, Eq. (83), along depth by Monte Carlo (Eqs. (84)–(87)). No expansion in $1/N$ or loops is made, and the maturity is derivation for the 1D Ising chain, approximate derivation for 2D, numerics for the networks, and analogy only for the layer-to-RG-step identification, which the paper itself calls structural and valid for any hierarchical model (Eq. (1)).
- Read the full text in arXiv HTML v2 (the whole paper, about 25 pages with appendices). The abstract says the networks and Ising models behave "qualitatively identically"; this rests on Figs. 1, 3, 7 and is a numerical observation. The "derived" parts are the Ising formulas (Eqs. (13)–(53)) and the Gaussian KL formulas (Eqs. (75), (82)–(87)); the network curves themselves are numerics.
- Network setting as read: fully connected tanh MLP, weights $W\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_w^2/N)$ and biases $b\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_b^2)$, $\sigma_b^2=0.05$, critical point $\sigma_w^2\approx1.76$ at $N\to\infty$. MNIST uses width 784 and 30 layers; CIFAR-10 (greyscale) uses width 1024 and 15 layers, with the last two layers narrowed to 400 and 10 and dropped from the plot (footnote 16) because a shrinking width makes the KL negative. Fig. 7 compares before and after training, with $\sigma_w^2=1.5$–$3.5$ (Table 1). Fig. 6 uses 15 epochs. The paper says "no optimizers" and gives no optimiser, learning rate or number of epochs for Fig. 7, hence `gd_step_size` and `gd_time` are `not-stated`; the paper also does not state how the layer mean and width $\mu_q$, $\sigma_q$ are set (as I read it, layer statistics).
- Quantity caveat: Eq. (83) evaluates $-S(p(\mathbf z^0))-\langle\ln q(\mathbf z^m)\rangle_p$ with $q$ a diagonal Gaussian model of layer $m$ and the expectation taken over the input-layer Gaussian pushed through the network. It is a KL divergence only up to the Jacobian and normalisation issue of Eqs. (91)–(92), which the paper avoids by keeping the width constant. The width in the bulk is large but finite, so the Gaussian-layer assumption is an approximation, which I recorded under `approximations` (`gaussian-closure`, `large-width`).
- Inconsistencies in the paper. (i) The Introduction says the 2D KL shows "no apparent change" through the critical coupling, while Sec. 2.2 finds a qualitative change above $K_c$ and attributes it to breakdown of the truncation; so the 2D insensitivity is established only for $K\le K_c$ (r5). (ii) The Discussion says broader weight distributions give a larger asymptote, but Table 1 shows the CIFAR-10 asymptote falling from 0.60 to 0.36 as $\sigma_w^2$ rises, which Sec. 3.2 acknowledges ("does not appear to hold for CIFAR-10"). (iii) Eq. (90) is a two-parameter fit and Eq. (37) is a fitted ansatz; neither is derived. (iv) In Sec. 3 the sentence on the two phases has "$\chi_1>1$" twice (the ordered phase is $\chi_1<1$). (v) Of the five $\sigma_w^2$ values in Table 1 only 1.5 lies below the critical value 1.76, so the phase comparison is lopsided.
