summary: "The paper studies one-pass SGD on a two-layer network of $N$ neurons with parameter dimension $D$, whose mean-field limit is a PDE for the neuron law. Earlier bounds needed $N\\gg D$. Here the approximation error is bounded by a term in $N$ alone plus a term in the step size, for bounded and unbounded second-layer weights and for noisy SGD. An application classifies two centred Gaussians with $O(1)$ neurons. Scaling the output by $\\alpha$ gives a short-time limit in which the dynamics is kernel regression with the initial-law kernel, and over longer times a kernel boosting dynamics with an evolving kernel."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-4) of the arXiv HTML; the long appendices (coupling proofs, PDE well-posedness, kernel limit details) were not read. Venue: the arXiv v1 first page states none, so `preprint`. Anchors checked against the HTML.
- Assumptions A1-A4 (boundedness and Lipschitz conditions on the activation, data and initial law) are in Sec. 3 and not repeated in the statements. Constants $K$ depend on them. The theorems are about the population or empirical risk of one-pass SGD; the step size must satisfy $\varepsilon\ll1/D$ in the bounded case.
- `width_limit` default is mean-field at fixed $D$, since the bound is in $N$ and $\varepsilon$ only. Thm 3 is placed at `high-dim` because $s_0=\gamma d\to\infty$ with $N=O(1)$. Thm 4 is placed at `inf-kernel`: it is a short-time result at large output scale $\alpha$, not a statement about the whole mean-field flow.
- The mean-field and kernel regimes appear as different time scales for the same scaling, not as different initialisations. The paper contrasts this with a cited view [DL19] that attributes the difference to the initialisation scale of the second layer.
- No mechanism for feature learning is analysed, so r1-r3 use `not-analysed`. The kernel boosting interpretation (Eq. (RD)) is mentioned but not exploited beyond the kernel limit.
