one_line: "Repeating each SGD step on the same sample (extragradient or SAM-type step) learns polynomial multi-index targets in $O(d)$ or $O(d\\log^2 d)$ samples, beyond the information-exponent limit."
summary: "A two-layer network with a frozen readout is trained on Gaussian data with a multi-index teacher, one sample per step. Each update first takes a gradient step of size $\\rho\\sim1/d$ and then a second step at the shifted point, as in extragradient or sharpness-aware updates. The second step sees the first on the same sample, which makes the update a nonlinear function of the label. Weak recovery then needs $O(d)$ samples if the polynomial generative exponent is 1 and $O(d\\log^2 d)$ if it is 2, which covers all polynomial links. Hard directions remain, such as 3-sparse parities, and can be reached by hierarchical learning."

- Read Sections 1 to 5 of the HTML version in full; the appendices (proofs, App. B on hard functions, App. D on larger batches, E on implementation) only through the places the main text points to.
- Venue left as `preprint`: the arXiv record has no comment and the first page names no venue.
- Theorem 2 is proved for a restricted version of Alg. 1: single-sample steps, correlation loss $1-y\hat y$, spherical normalisation, and an event of probability 1/2 on the initialisation. The paper expects it to hold more generally.
- Assumption 4 (second part) is a technical condition on $\sigma$; Lemma 1 shows it holds for $\sigma(x+b)$ for almost every bias $b$.
- The 3-sparse-parity hardness (App. B) is cited in Sec. 4.2 and not proved in the main text; r4 itself is simulation only.
- The concurrent paper of Lee et al. (arXiv 2406.01581) is acknowledged by the authors.
