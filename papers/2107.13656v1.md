summary: "The paper gives an exact formula for the expected generalisation error of the Gibbs algorithm, the posterior-like distribution proportional to the prior times $e^{-\\alpha L_E}$, with $L_E$ the empirical risk. The error equals $I_{\\rm SKL}(W;S)/\\alpha$, the symmetrized KL information between the training set and the output hypothesis, for any non-negative loss, even with dependent samples. Non-negativity and concavity in the data law follow from properties of this information, which explains why mixing domains hurts. A Gaussian mean-estimation example is solved exactly and decays as $1/n$, where an individual-sample mutual-information bound only gives $1/\\sqrt n$. Combined with a mutual-information bound, it gives an $O(1/n)$ upper bound under a sub-Gaussian assumption."

- Read the whole paper, including the proofs of Thms 1-2 and the appendix example (App. A-C).
- This is an information-theoretic bound paper on the Gibbs algorithm, placed under `bayes` because the Gibbs distribution is the posterior-like stationary law; the rule is a judgement call. It contains no neural-network setting, so architecture and most setting fields are `not-stated`, and `depth` lists both values only because the validator needs one.
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call, as for other entries without a fitting regime: it is used for the classical Gibbs setting with likelihood scale $O(1)$ in $n$. `high-dim` is placed on r2 only, where the parameter dimension $d$ and sample size $n$ appear explicitly in an exact formula; no high-dimensional limit is taken.
- In Eq. (11) the formula is for the example with $\alpha=n/(2\sigma^2)$; the exact expression in Eq. (17) of the appendix is $2d\sigma_0^2\sigma_Z^2/(n\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2)$, which agrees.
- The paper states that Thm 2 holds for any $C_E\ge0$ and that $C_E=0$ is always valid (Remark 2). It calls the mean-estimation example's loss left-tail sub-Gaussian but not sub-Gaussian.
