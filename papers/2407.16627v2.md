summary: "The paper studies approximate sampling from the uniform measure on the solutions of the symmetric binary perceptron, with $m=\\lfloor\\alpha n\\rfloor$ random constraints. It proves transport disorder chaos: a small resampling of the constraint matrix moves a constant fraction of the measure by Wasserstein-2 distance of order $\\sqrt{n}$, at every density $\\alpha>0$. Algorithms that are stable under such perturbations therefore cannot sample nearly uniformly. Sublogarithmic-depth polynomial-size circuits and bounded-depth subexponential circuits are shown to be stable by Fourier analysis. Efficient search, possible at low density, must then return atypical solutions."

- Read Secs. 1-3 including the proof of Thm 2.1 and the Boolean-circuit stability argument; the Fourier-analysis details for Thm 3.5 and App. material were skimmed.
- Learning rule `bayes`: sampling the uniform measure on solutions is the zero-temperature posterior of a perceptron with binary weights, in the classical $P=\alpha\cdot\#$params setting (R6, `high-dim`). The paper itself speaks only of sampling the solution set.
- The satisfiability threshold $\alpha_{\rm SAT}(\kappa)$ and the constraint on $\alpha$ in Thm 2.1 were garbled in the HTML text I read; I recorded "below the satisfiability threshold" from the introduction's description (the set is non-empty only there). Check against the PDF.
- The paper gives a rigorous hardness result for two algorithm classes only, and says MCMC/Glauber is not ergodic here and simulated annealing is left open. Gaussian and Rademacher disorder are both treated; the Rademacher case has a weaker perturbation rate.
