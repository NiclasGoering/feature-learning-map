one_line: "In Bayesian linear parallel-branching networks at $P\\gg N$, branch kernel scales learn the teacher's branch norms; bias falls with narrower width."
summary: "The paper studies Bayesian parallel branching networks, mainly linear graph convolution networks whose branches use different powers of the adjacency matrix. Integrating out the weights gives one order parameter per branch, a scalar that renormalises that branch's kernel and equals the posterior mean squared readout norm. For $P/N\\to\\infty$ at zero temperature in a student-teacher setting, the order parameters converge to the teacher's branch norms (an equipartition result). Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and theory show the bias decreasing with narrower width, while variance can offset the gain. Cora and a residual MLP on CIFAR-10 show similar trends, but real-data errors are variance-limited."

- Read the HTML of v3 (10 Mar 2025): main text in full, Appendix B.1 and B.2 (generalised equipartition and residual MLP) and C.5 (HMC). The residual-MLP on CIFAR-10 results (App. B.3) were not read in detail.
- The paper calls the narrow-width result an "equipartition conjecture" in the heading of Sec. 4.1 but states it as a theorem with a short proof (law of large numbers on the teacher outputs, existence of a saddle solution). Marked `phys` because the saddle-point equations themselves come from a physics-style derivation.
- Architecture is `other` (linear graph convolution branches) with `mlp` for the residual-MLP extension; activation is linear for the main theory, ReLU in the appendix branch.
- HMC details are sparse: temperature and chains are not stated, no convergence diagnostic is reported, and the paper says HMC converges faster than Langevin. `mixing: low` is a judgement call. Samples reach only $N=1024$ (CSBM) and $N=64$ (Cora).
- The paper itself says the Bayesian setup stays in the lazy regime with minimal feature learning and that real-data error is variance-limited, so the narrow-width gain is small in practice.
