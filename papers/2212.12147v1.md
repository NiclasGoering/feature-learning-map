one_line: "Finite-width ReLU networks fall below their infinite-width limit once $P\\gtrsim\\sqrt N$, because the final NTK varies over initialisations; feature learning delays this onset."
summary: "The paper trains depth-3 ReLU networks of width $N$ by full-batch gradient descent to interpolate polynomial targets on the sphere, varying the output scale $\\alpha$ between lazy and rich training. Beyond a critical sample size $P^*\\sim\\sqrt N$ the networks do worse than the infinite-width kernel or mean-field limit, and ensembling over initialisations removes the gap, so initialisation variance drives it. Kernel regression with the final empirical NTK reproduces each network's learning curve. Richer networks reach the variance-limited regime later and plateau lower. A Gaussian covariate model with noisy features, solved by replicas, reproduces the $\\sqrt N$ scaling and mimics feature learning by amplifying the target eigenvalue."

- Read the main text in full; Appendices A to F (bias-variance decomposition, replica derivation, CIFAR/ResNet checks) were skimmed.
- All main empirical results use depth $L=3$ ReLU networks with width up to 1000 in the figures and up to 2150 in sweeps, and polynomial targets on the sphere with $D=10$. A CIFAR/wide-ResNet check is in an appendix and I only note it under datasets.
- The output scale $\alpha=\sigma^L$ is what the paper uses for laziness; I entered amplitude as small and large and set overrides per result.
- Width limit: the argument is $O(1/N)$ kernel variance and finite $N$ with $P\sim\sqrt N$, so `large-width-1/N`. The paper does not use that label itself.
- The silent-alignment label in r3 follows the paper's own comparison with the kernel picture of Atanasov et al. 2021 (arXiv 2111.00034); no relation entry is made because the paper only calls the picture similar.
