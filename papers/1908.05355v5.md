summary: "The paper studies ridge regression on $N$ random features $\\sigma(\\langle\\theta_i,x\\rangle/\\sqrt d)$ with $n$ samples on the sphere, in the limit $N,n,d\\to\\infty$ at fixed ratios. It proves the limiting test error, split into bias and variance, for a linear target plus a random nonlinear part. The nonlinear part behaves like extra noise, so only the linear component is learned. The ridgeless error has a double-descent peak at $N=n$ in both bias and variance, and falls monotonically at larger width. Vanishing regularisation is optimal in the wide limit above a critical signal-to-noise ratio."

- Read the HTML of v5 through Sec. 6 (introduction, insights, Theorems 1 to 5, Propositions 5.1 and 5.2, start of the training-error section); the proofs (Secs. 8 to 13 and appendices) were not read, and the Gaussian-covariates theorem (r4) was taken from the section overview.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for other kernel-regression entries: the object is ridge regression on fixed random features; the paper remarks that gradient flow from zero converges to the ridgeless limit.
- `width_limit: high-dim` because $N$, $n$ and $d$ grow together; infinite width ($\psi_1\to\infty$) is taken after $d\to\infty$ and is not the kernel limit.
- All theorems are for fixed $\lambda>0$. The paper conjectures but does not prove that the $\lambda\to0$ limit equals the min-norm interpolator.
- Statement numbering: Thm 3, 4, 5 are the ridgeless, wide and large-sample limits, read from the proof headings in Sec. 12, and Props. 5.1, 5.2 are the monotonicity and critical-SNR statements; I did not see the numbered headers of Thms 3 and 4 directly.
