one_line: "Lecture notes on linearised two-layer networks in high dimension: kernel, random-feature and neural-tangent regression, benign overfitting, and why lazy training fails on ridge functions."
summary: "The notes study what can be learned about wide networks from their linearisation, the neural tangent model. They start from ridge regression with concentrated features, where bias and variance have deterministic equivalents, and move to kernel ridge regression, random features and finite-width tangent models with $n\\sim d^{\\ell}$ samples. These models fit only the best degree-$\\ell$ polynomial approximation of the target when $d^{\\ell}\\ll n\\ll d^{\\ell+1}$, and interpolation can be benign. The last lecture shows that linearised networks cannot learn single-index ridge functions from polynomially many samples, which motivates mean-field scalings."

- Lecture notes, so many results are quoted from other papers (for example Thm 2 from earlier work cited in the text, Figs. 4.1, 5.1, 5.2 from others). I read the statements of Thms 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and Sec. 6.1 in the HTML of v1; the proof sketches and Secs. 6.2, 6.3 (mean-field ridge functions) were not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for other kernel-regression entries. `width_limit` lists `inf-kernel` (Chapters 1, 3, 5) and `high-dim` (polynomial scaling $n\sim d^\ell$).
- r3 uses an architecture override because the first-layer weights are frozen there; r1 to r2 have no neural network, only features or kernels.
- Theorem statements are given with the paper's conditions that $n$, $N$ and $d^\ell$ are well separated. At exactly $n\asymp d^{\ell}$ the notes say the risk curve can have extra peaks and the result does not apply.
- r5 collects Sec. 6.1's lower bound and the cited one-neuron gradient-descent result. The latter is quoted from earlier work and not proved in the notes, hence `grade: none` and `compared_other_rule: "no"`.
