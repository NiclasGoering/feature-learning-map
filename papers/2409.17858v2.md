summary: "The paper defines a two-layer linear model whose features are linear combinations of the initial infinite-width NTK eigenfeatures and evolve by projected SGD, with a parameter $\\gamma$ setting the feature-learning rate. Dynamical mean field theory gives the loss as a function of time, width, batch size and $\\gamma$. For kernel eigenvalues $\\lambda_k\\sim k^{-\\alpha}$ and source exponent $\\beta$, feature learning leaves the exponents unchanged for easy tasks ($\\beta>1$) and changes the training-time exponent for hard tasks ($\\beta<1$). It also gives the compute-optimal exponents. Depth-4 μP MLPs on power-law Fourier targets and CNNs on MNIST-1M follow the predicted exponents, and CIFAR-5M only partly."

- Read Secs 1 to 6; the DMFT derivation (Appendix C) and the exponent derivations (Appendix E) were not read.
- The model is a linear student with learned feature matrix $A(t)$ and a fixed projection $A(0)^TA(0)/N$, so features stay in the span of the initial ones. I entered `linear` architecture and `deep` only for the nonlinear-network override r5.
- The width limit is my choice: the DMFT is exact in the proportional limit $N/M, B/M\to$ const, and the analysis takes $N\to\infty$ with feature learning kept. The paper does not give a taxonomy label.
- Sec. 5 data: MNIST-1M is generated by a diffusion model and CIFAR-5M by Nakkiran et al.; I entered the generated sets under `mnist` and `other-real`.
- The paper's own caveat: on CIFAR-5M the observed exponent exceeds the prediction at large richness, and the result assumes MSE loss and SGD.
- The extends relation to 2402.01092 follows the related-work statement that the paper builds on Bordelon et al. 2024a and the reference list gives it as arXiv:2402.01092.
