summary: "The paper defines the initial alignment (INAL) between a network at initialisation and a target as the largest squared correlation of a neuron's output with the target, averaged over the initial weights. For Boolean targets on the uniform cube, it proves that if the INAL for an expressive activation is negligible, a slightly extended version of the target is not weakly learnable by polynomial-size fully connected networks with iid initialisation trained by polynomially many steps of noisy GD. The INAL splits into a sum over Fourier monomials, so a negligible INAL means the target is of high degree. Experiments on Boolean functions and CIFAR show INAL tracks learning accuracy."

- Read the HTML version through the introduction, Sec. 2 and Sec. 5; the proofs in the appendices were not checked.
- The formal Thm 1 and Cor 1 are for "fully connected" networks of polynomial size with iid initialisation and noisy GD (Appendix E explains the definition), so depth lists both values. The result is a necessary condition for learning, with no claim that high INAL suffices.
- "Expressive activation" is Definition 4; Prop 1 shows it is 1-strongly correlating. The extended target is the $N$-extension of Definition 5 (Boolean function on $N=n^{1+\epsilon}$ inputs depending only on the first $n$).
- The paper itself proves nothing for the full parity, which it conjectures is also hard on symmetric networks. Experiments use ReLU two-layer nets and, for CIFAR, a CNN with one VGG block.
