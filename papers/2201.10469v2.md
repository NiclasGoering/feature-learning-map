summary: "The paper studies noisy gradient descent on mean-field two-layer networks, written as mean field Langevin dynamics minimising an entropy-regularised convex functional of the neuron law. A proximal Gibbs distribution $p_q$ built from the functional derivative measures the optimality gap, in the way a gradient norm does in convex optimisation. With a uniform log-Sobolev inequality this gives linear convergence in continuous time for any regularisation strength, and convergence up to a step-size-dependent error in discrete time. For empirical risk, the duality gap equals $\\lambda\\,\\mathrm{KL}(q\\|p_q)$. A small simulation illustrates the gap."

- Read the full main text (Secs. 1-5) of the arXiv HTML; the appendices (LSI verification, discrete-time proof details) were only skimmed. Checker pass: full text incl. App. A-B read. The venue is not on the first page of v2; AISTATS 2022 (PMLR 151) is taken from the reference list of 2212.03050. Anchors checked against the HTML. The iteration complexity $O(\epsilon^{-1}\alpha^{-2}\lambda^{-2}\log\epsilon^{-1})$ was checked against the HTML.
- Assumption 1 asks $\delta F/\delta q=O(1+\Vert\theta\Vert^2)$ uniformly over $q$, and App. A.1 gets the uniform LSI only for $|\partial_z\ell|\le C_1$, $|h_\theta|\le C_5$; Lemma 1 also needs $\Vert\partial_\theta h_\theta\Vert\le C_3$. An untruncated readout $a\sigma(w\cdot x)$ with square loss meets none of these.
- The weight decay sits in $F$ and the entropy is against Lebesgue measure, so the implied prior is $\mathcal N(0,\frac{\lambda}{2\lambda'}I)$, with variance proportional to $\lambda$. Empirics: the student is trained by noisy GD (a discretised Langevin dynamics of the particles) and $p_q$ is sampled with the Langevin algorithm, hence `ula`.
- Learning-rule convention follows INGEST.md: stationary and dual statements (Prop 1, Thms 3-4) are `bayes`, the dynamics (Thms 1, 2) are `gd`. No result compares the two numerically. The tag R3 is used for the mean-field fixed-data limit, as in the Hu et al. entry.
- The uniform LSI of Assumption 2 is verified only for bounded smooth neurons with an $\ell_2$ penalty, with constant $\alpha=2\lambda'/(\lambda e^{O(1/\lambda)})$, which is exponentially small in $1/\lambda$. The paper lists this dependence as a limitation. Theorem 2 and Corollary 2 require the discretisation error $\delta_\eta$ to be $O(\eta)$, shown in Lemma 1 under a second-moment condition and smoothness.
- Finite-particle error is not analysed; the paper only remarks that Mei et al.'s propagation-of-chaos bound grows exponentially in time. Empirics: one regression simulation (Fig. in Sec. 4.2) with a two-layer sigmoid teacher; the primal entropy is estimated with a $k$-nearest-neighbour estimator and $p_q$ is sampled with the Langevin algorithm.
- The related work says Chizat (2022) was concurrent and independent and analysed the continuous-time rate and annealing; that paper is now in the map and the `same-result-as` relation is added (same LSI convention, same rate).
