summary: "The paper derives a self-consistent mean-field theory for the Bayesian posterior of two-layer nonlinear networks sampled with Langevin dynamics. At infinite width with NTK scaling it reduces to kernel ridge regression, while at finite width it predicts a symmetry-breaking transition at a critical sample size or noise, where the order parameter jumps from zero. Plain mean field underestimates the post-transition gain on sparse parity. The authors attribute this to self-reinforcing input feature selection, and capture it with a coordinate-wise learnable prior variance (MF-ARD). They prove that ARD removes the ambient-dimension penalty from the critical noise, and the theory then tracks the learning curves of trained networks."

- Read the v1 HTML through Sec. 5 and the experimental tables; the appendix derivations (cavity free energy, App. D proofs) were not checked.
- Regime: experiments use $\gamma=1/2$ (NTK scaling) with finite $N$ and $P/N$ between 0.02 and about 70, with a fixed noise $\kappa$, so I tagged R1 and `finite-proportional`. The theory has $\gamma$ as a parameter, and I did not check the other values of $\gamma$. The R0 tag and `inf-kernel` are for the explicit NNGP limit (r5).
- Mechanism labels: onset of learning is recorded with the existing `kernel-grows-along-target` (the paper links the transition to an outlier eigenvalue of the learned kernel, App. A.4). The self-reinforcing selection of relevant input coordinates and neurons (IFS) had no matching label, so `input-feature-selection` (T4) is new. The tag `specialisation` marks that neurons specialise to the target after the transition.
- `closure: gaussian-mixture` and `prior_family: scale-mixture` describe MF-ARD (Gamma prior on the coordinate precisions integrates to a Student-type marginal); the plain MF theory uses an isotropic Gaussian prior.
- Status of r2: the statement that the transition exists in plain MF is proved as Thm D.7 in the appendix; the agreement with SGLD networks is numerical (Fig. 3).
- Mixing marked `high`: full-batch Langevin at $T=2\kappa^2$ down to $10^{-6}$ with an equilibrium that is assumed after $7.5\cdot10^6$ epochs; the paper reports no convergence diagnostics. The statement 'semi-quantitative' in the abstract refers to plain MF.
