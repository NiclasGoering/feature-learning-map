one_line: "A self-consistent mean-field theory of the SGLD posterior of two-layer networks predicts a feature-learning transition; adding learnable per-coordinate prior variances (ARD) matches learning curves."
summary: "The paper derives a self-consistent mean-field theory for the Bayesian posterior of two-layer nonlinear networks sampled with Langevin dynamics. At infinite width with NTK scaling it reduces to kernel ridge regression, while at finite width it predicts a symmetry-breaking transition at a critical sample size or noise, where the order parameter jumps from zero. Plain mean field underestimates the post-transition gain on sparse parity. The authors attribute this to self-reinforcing input feature selection, and capture it with a coordinate-wise learnable prior variance (MF-ARD). They prove that ARD removes the ambient-dimension penalty from the critical noise, and the theory then tracks the learning curves of trained networks."

- Read v1 (ICLR 2026 camera-ready header) in full from the PDF text, including App. A, D (all proofs, Eqs. 46-137), E and F; checked the HTML anchors.
- Regime: the loss is normalised by $P$ (Eq. 2: $\frac1{2\kappa^2P}\sum_\mu(f-y)^2$), so at fixed $\kappa$ the posterior tends to a fixed population posterior as $P\to\infty$; Thm D.7 is stated in this infinite-$P$ limit at finite $N$. There is no $P/N$ limit, so the entry uses `finite-exact` and `p_vs_n: other` (not R1 / `finite-proportional`). The critical $P_c$ seen in the figures comes from finite-sample estimates of $J$, $\Sigma$. R0 / `inf-kernel` is the NNGP limit (r5).
- Status: the MF factorisation is an uncontrolled approximation at finite $N$, Thm D.7 holds for the reduced equations after a point-mass ansatz (proof of Thm D.6), Thm 4.1 is conditional on Assumption (100), and Thm D.1 takes term-wise $N\to\infty$ limits inside the MF model, so r2, r3, r5 are `phys`.
- The main text says the transition occurs "when $\omega_0>1$"; by Eq. (11) $m_S=\omega_0/(1+\omega_0)$ at fixed $\omega_0$, and Thm D.7 gives the onset as $A^\star(0)>\sigma_a^{-2}$, i.e. $\kappa<\kappa_c$. Eq. (93) follows from the quadratic in the proof of Thm D.6 (last term divided by $\kappa^2$); Eqs. (92), (94), (96) drop that $1/\kappa^2$. App. D.1 Eqs. (59)-(60) write the ridge as $\kappa^2/(2\sigma_a^2)$; Eq. (10) ($\kappa^2/\sigma_a^2$) is the correct one. Lemma D.13 calls $N$ the number of samples; it is the width.
- Mechanism labels: onset recorded with `kernel-grows-along-target` (outlier eigenvalue, App. A.4); `input-feature-selection` (T4) for the ARD feedback. `closure: exact` because the MF single-site law is sampled with particles (App. E); Thm D.7 evaluates it at a point mass. `prior_family: scale-mixture` is MF-ARD: the Gamma hyperprior on $\rho_j$ is shared by all neurons (Eq. 37), so the network prior is a Gaussian scale mixture; the Student-type marginal quoted after Eq. (13) is for one neuron only.
- Mixing marked `high`: full-batch Langevin at $T=2\kappa^2$ down to $5\cdot10^{-7}$ with an equilibrium that is assumed after $7.5\cdot10^6$ epochs; the paper reports no convergence diagnostics. The statement 'semi-quantitative' in the abstract refers to plain MF.
