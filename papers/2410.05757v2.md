summary: "The paper addresses how to choose the posterior temperature in Bayesian deep learning without a grid search. It treats the tempered posterior as the posterior of a tempered model, and estimates the inverse temperature by maximum likelihood jointly with the weights during warm-up. A singular-learning-theory expansion shows temperature has little effect for regular models at large $n$, and a bound for Gaussian linear regression motivates the method. On UCI regression, MNIST and CIFAR-10, with SGMCMC, the selected temperatures give test log predictive density and accuracy close to grid search, 4 to 8 times faster. The paper also contrasts the cold-posterior view of Bayesian deep learning with that of generalised Bayes."

- Read the full HTML text of v2 (Secs. 1-7, main experiments); appendices (counter-example in App. A, proofs in App. E, sampler settings in Apps. H-J) were not read in detail. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call, as no regime fits a non-asymptotic posterior. `high-dim` is put on r3 (ResNet20 on CIFAR-10) to satisfy the tag rule. r1 is a large-$n$ expansion at fixed model, recorded with `P: to-infinity`.
- Mixing is `high`: the SGMCMC runs reach $\beta$ up to 1000 ($T=10^{-3}$) without a Metropolis correction. Convergence is judged by the kinetic-temperature diagnostics of Wenzel et al., listed as `other`, not by agreement with a theory.
- The regression models are one-hidden-layer ReLU networks and the classifiers are a CNN (MNIST) and ResNet20 (CIFAR-10); `depth` lists both and r2, r4 narrow it.
