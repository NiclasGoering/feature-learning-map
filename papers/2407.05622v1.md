summary: "The paper introduces Differentiable Learning Queries (DLQ), statistical queries given by gradients of a chosen loss, and characterises the query complexity of recovering the support of a sparse function over a general product input distribution. Adaptive and non-adaptive complexities are $\\Theta(d^{k_*})$, with $k_*$ a leap or cover exponent of the set system of subsets detectable by the queries. With squared loss DLQ equals correlation queries, while with $\\ell_1$, hinge or exponential loss it equals general SQ. For a two-layer network in the mean-field and linear-sample regime, the DLQ leap exponent decides whether SGD escapes the saddle."

- Read the HTML version through Sec. 7 and the informal statements of Thms 5.1, 6.3, 7.1 and 7.2. Proofs and appendices were not checked.
- The main theorems (r1, r2) are about query algorithms, so `learning_rule: [gd]` is a judgement call: DLQ is the paper's model of gradient-based learning for a chosen loss, but it is more powerful than (S)GD on a fixed model and its lower bounds are for worst-case noise.
- The neural-network results (r3, r4) are for hypercube inputs and a two-layer network. r3 is the mean-field PDE limit in linear scaling; r4 is layer-wise SGD with a high-degree polynomial activation, so `activation` and `input` are overridden there.
