one_line: "Overparametrised ReLU networks with Gaussian priors have balanced layer norms, Dirichlet norm reallocation on equal-probability manifolds, and prior-like weight marginals along likelihood-flat directions."
summary: "The paper studies how redundant hidden units reshape the weight-space posterior of Bayesian neural networks with isotropic Gaussian priors. For two-layer ReLU networks, the norm of each minimal-norm neuron is spread over its copies with a symmetric Dirichlet law, so weight means and covariances shrink as width grows. For deep homogeneous networks, stationarity gives an exact balancedness identity between adjacent layers' expected squared norms. A Laplace argument shows the posterior covariance equals the prior covariance along likelihood-flat directions. Experiments with up to 10 million posterior samples from thousands of chains support these three effects on UCI, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-6) and the setup parts of Apps. D-E; the proofs in App. C were not checked. Theorem 1 is stated informally in the main text.
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call: no regime fits a non-asymptotic finite-width posterior. R6 is used for the experiments where the parameter count exceeds the sample count ($P<\#$params), placed under `high-dim` in r4 only.
- Cor. 1-2 need the posterior to put substantial mass near the minimum-norm manifold and a weak-limit assumption; the paper says its analysis is local to that neighbourhood. Theorem 2 assumes a stationary law with standard regularity.
- Sampler: the large sample runs use MILE (warmstart plus sampling, as configured by Sommer et al. 2025); CIFAR-10 uses scale-adapted SGHMC. `mixing: low` is a judgement call; no convergence diagnostics are reported beyond cumulative LPPD over chains.
- Mechanism: the paper analyses weight geometry, not features, so `not-analysed` is used for all results.
