summary: "A teacher with quadratic activation, Gaussian weights and extensive width $m=\\kappa d$ generates labels from $n=\\alpha d^2$ Gaussian inputs. Expanding the square reduces learning to estimating a Wishart matrix from generalised linear observations. Universality and a matrix-GLM free entropy, stated as conjectures, plus extensive-rank matrix denoising, give the Bayes-optimal error and a perfect-recovery threshold matching degree-of-freedom counting. The GAMP-RIE algorithm combines AMP with rotationally invariant denoising and reaches this error. In noiseless runs, randomly initialised gradient descent has about twice the Bayes-optimal error, and averaging over initialisations matches it."

- Read Secs. 1-6 of the HTML v1; the derivation appendices were not checked. The abstract says "closed-form", but the error needs a numerical solution of Eq. (9). The paper states the universality of the sensing matrices (Conj. 4.1) and the matrix-GLM free entropy (Conj. 4.2) as conjectures, so the main result is recorded as `phys`.
- Quadratic activation with readout fixed to 1: `learned_layers: first-layer-only`. `quadratic-term-alignment` is introduced here and reused in the 2510.24616 entry. T2 is the closest FL type; the network does not specialise by construction, so please check the fit.
- The paper's GD claims are empirical: the factor of two in the noiseless case is "very close to exactly" twice, and the heuristic is explicitly non-rigorous. Learning rates used were 0.2 ($d=200$) and 0.07 ($d=100$), constant.
- Grade A is a judgement: Eq. (9) needs only $\kappa,\alpha,\Delta$ and a numerical free-convolution integral.
