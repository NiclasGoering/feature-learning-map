summary: "Weakly non-linear (finite-width or quartic-loss) corrections to Gaussian-process regression with a power-law kernel spectrum are treated as a field theory whose modes are kernel eigenmodes. Integrating out the low-eigenvalue modes renormalises the ridge parameter and the quartic coupling. Because the vertex is non-local in mode space, the paper replaces scaling dimensions by scaling intervals and finds no Wilson-Fisher fixed point, only scale-dependent separatrices. The quartic term is irrelevant at large data size, so learning curves approach the Gaussian-process scaling with power-law corrections. The renormalised ridge predicts the mean predictor, and the flow suggests how to rescale hyperparameters between data sizes."

- Read the HTML of v2 (1 Jan 2026) for Sec. I to VII and parts of Appendices A, B, I; the appendix derivations (C, D, F, G, H) were not checked.
- The field theory is derived for GP regression with an added quartic loss term, motivated in App. B by $1/N$ finite-width corrections of a one-hidden-layer network. The main text states the theory for fully connected deep networks and says CNNs and transformers follow by trading width for another redundancy parameter. Depth therefore lists both `one-hidden-layer` and `deep`; a judgement call.
- Mechanism: the only change from the Gaussian-process kernel is the flowing ridge $r(\ell)$, i.e. a scalar per mode scale, not a change of kernel eigenfunctions. T1 / `kernel-scale-renormalised` is the nearest existing entry; the checker may prefer `not-analysed` if a ridge change is not read as a kernel change.
- Simulations use a custom Langevin class with burn-in and thinning on synthetic power-law data (App. H, I). The paper does not report autocorrelation or convergence checks, so `mixing: low` is a judgement call.
- The paper states that the scaling-law agreement between the continuous formulas and the discrete sums is good for $P\gtrsim10^3$ (Gaussian) and $P\gtrsim10^6$ (non-Gaussian); the paper does not state the largest simulated $P$ in the sections read.
