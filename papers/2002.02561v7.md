For dot-product kernels on a sphere, including the NTK, a mode of degree $k$ is learned when the sample size reaches $P\\sim d^k$. Since wide networks trained by gradient flow equal NTK regression, the curves predict network learning curves; synthetic data and MNIST agree."

- Read the HTML of v7 up to the Conclusion; the Supplementary Information (proofs, replica details, spectra of the NTK, Secs. 1-14) was not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice: the theory is for kernel ridge regression (no gradient descent or Bayes inference), and applies to wide networks through the NTK equivalence with gradient flow to zero training loss (Sec. 3.2). The ridgeless limit is the case relevant to networks.
- The mode error in Prop 3 holds "under the PDE approximation" (Sec. 2.2): the paper notes that its dependence on a single $\bar w_\rho$ is an artefact of the approximation. Its replica route is a finite-size saddle point, so status is `phys` and the theory is called approximate by the authors.
- Grade `B`: the learning curve needs the kernel eigenvalues and the target's mode weights. For spherical inputs these are known in closed form for a given kernel, but Prop 3 itself does not assume this.
- Eq. (30) is stated for large $\alpha$ with a constant given only in the SI. The multi-output extension (Sec. 2.5) and the Gaussian-kernel experiment (Fig. 4a) are not recorded as results.
