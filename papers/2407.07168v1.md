one_line: "Single-instance Franz-Parisi replica theory of transfer learning in one-hidden-layer networks at $P\\propto N$; a renormalised source-target kernel makes transfer beneficial where infinite width fails."
summary: "A target network is trained while its first layer is coupled with strength $\\gamma$ to a quenched sample from the source-task posterior. The replica method, with kernel renormalisation justified by Gaussian equivalence, gives a free energy in the proportional limit with four order parameters and a source-target kernel. Theory matches simulations on correlated EMNIST and CIFAR-10 pairs. Test loss falls with $\\gamma$ when the tasks share many features, and only a small fine-tuning coupling helps when they share half. In the infinite-width lazy limit the coupling gives no gain, while narrower networks beat the best uncoupled predictor."

- Read the full HTML of v1 (9 Jul 2024; a short main text of 4 sections) and the experimental-setup appendix; the replica derivation in the appendices was not checked line by line.
- The paper does not name its sampler or temperatures for the "equilibrium configurations" of the source and target networks, so `sampler: other`, `temperature: not-stated`; `mixing: low` is a judgement call. Results are averaged over 5 to 10 source configurations.
- Source-target coupling is on the first layer only; the readout layers are trained separately with independent Gaussian priors. $\beta_t\to\infty$ is available as an interpolation limit.
- The paper conjectures extensions to convolutional and deep layers in the appendix; these are not recorded as results.
- The venue is not given on the arXiv page, so `preprint`.
