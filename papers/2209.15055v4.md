summary: "The paper studies the function-space implicit bias of fully connected networks with homogeneous nonlinearities under $L_2$ regularisation, via the representation cost $R(f;L)$ of a function at depth $L$. It shows that $R(f;L)/L$ converges, as $L\\to\\infty$, to a value between two notions of rank for piecewise linear functions: the Jacobian rank and the bottleneck rank. It conjectures that the limit equals the bottleneck rank. Global minima become approximately rank one when depth is too large, with both a Jacobian and a hidden-representation statement. A range of depths growing with the number of data points recovers the true rank."

- Read the abstract, introduction and Secs. 2 to 5; the statements in Appendices A to E were not checked in detail, though main-text results are restated there (Props 6 to 11).
- No learning rule is stated: the representation cost describes the minimum of an $L_2$-regularised loss (and losses like cross-entropy), so `learning_rule: [gd]` is the closest bin. Please re-check.
- The anchor for Remark 4 is `Thmrem4`; the conjecture is stated in Remark 4 and in the contribution list of Sec. 1.
- `open_problems: [op-09]` because the open-problem note names this paper's depth-dependent rank results among the known limits; the paper itself does not treat Bayesian posteriors.
- New mechanism `bottleneck-rank-bias` (T4). The paper identifies it in Sec. 4, where global minima are approximately rank one in the Jacobian and in a hidden layer. It describes a change in the features, not an outcome, and no existing label covers rank collapse of a representation across layers.
