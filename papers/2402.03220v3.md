one_line: "Reusing one batch of $O(d)$ samples lets a two-layer network learn, in two gradient steps, multi-index directions that one-pass SGD cannot, beating the exponents."
summary: "A two-layer network with fixed readout and symmetric initialisation is trained by full-batch gradient descent on $n=\\alpha d$ Gaussian samples with a multi-index teacher. Using rigorous dynamical mean-field theory, the paper shows that after two steps the first layer overlaps with every target direction that is not hard in the sense of Definition 3.1. Hard directions, such as those with even or exchange symmetry, are not learned in any finite number of steps. This disproves a conjecture of Abbe et al. and separates multi-pass GD from one-pass SGD, which is limited by information and leap exponents."

- Read Sections 1 to 5 of the HTML version in full; the appendices (proofs, the examples in A.7 to A.9, generalisation results in A.10) were not read.
- Readout $a$ is frozen at initialisation with a symmetric initialisation so that $f=0$ at start. This is stated as an assumption "amenable to rigorous theoretical guarantees", hence `learned_layers: first-layer-only`.
- Thm 3.2 holds for some $\eta,\lambda>0$ and requires $\sigma$ analytic with $\sigma^{(k)}(0)\neq0$ for all $k$; the main-text figures use ReLU, which does not meet this, so Figs. 1-2 are illustrations beyond the theorem.
- r3 collects the Sec. 3.3 comparison. The one-pass rates are quoted from cited work (Ben Arous et al.; Abbe et al.; Damian et al.); the new content is the two-step claim, which rests on Thm 3.2.
- The conclusions section says the limitations of information and leap exponents, staircases and CSQ bounds apply to online SGD only.
