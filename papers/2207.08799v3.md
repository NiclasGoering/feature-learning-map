summary: "The paper studies learning $k$-sparse parities of $n$ bits with SGD on 2-layer MLPs, one-neuron networks, small Transformers and product networks. Experiments on small $(n,k)$ find convergence in about $n^{O(k)}$ steps with a sharp phase transition, which is close to the statistical-query limit and unlike random search. A theorem shows that for a ReLU MLP of width $2^{O(k)}$ the population gradient at initialisation already reveals the parity support through a gap between Fourier coefficients of orders $k-1$ and $k+1$. For an idealised product network the paper also gives the gradient-flow plateau and an $n^{O(k)}$ SGD bound."

- Read Secs 1 to 6 in full. The appendix proofs and the extra experiments in Appendix C were not read line by line.
- Product networks (PolyNets) multiply $k$ linear maps, so I set depth to deep for the two PolyNet results r4 and r5 only; the paper does not call them deep. The architecture list for the paper default also contains one-neuron sinusoidal networks and a Transformer under `other` and `attention`.
- Thm 4 needs sign initialisation and a batch of size $n^{\Omega(k)}$; the paper says the parity support appears after one SGD step at that batch size and leaves small batches open.
- The result r1 is empirical only. The `fourier-gap-amplification` label is the paper's reading; the measured Fourier gaps are in Appendix C.1 and the weight-movement progress measure in Fig. 3 and Appendix C.3.
- Grokking at small sample size and a deep counterexample to layer-wise training are reported in Sec. 5 and Appendix C; I did not make results for them.
