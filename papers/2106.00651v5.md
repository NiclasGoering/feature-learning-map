one_line: "Conjectures a universal form for the leading $1/n$ finite-width correction to hidden-layer kernels of Bayesian networks with linear readout, and verifies it on linear networks."
summary: "For a Bayesian network with linear readout, Gaussian likelihood and a Gaussian-process limit, the paper argues that the leading output-dependent correction to the posterior mean of any hidden-layer observable is fixed by the prior covariance of that observable with the last-layer kernel. It states this as Conjecture 1, not a theorem. The correction is evaluated for deep linear fully connected and convolutional networks, where it interpolates between the input and output Gram matrices at low temperature, and for a single nonlinear hidden layer. Langevin experiments on MNIST match the linear-network predictions, and a ReLU network shows stronger learning after a narrow bottleneck."

- Read the main text of v5 in full (HTML and PDF), App. B (derivation of Conjecture 1), App. C.1, App. G (predictor statistics) and App. I (numerics); App. C.2, D, E, F, H for the statements cited only.
- The central statement is an explicit conjecture ("not rigorous", Sec. 3.1 and Sec. 7); r1 is `conjecture`. The linear-network results come from the perturbative cumulant calculation (App. D) and are `phys`. Conjecture 1 has a prior-mean shift, an energy term and a log-determinant term weighted by $n_d$; no fluctuation term enters a hidden observable at this order (B.37).
- The regime has $p$ fixed while width diverges; large-$p$ corrections are left for future work (Sec. 6). App. F.2 recovers Eq. (9) as the $p/n\to0$ limit of Li and Sompolinsky, hence `special-case-of`.
- The deep-linear correction is of order $1/n$ in every hidden layer (Eq. 8, App. C Eq. C.12). This contradicts footnote 35 of 2106.10165 (lower layers at $O(1/n^2)$); that footnote is wrong by the book's own Eqs. (8.54), (8.70). The paper does not mention the footnote.
- `moves_mean` of r2: at finite $\beta$ the deep-linear mean predictor moves at $O(1/n)$, proportional to $1/\beta$ (App. G Eq. G.9); at $\beta\to\infty$ it is the least-norm interpolant (G.15). App. G (predictor with a $\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{-1}K)$ fluctuation term, test-error criterion G.20) is not a separate result because the entry has five.
- Empirics: full-batch Langevin (Euler-Maruyama) at $\beta=1$, burn-in about $2\times10^6$ steps, single network per setting, no diagnostics (App. I). `mixing: low` is the closest value ($T=1$, no autocorrelation analysis); `high` requires $T\le10^{-2}$.
