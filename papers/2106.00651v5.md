one_line: "Conjectures a universal form for the leading $1/n$ finite-width correction to hidden-layer kernels of Bayesian networks with linear readout, and verifies it on linear networks."
summary: "For a Bayesian network with linear readout, Gaussian likelihood and a Gaussian-process limit, the paper argues that the leading output-dependent correction to the posterior mean of any hidden-layer observable is fixed by the prior covariance of that observable with the last-layer kernel. It states this as Conjecture 1, not a theorem. The correction is evaluated for deep linear fully connected and convolutional networks, where it interpolates between the input and output Gram matrices at low temperature, and for a single nonlinear hidden layer. Langevin experiments on MNIST match the linear-network predictions, and a ReLU network shows stronger learning after a narrow bottleneck."

- Read the main text of v5 in full from the HTML version, plus App. G (predictor and regularisation remarks) and App. I (numerics); App. B-F and H were read for the statements cited only.
- The central statement is an explicit conjecture in the paper ("not rigorous"); r1 is therefore `conjecture`. The linear-network results are derived by the paper's perturbative cumulant calculation (App. D) and marked `phys`.
- The regime has $p$ fixed while width diverges; the paper says large-$p$ corrections are left for future work (it points to Li and Sompolinsky for $p\propto n$). So the width group is `large-width-1/N` only.
- Mixing is `high` by the vocabulary rule, but the Langevin runs use $\beta=1$ with long burn-in and a single network per setting, no diagnostics; the effect on trust is small since the theory agrees closely.
- r1 has no tested architecture of its own: it applies to any network with a Gaussian-process limit, hence the generic overrides.
