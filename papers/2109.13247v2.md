one_line: "Field theory of random recurrent and feedforward networks: mean-field criticality from the largest Lyapunov exponent, with $O(1)$ and $O(T/N)$ loop corrections to the correlation length."
summary: "The paper builds the field theory of an ensemble of random recurrent or feedforward networks with Gaussian weights, using a response-field path integral with depth as time. Its leading saddle point (mean-field theory) gives a self-consistent equation for the autocorrelation, and a double-copy stability analysis gives the largest Lyapunov exponent and the edge-of-chaos condition of earlier work. Loop corrections in the depth-to-width ratio $T/N$ are computed for linear networks and for tanh truncated at cubic order, by summing infinite series of cactus ($O(1)$) and mushroom ($O(T/N)$) diagrams, with the weight variance playing the role of the 't Hooft coupling of the $O(N)$ vector model. These corrections change the correlation length, but the critical point is found unshifted, in a regime where perturbation theory fails. There are no numerical experiments."

- RG record: Sense (c), overlapping (b); it is not a Wilsonian RG paper. Integrated out: the Gaussian weight and bias disorder, exactly (Eqs. (2.17)-(2.28)), leaving $N$ identical neurons in bilocal fields whose saddle is the mean-field theory (Eq. (2.32)); nothing is coarse-grained layer by layer. No flow equation or beta function is derived: the paper computes the mean-field Lyapunov exponent and edge of chaos (Eqs. (3.33), (3.38)-(3.39)), then the two-point function and correlation length at tree level (Eq. (4.46)) plus all $O(1)$ cactus diagrams and the $O(T/N)$ mushroom diagrams (linear: Eqs. (4.57)-(4.58), (4.70); tanh truncated at $h^3$: Eqs. (4.74)-(4.75), (4.95), App. C), where the effective weight variances $\sigma_{w,\mathrm{eff}}^2$ (4.35) and $\hat\sigma_w^2$ (4.74) set where $\xi$ diverges. Order and maturity: first order in $\gamma T/N$ (higher $T^m/N^n$ terms are noted, not computed), a perturbative derivation with no numerics, and RG enters only as an analogy (Sec. 1), a qualitative remark on a flow to a new fixed point (start of Sec. 4) and future work (Sec. 5: beta function, flow from a semi-stable critical point); equation numbers are the printed (PDF) ones.

(C.1)-(C.9e)) was skimmed only, and no diagram or convolution in Secs. 4.3-4.4 or App. B was re-derived. The paper contains no experiments; Figs. 1-4 are Feynman diagrams.

- Paper's own caveats: the finding that the critical point does not shift "should however be regarded with skepticism" because the theory breaks down at criticality (Sec. 1); Eq. (3.38)-(3.39) is a necessary condition for chaos only (Sec. 3.2); the divergence of $\hat\sigma_w^2=\gamma^2$ lies outside weak coupling (Sec. 4.4.3). The paper states that the mean-field and central-limit infinite-width correlation functions differ by an $O(1)$ term (Sec. 1, App. A), while saying it has not proven that this term leaves the law Gaussian (Sec. 5) and that it is unclear whether the earlier central-limit critical condition already contains it (footnote 3). A checker may want to read these statements against the Gaussian-process literature.
- Orders and approximations: tanh is expanded as $h-h^3/3$ and only the first two terms enter the action (Eqs. (4.13), (4.32), Sec. 4); the paper notes that corrections of order $T^m/N^n$ with $0\le m\le n$ exist (Sec. 4.4, Sec. 5), and that a $T^2/N$ term from $\delta(0)$ (Eqs. (4.76)-(4.79)) is kept under the assumption of small $\sigma_{b,\mathrm{eff}}$. $T$ has units of inverse $\gamma$ and acts as an infrared cutoff; for an MLP set $\gamma=1$, $A=B=U=0$ and read time steps as layers. The expansion parameter is $\gamma T/N$, not $1/N$.
- Relations left empty: the paper calls its approach complementary to 2106.10165 (same expansion parameter $T/N$, but no QFT there), cites 1910.00019 as earlier work on perturbative depth/width corrections and 2008.08601 as an RG-inspired Wilsonian approach, 2104.11734 for heavy-tailed exact priors that an $O(1)$ variance shift cannot explain (Sec. 5), and 2106.00651 for finite-width feature-kernel corrections. None of these is a same-result or contradicts statement, and no relation type matches "complementary".
