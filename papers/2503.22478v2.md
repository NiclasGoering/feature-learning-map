summary: "The paper models long-run SGD as fractional diffusion on a loss surface whose geometry is described by the local learning coefficient from singular learning theory. It relates the walk dimension to the learning coefficient and a spectral dimension, and derives a scalar diffusion coefficient at a chosen length scale. The local steady state is then the posterior with likelihood tempered by that coefficient, and the spectral dimension is bounded by the learning coefficient. Experiments on small language models, vision models and MNIST networks show sub-diffusive weight displacement, and on a moons dataset the tempered SGD distribution matches an SGLD posterior."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-5); proofs (App. D), the fractal-dimension background and the extra experiments (Apps. A-C, G-I) were not read.
- The paper has no regime in the sense of the map: the claims are for a fixed model in the long-time limit. The validator requires a regime tag for `bayes`, so R6 with `high-dim` was used as a forced fit. A tag for "finite model, singular learning theory" would be more accurate.
- Theorem 3.1 is a restatement of the Alexander-Orbach relation from the diffusion literature, with the learning coefficient as the medium's fractal dimension. Lemma 3.1 (scalar diffusion coefficient) is stated informally and is the key approximation behind r2, so r2 is a theorem only under that approximation, a constant $D_\xi$ and an assumed steady state. The paper does not prove that these hold for real networks.
- The length scale $\xi$ is a free parameter set by minimising the KL divergence to the SGD histogram on moons (Fig. 3), so the match in r5 is partly fitted.
- Table 1 gives $d_s$ of the same order as $\lambda$ for the language models but $d_s<1$ for the vision models, whose fitted exponent $\alpha$ is tiny (0.001 to 0.004); the claim of agreement rests mainly on the quality of the power-law fit ($r^2$).
