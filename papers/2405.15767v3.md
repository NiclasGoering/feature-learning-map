one_line: "For mean-field two-layer networks the finite-particle approximation error of mean-field Langevin dynamics is $O(1/N)$, free of the log-Sobolev constant, improving convergence and sampling guarantees."
summary: "Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) minimises an entropy-regularised convex risk of an infinitely wide two-layer network, and its $N$-particle version is noisy gradient descent. Earlier particle approximation errors scale as $\\lambda/(\\alpha N)$ and so deteriorate with the log-Sobolev constant $\\alpha$, which can be exponentially small in $1/\\lambda$. Using the Bregman divergence of the risk and the variance of an $N$-neuron network, the paper proves an $O(1/N)$ bound on the objective gap that does not involve $\\alpha$. It derives improved finite-particle convergence rates, a sampling guarantee for the mean-field stationary law and a uniform-in-time Wasserstein propagation of chaos. There are no experiments."
