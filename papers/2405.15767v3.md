one_line: "For mean-field two-layer networks the finite-particle approximation error of mean-field Langevin dynamics is $O(1/N)$, free of the log-Sobolev constant, improving convergence and sampling guarantees."
summary: "Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) minimises an entropy-regularised convex risk of an infinitely wide two-layer network, and its $N$-particle version is noisy gradient descent. Earlier particle approximation errors scale as $\\lambda/(\\alpha N)$ and so deteriorate with the log-Sobolev constant $\\alpha$, which can be exponentially small in $1/\\lambda$. Using the Bregman divergence of the risk and the variance of an $N$-neuron network, the paper proves an $O(1/N)$ bound on the objective gap that does not involve $\\alpha$. It derives improved finite-particle convergence rates, a sampling guarantee for the mean-field stationary law and a uniform-in-time Wasserstein propagation of chaos. There are no experiments."

- Read the full arXiv HTML text of v3 (main text and Thm 1-2 statements; appendix proofs skimmed only). The queue note named this paper as the resolved reading of "Nitanda 2024"; the ICLR 2024 paper by Nitanda, Oko, Suzuki and Wu is the alternative and was not added.
- Learning rule: the paper treats MFLD as noisy gradient descent, so the default is `gd`. Thm 1 and Cor 1 concern the stationary Gibbs law and sampling from it, which INGEST.md 4 assigns to `bayes`, so those two results override it. No regime tag is given because the paper-level rule is `gd`.
- The paper has no experiments. Assumption 2 (uniform LSI) is used only in Cor 2; Assumption 4 (LSI for $\mu^{(N)}_*$) is used in Thm 2 and Cor 1.
- The relation to 2402.07355 is stated in the paper as an improvement of the particle and iteration complexity in Sec. 3.2.2.
- Full-text check (v3 pdf incl. App. 5.1): r5 added (exact factorisation Eq. (19), Props. 1-2, Thm 3), the identity behind Thm 1. Assumption 1 bounds the gradient of the first variation, not the first variation itself (r1 corrected).
- Conventions: Eq. (9) is $\propto\exp(-\frac N\lambda F_0-\frac{\lambda'}{\lambda}\|x\|^2)$ with $F_0=\frac1n\sum_j\ell$ averaged over data, so the entropic weight $\lambda$ is $T$, the likelihood per datum is $N/(\lambda n)$ and the Gaussian prior $\mathcal N(0,\frac{\lambda}{2\lambda'}I)$ has variance proportional to $T$. With the loss summed over data the bound reads $nLR^2/(2N)$, i.e. $O(P/N)$.
- The proof of Thm 1 uses Assumption 1 only through the existence of $\mu_*$ and $\mu_*=\hat\mu_*$; the bound itself needs only Assumption 3 ($L$-smooth loss, $R^2=\sup_z\mathbb E_{\mu_*}h^2$). A linear Gaussian readout violates Assumption 1, so it is outside the theorem as stated.
- `tags: []` kept because the paper-level rule is `gd` (regime tags are for Bayes papers); r1, r3, r5 are stationary-law results that would carry R3. Venue: none printed in v3.
