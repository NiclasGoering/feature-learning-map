one_line: "A finite deep linear Bayesian network is a data-dependent scale mixture of Gaussian-process predictors coupled across output channels, unifying several known asymptotic kernel results."
summary: "Integrating out the first-layer weights of a deep linear Bayesian network with Gaussian likelihood writes its prior, posterior predictive generating function and mean first-layer kernel as averages over an $n_d\\times n_d$ scale matrix $L$ built from the later layers. The averaging measure depends on the data and couples output channels, which a shallow network cannot do. At zero temperature the mean predictor is the minimum-norm interpolant and, for two layers, $L$ is matrix generalised inverse Gaussian. Saddle points in several width, dataset-size and output-dimension limits reproduce earlier results of Li and Sompolinsky and of Aitchison."

- Results are derived exactly but not stated as numbered theorems; r1 and r3 are marked `thm` as exact calculations, the asymptotic ones as `phys`.
- Several formulas (Eqs. 24-25, 36, 38) are marked "expected" in the text because the limit and the average over $L$ are interchanged without proof.
- Eq. (41)'s remainder is garbled in the HTML (written $\mathcal O(\gamma^{-2})$ in Eq. 37, probably $\gamma^2$); not used.
- Mechanism labels: r1-r4 treat the output-space matrix $L$ as an output-block renormalisation (T1); r5 is the $O(1/n)$ target-aligned correction (T2). The paper does not name a mechanism type.
- No numerics. Sec. V-E (joint $n,n_d,p\to\infty$) is left open by the paper.
