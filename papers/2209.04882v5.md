one_line: "Effective action for Bayesian fully connected networks at $P/N_\\ell\\to\\alpha_\\ell$: each layer's NNGP kernel is rescaled by one order parameter, giving Student's $t$ processes."
summary: "The paper computes the partition function of fully connected regression networks with Gaussian priors in the limit where $P$ and all widths diverge at fixed ratios. For one hidden layer, a Gaussian-equivalence step based on a Breuer-Major theorem leaves a two-variable integral, solved at zero temperature to give the generalisation error in closed form from the NNGP kernel. The same scalar order parameters rescale the kernel recursively in deep networks, and the output law becomes a Student's $t$ process. At zero temperature the one-hidden-layer bias does not depend on width, and a simple observable decides whether finite width beats infinite width. Predictions are compared with Langevin sampling on MNIST and CIFAR-10."

- Read the main text and Methods (HTML v5); supplemental-material derivations (finite-mean activations, linear and quadratic cases, multi-output) were not read. The published Nature Machine Intelligence version was not compared with v5.
- The one-hidden-layer action rests on a Gaussian-equivalence step from a Breuer-Major-type theorem (Bardet and Surgailis); the paper conjectures it is exact but gives no full proof, so results are `phys`. For deep networks it adds an assumption in the same spirit and says non-Gaussian corrections were not checked.
- The theory only covers zero training loss (overparametrised, $\alpha$ below interpolation); the paper reports a different behaviour for random labels at large $\alpha_1$ (Fig. 3, left). The depth-to-infinity check ($\bar Q\to1$ numerically, Fig. 3 right) is a kernel-limit statement and is not listed as a result.
- Agreement of theory with Langevin sampling is limited by long autocorrelation times at $T=10^{-3}$ (hundreds of thousands of epochs, per the supplement), so `mixing: high`. Mean-field scaling is not used: the readout prior $\lambda_1^{-1}$ is NNGP-scaled.
