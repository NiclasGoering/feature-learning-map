summary: "The paper rewrites the exact renormalisation group (Polchinski, Wegner–Morris) as a Fokker–Planck gradient flow of a relative entropy. It then treats Bayesian inference as a flow in the number of observed data $T$ and shows that the posterior predictive, run backwards in $\\tau=1/T$, obeys a convection–diffusion equation (Eq. (141)) at first order in $\\tau$. The drift comes from the maximum a posteriori path and the diffusion from the pulled-back inverse Fisher metric. Matching this with the Wegner–Morris form gives a dictionary (Table 3): renormalisation discards data and inference reincorporates it. The main claim has no theorem, there are no numerics and no neural network is studied."

- RG record: (a) sense (d), learning/inference as inverse RG on data; the paper is formal and not about networks. (b) On the ERG side the modes between $\Lambda_R$ and $\Lambda$ are integrated out (Eqs. (2)–(6)); on the inference side the coarse-graining is the continuous discarding of observed data, indexed by $\tau=1/T$, and no neurons or weights are integrated out. (c) What flows is the posterior predictive $p_\tau$ over the data variable: Polchinski (6) and Wegner–Morris (8) are written as the Wasserstein gradient flow of $D_{KL}(p\parallel q)$, Eqs. (53)/(139), and Bayesian Diffusion is Eq. (141) with drift $m$ (135) and diffusion $K$ (134), matched to (143)–(144) in Table 3; the Normal model is exact (Eq. (111)) and the general case is first order in $\tau$ (Eqs. (133)–(137)). (d) Nothing beyond first order in $\tau$, no theorem for the main claim (only Appendix B, Eq. (183), is called a proof), no numerics; maturity is formal derivation plus analogy.
- What is proved and what is not: the paper has no theorem, proposition or lemma environments. Eqs. (111)–(116) are an explicit calculation for the Normal-mean model; Eqs. (124)–(127) and (130)–(137) are late-$T$ formal expansions (Taylor expansion to $O(\tau)$ using the Gaussian moments (132)); r1 to r4 are therefore marked `phys`. Only r5 (Appendix B, "We prove this statement now") is marked `thm`. The claim in Sec. 6 that Bayesian inference is the optimal reconstruction operator $F^*$ of Eq. (151) is asserted, not derived. No neural-network claim and no kernel-adaptation claim is made: Sec. 6 only says that (151) is the diffusion-learning problem (score-based generative models) and cites ref. [53] (Bayesian renormalization) for an application to autoencoders.
