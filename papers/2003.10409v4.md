summary: "The paper studies online SGD from a random start for rank-one estimation problems on the sphere, where the population loss depends only on the correlation with the unknown direction. It defines the information exponent $k$, the order of the first non-zero Taylor term of that loss at the equator. It proves that consistent estimation is reached with $N$, $N\\log N$ or $N^{k-1}$ samples, up to logarithms, for $k=1$, $2$ or $k\\ge3$, and that fewer samples leave the correlation near zero. Once macroscopic correlation is reached, the descent is rapid and follows the population gradient flow. Examples include single-layer networks, phase retrieval, GLMs, online PCA and spiked tensors."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 and 2 (setting, assumptions, main theorems and the discussion of thresholds); the proofs and the per-example calculations of information exponents were not read in detail.
- The setting is rank-one estimation on the sphere with a population loss depending only on the correlation $m$; the "single-layer network" application has a fixed first-layer vector and Gaussian covariates. I recorded depth `one-hidden-layer`, architecture `other` and no hidden-unit count, since the paper has one weight vector and not a hidden layer.
- Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 assume Assumption A (the population loss decreases in $m$ on $(0,1)$) and Assumption B (moment bounds on the gradient noise), and uniform initial distribution conditioned on the upper half-sphere. For $k=1$, thresholds hold only for $\alpha\to\infty$ and $\alpha\to0$ (Remark 1.6).
- The identification of $k$ with the first non-zero Hermite coefficient of the activation (for single-layer networks and GLMs) is made in Sec. 2 and was not checked line by line.
- `op-06` is listed because the information exponent is the quantity that GD sample complexities on structured targets are written in; the paper has no posterior counterpart.
