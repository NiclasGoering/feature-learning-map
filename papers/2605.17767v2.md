summary: "A two-layer network with $N\\asymp n\\asymp d$ takes two gradient steps on the first layer with step sizes $\\eta_1\\asymp N^{\\alpha_1}$ and $\\eta_2\\asymp N^{\\alpha_2}$, $\\alpha_i<1/2$. The paper shows that the weight matrix is approximated in operator norm by its initial value plus a low-rank term whose right singular vectors are nonlinear estimators of the target. The number of outlying singular values is $\\lfloor\\alpha_2/(1/2-\\alpha_1)\\rfloor$. When both steps use the same batch, some of these spikes align with target directions that have no linear component. With a fresh batch for the second step, they never do."

- Read Sections 1 to 6 of the HTML version in full; the appendices (proofs) were not read.
- Venue left as `preprint`: the arXiv record carries no comment.
- Training is layer-wise: the readout $a_0$ is fixed during the two first-layer steps, and the paper's results concern $W_2$ only. A ridge fit of $a$ afterwards is cited from earlier work, not analysed here.
- The loss in the updates is the correlation loss $1-|\bar y^\top\sigma(\bar XW^\top)a|/\bar n$; the paper says the squared loss with its initialisation is effectively equivalent.
- The cases $\alpha_1=1/2$ or $\alpha_2=1/2$ are explicitly excluded.
- Activation and target are polynomials in Hermite form for simplicity (Condition 2); the paper says this can be relaxed to fast-decaying Hermite coefficients.
- No result is stated on test error of the full network after the second step; the entry records the weight-level results only.
