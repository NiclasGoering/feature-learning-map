summary: "The paper asks whether posterior tempering in Bayesian neural networks can be replaced by a valid prior that induces confident predictions. It shows the Dirichlet prior of earlier work has unbounded density and gives an improper posterior, and fixes this by clipping log-probabilities (DirClip). On ResNet20 with CIFAR-10 and augmentation, DirClip raises test accuracy from 89% to almost 94%, close to a cold posterior, if sampling starts from a trained checkpoint. A confidence prior proportional to the maximum predicted probability converges to a cold likelihood as the temperature goes to zero, but has many local maxima and cannot be sampled in practice."

- Read the full HTML text of v1 (Secs. 1-9, appendix outline and Appendix D statement); the proofs in Appendices C-F were not checked. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call, as no regime fits a non-asymptotic posterior over a ResNet. `high-dim` is put on r2 and r4 (ResNet20 with far more parameters than samples) to satisfy the tag rule.
- Mixing is `low`: posterior samples come from SGHMC at $T=1$ (and HMC for a toy figure) with no autocorrelation or R-hat diagnostics. The paper itself notes the confidence prior is practically unsamplable because of local maxima, so claims about it are not sampler-validated.
- r1's divergence claim is proved in Appendix E.2 (not read); the paper also shows the NDG prior of earlier work is a bounded prior plus a quadratic likelihood term (Sec. 6), which is not recorded as a result.
