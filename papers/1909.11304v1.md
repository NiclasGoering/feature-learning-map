one_line: "Feynman-diagram power counting for width-$n$ correlation functions gives an $O(1/n)$ bound on NTK change during SGD and closed-form first-order $1/n$ corrections to training."
summary: "The paper adapts Feynman diagrams and the 't Hooft double-line expansion to count powers of the width $n$ in ensemble averages of a network's output and its parameter derivatives, called correlation functions. Its main result, stated as a conjecture, bounds such a function by $n^{s_C}$ using the cluster graph of contracted derivative tensors, and is proved for linear activations, for ReLU with equal inputs and for one hidden layer with smooth activation. Applied to MSE gradient flow and SGD, it gives an $O(1/n)$ change of the neural tangent kernel and updates linear in the learning rate. The authors then derive the first $1/n$ corrections to the kernel and network output in closed form, in continuous and discrete time, and test them on MNIST. An order-by-order scheme for higher orders is outlined but not carried beyond $1/n$."

- RG record: Sense (b) at best, and only in the loose sense of a perturbative $1/n$ effective theory of finite-width networks: no renormalisation is done (no coarse-graining, integrating out, counterterms, running couplings or scale dependence). The Gaussian parameter average is evaluated by Isserlis' theorem and Feynman / 't Hooft double-line power counting (Conjecture 1, Eq. (4); Thms 3, 5), and what evolves is the training-time kernel hierarchy $dO_s/dt=-\sum O_{s+1}(f-y)$, Eqs. (11), (93), truncated at $s=2r$ for accuracy $O(n^{-r})$ (Eq. (97)); this is a dynamical hierarchy, not an RG flow. The closed-form higher-order terms are first order in $1/n$ only: Eqs. (12), (13) (main text, MSE gradient flow), Eqs. (108), (110)–(112) (App. E.4.1) and Eqs. (116)–(128) (discrete time, App. E.4.2), while higher orders are described as possible in principle (App. E.4) and never computed. Maturity: derivation (Thms 1, 10, 11 proved for deep linear networks) plus numerics (Tables 1–3, Figs. 1, 10) for nonlinear networks, where the main result, Conjecture 1, stays unproved.

- Judgement calls: r1 is `conjecture` because the paper states its main result as a conjecture. r3 and r5 are `thm` only for deep linear networks (Thms 10, 11); for nonlinear networks they also need Conjecture 1 and an exchange of the large-width limit with Taylor series, hence the `linear` override. r2 and r4 are `phys` and use `generic` activation because they assume $f$, $\Theta$, $O_s$ are differentiable in $t$ (the paper says ReLU is subtler and shows smooth activations only, without naming them per figure). Dials come from the theory: training set fixed, labels $O(1)$, width only to infinity. The late-time Eqs. (111)–(112) are the $t\to\infty$ limit of the truncated expansion and are not shown to commute with the large-$n$ limit, so `gd_time` is `constant`.
- Inconsistencies for the checker: Sec. 2.1 calls Eq. (2) a deep linear network although it contains $\sigma$ (App. B sets $\sigma$ to the identity). The proof of Thm 6 (ReLU, equal inputs) is written for correlation functions without contracted derivatives ("assume for now") and the derivative case is not completed in the text. Table 1 row 5 gives exponent $-2.01$ for linear and ReLU against the bound $-1$ (tanh gives $-0.98$), and Table 3 shows several non-tight variance bounds. Figs. 1(b,c) and 10 use learning rate 0.1 and the text does not say whether the continuous-time Eqs. (12), (13) or the discrete-time formulas (116)–(128) are plotted. The improvement from $O(n^{-1/2})$ to $O(n^{-1})$ is for the expectation (with the variance bound of Thm 10), proved only for deep linear networks, so it is not directly comparable with the nonlinear-network theorem it is measured against.
