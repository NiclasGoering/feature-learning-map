one_line: "Mean-field theory of the weight posterior of non-lazy networks shows analog, redundant or sparse coding schemes depending on the nonlinearity."
summary: "The paper studies the Bayesian weight posterior of fully connected networks with readout scaled by $1/N$ and temperature scaled by $1/N$, in the limit $N,P\\to\\infty$ at zero rescaled temperature, on classification tasks. The posterior factorises over neurons: linear networks learn an analog coding scheme with a low-rank kernel and the same mean predictor as the lazy case, sigmoidal networks break permutation symmetry into a few redundant codes that depend on the target values, and ReLU networks use a few outlier neurons that carry a class each, a sparse scheme. Sampling agrees on toy tasks and MNIST, where the non-lazy predictors beat the Gaussian process through lower variance."

- Read the main text of v1 in full from the HTML version; the supplement (saddle-point derivations, sampling details D.1, D.2, extra sigmoidal and ReLU figures, GD comparison) was read for the setup and the statements cited, and by heading elsewhere.
- Mixing is `critical`: the theory predicts disconnected posterior branches (broken permutation symmetry) and the empirical samples are taken at $T=10^{-4}$ with NUTS, with no multi-chain diagnostics reported. The paper says that readouts freeze within a branch; it also compares to Langevin and gradient descent runs in the supplement, with local-minima effects noted there.
- Theory is at $T\to0$ with scaled temperature $T/N$; the sampling uses a small finite $T$. The ReLU theory is stated for $L=1$ only; deeper ReLU and sigmoidal networks are numerical.
- On CIFAR-10 (three classes, $P=100$) the coding scheme is lost on test inputs and generalisation is poor (supplement); with all ten classes and $P=50{,}000$ accuracy is 0.45. These are not recorded as separate results.
- The relation to Li and Sompolinsky is the paper's own remark that it adapts reference [20]'s techniques; Reference [20] is Li and Sompolinsky, Phys. Rev. X 11, 031059 (2021), checked in the bibliography.
