summary: "For fully connected Gaussian networks with width-tempered quadratic likelihood, the paper uses large-deviation principles for layer kernels and outputs. The posterior rate of the output equals the loss plus an RKHS norm plus the kernel rate, minimised jointly over kernels. Whenever the training labels are non-zero, the minimising kernel differs from the NNGP kernel, which the authors read as feature learning at the large-deviation scale. Numerical experiments on one-dimensional toy data with one hidden layer compare computed rate functions with Monte Carlo tails and MALA posterior samples at width 128."

- Read the full HTML v1 including appendices B-D. Proofs of Thm 4.2 are sketched from Macci et al.; Thm 4.3 is proved in App. B.3 via Varadhan's lemma.
- The tempered likelihood $e^{-n\mathcal{L}}$ with fixed $P$ is placed in the μP-like group `inf-meanfield-fixedP` (tag R3) by analogy; the paper does not use the words μP or mean-field. Weights use LeCun scaling, so `parametrisation: sp`.
- Grade B is a judgement: the rate functions are computed by nested Adam/L-BFGS optimisation over kernels (App. D), not by inner Monte Carlo. The posterior samples use "10 chains" per the appendix; no R-hat is reported, only trace plots and MALA acceptance (0.75 and 0.82), so diagnostics is `other` and mixing `low`.
- Theorems are stated for outputs on a finite input set; experiments use only one hidden layer and $d=1$ although the theory is for depth $L\ge2$ (at least one hidden layer). The paper calls the effect "feature learning" via kernel selection and does not claim it for infinite-width GD.
