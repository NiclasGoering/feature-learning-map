summary: "The paper tests theories of generalisation on kernel ridge regression with empirical neural tangent kernels of ResNets on CIFAR, Fashion-MNIST and other vision data. Norm-based bounds, spectrum-only formulas and training error all fail, while the classical generalised cross-validation (GCV) estimator is accurate over dataset sizes and ridges. Under a local Marchenko-Pastur hypothesis the paper proves a non-asymptotic bound on the GCV error, which holds for ground truths of large norm. Pretrained kernels have slower eigendecay than random ones but better alignment with the target, which explains their faster scaling."

- Read the HTML of v1 through the discussion (Secs. 1 to 7); the appendices (proofs of Thm 2 and Props 3 to 5, extra figures) were not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for other kernel-regression entries: the objects are ridge-regression predictors on fixed kernels. `inf-kernel` is used because the analysis is kernel regression; the kernels are the empirical NTKs of finite, possibly pretrained ResNets, not infinite-width limits.
- The theorems are proved under Hypothesis 1, a local Marchenko-Pastur law that the paper cannot verify directly. Appendix G only checks that its two parts are consistent on the real eNTKs.
- The pretraining finding (r4) is empirical. I used `not-analysed` with type T6 because the paper states the alignment effect but not how pretraining creates it.
- The entry for Food-101 in Table 3 has a measured rate $\alpha=0.985$ against an estimate of 0.087, which the paper does not comment on in the text I read.
