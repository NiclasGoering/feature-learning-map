summary: "The paper derives the Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) for SGD and Adam in a table of per-layer initialisation variances, learning rates and multipliers. It shows empirically on Transformers and ResNets that the optimal learning rate and other hyperparameters stay nearly constant as width grows in μP, but drift in the standard parametrization, and that wider models do not do worse in μP at any point of training. It proposes μTransfer: tune a small proxy in μP, copy the hyperparameters to the target. With proxies of 13M and 40M parameters it beats published BERT-large and 6.7B GPT-3 results. The theoretical reasons rest on earlier Tensor Programs papers; why transfer is useful is left open."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 9 and the derivation sketches in App. J; the experimental appendices (F, G) were skimmed. The paper is a method paper: the theory (the Dynamical Dichotomy and the Master Theorem) is cited from Tensor Programs IV and earlier, not proved here, so no result is `thm`.
- Sec. 9 states openly that why useful hyperparameter transfer holds is a theoretical puzzle: the optimum converges with width long before the function does.
- `width_limit: inf-meanfield-fixedP` follows the gold entry for Yang and Hu; the experiments are at finite width and with Transformers, and the infinite-width limit is the theoretical justification.
- Transfer across depth, batch size and sequence length is tested only empirically; initialisation scale does not transfer across depth (Sec. 6.1).
- The venue is listed as `preprint` because the arXiv record has no journal reference.
