summary: "The paper trains ResNets, MLPs and transformers in the maximal update parametrisation at several widths on CIFAR-5m, ImageNet, Wikitext-103 and C4, mostly with online data. Loss curves, test-point logits, preactivation distributions, feature kernels, attention matrices and edge-of-stability sharpness agree across widths above a task-dependent threshold. Narrower networks deviate later in training, through an initialisation variance that falls as $1/N$ and a bias that ensembling does not remove. The bias follows a power law in width with a task-dependent exponent. A spectral toy model attributes the bias to the deformation of kernel eigenfunctions, with the spectrum unchanged."

- Read Secs 1 to 6 in full; appendices (comparison with NTK parametrisation, bias-variance definitions, ImageNet details, hyperparameters) were not read.
- This is an empirical paper; every result is numerical. The paper default is the μP experiments; r5 uses a lazy MLP and the final kernel of ResNets, hence the overrides.
- The listed widths and epoch counts are the ones quoted in Sec. 2 as the approximate point of consistency, not sharp thresholds.
- Width limit: the paper compares to the infinite-width μP/mean-field limit at fixed training time, with online data. I chose `inf-meanfield-fixedP` since $P$ is not scaled with $N$; the paper does not give a label.
- Mechanism `eigenfunction-deformation` (r5) is a finite-width bias effect rather than a gain from feature learning. I used type T2 as the closest; a reviewer may prefer a different type.
- The datasets list includes a synthetic polynomial task used in Sec. 5.
