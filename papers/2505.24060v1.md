one_line: "Depth-two discrete networks map one-to-one to DNF formulas; prior function probability scales with DNF complexity, and weight decay concentrates the posterior on minimal formulas."
summary: "The paper studies depth-two ReLU networks with ternary first-layer weights, which are in bijection with disjunctive normal form (DNF) formulas, so the weight norm equals a DNF complexity. It derives bounds on the prior probability of Boolean functions for constant, random sparse and parity targets, showing a strong simplicity bias. A Metropolis sampler and a greedy SGD-like algorithm show that generalisation tracks prior probability and that high-order parity is unlearnable. With weight decay the posterior gains a factor exponentially small in the complexity, and the network recovers the exact minimal DNF for low-order parity. Experiments use $n=7$ inputs."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-5); the appendices (complexity-measure notes, proofs of the $P(f)$ bounds, algorithm details) were not read. Theorem 3.2 is cited from the main text only, with proofs in App. D.
- Model is a finite discrete network, width $\alpha_w 2^{n-1}$. No regime slug fits exactly: experiments at $n=7$ are `finite-exact`, and the $n\to\infty$ bounds (r2) are placed in `high-dim` with tag R6 as the nearest Bayes regime, which is a judgement call. Sampler: Metropolis-Hastings at $\kappa=1000$ with a norm penalty (`other`); the paper reports no convergence diagnostics, and says sampling is hard for large $n$ and that 6- and 7-parity are not found within budget. `mixing: low` is a judgement call (Metropolis-corrected, small $n$).
- Op-10 is listed because the greedy SGD-like algorithm is compared with the sampler on parity. The only continuous-weight SGD run (App. E.6, Fig. 13) was added as r5 by the entry check: it differs from the sampler on high parity, but architecture and rule change together.
- The entry check read App. E (algorithms, E.4-E.6). The MH loss is called MSE in Sec. 4.1 and "e.g. classification error" in App. E.2; the greedy search uses batch accuracy (Alg. 3). Bounds (r2) are leading order in $n$ for $\alpha_w\gg(3/4)^n$; all experiments are at $n=7$, $N=128$, so the scaling fields list both.
- New mechanism `minimal-clause-representation`; the paper states it in Secs. 2.2 and 4.2 (norm equals DNF length, weight decay factor $e^{-\lambda K}$).
