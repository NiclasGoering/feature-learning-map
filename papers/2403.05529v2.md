summary: "The paper studies recovering the hidden direction of a Gaussian single-index model from $n$ samples in dimension $d$. It defines a generative exponent $k^\\star$, the first Hermite order of $\\mathbb{E}[h_k(Z)|Y]$ that is non-zero, which never exceeds the information exponent. Statistical-query and low-degree lower bounds show that polynomial-time algorithms need $n\\gtrsim d^{k^\\star/2}$ samples. A partial-trace algorithm on transformed labels attains this rate, while brute force needs only $n\\sim d$. Smooth link functions with any prescribed $k^\\star$ are constructed."

- Read the HTML version, including the statements of Thms 3.2, 3.5, 4.1, 5.1, 5.2 and 6.1. Proofs were not checked.
- Not a gradient-descent paper: the results are for SQ and low-degree algorithms, so `learning_rule: [gd]` is a judgement call (SQ algorithms include gradient methods; the paper contrasts with the CSQ and online-SGD results of Ben Arous et al.). Bayes dials are `not-applicable`; GD step size and time are `not-stated`.
- No network is trained, so setting fields on architecture, activation and layers are `other` or `not-stated`, and every mechanism is `not-analysed`.
- Thm 3.5 rests on the low-degree conjecture (the paper's Footnote 1).
- The SQ bound is for recovery up to overlap $\tilde\omega(d^{-1/2})$ (weak recovery). The informal Thms 1.2 to 1.5 in Sec. 1.3 are summaries of the cited formal statements.
