summary: "Random fully connected networks with Gaussian weights, variances scaled by inverse width and a continuous nonlinearity obeying a linear-envelope bound converge in distribution to a Gaussian process with a recursive kernel. Widths of different layers may grow at different rates, provided each strictly increases. Convergence is measured empirically with maximum mean discrepancy and is slower for deeper networks. Hamiltonian Monte Carlo posteriors of 3-hidden-layer, 50-unit Bayesian networks on six small datasets agree closely with the GP posterior on five of them; yacht is the exception. The paper discusses non-Gaussian alternatives that would learn hierarchical representations."

- The theorem covers countable input sets and does not assume equal widths; Cor 5 is the equal-width case. The weights must be Gaussian, with variance scaled by inverse width.
- Sampler: HMC interleaved with elliptical slice sampling, plus Hamiltonian annealed importance sampling for the marginal likelihood. No convergence diagnostics are reported, so `mixing: low` is a judgement call (small T = O(1) problems, no autocorrelation analysis stated). The number of chains is not stated.
- op-03 is tagged because the paper uses MCMC as the reference posterior on small problems, and states that this is why only small networks could be tested.
