one_line: "A two-layer network trained by SGD with each minibatch used twice learns single-index polynomials with $\\tilde O(d)$ samples, governed by the generative exponent."
summary: "A two-layer network with random polynomial activations and random biases is trained in two phases on Gaussian single-index data. In Phase I the first layer is trained by normalised SGD in which every fresh minibatch is used for two consecutive steps; in Phase II the second layer is fitted by ridge regression. The second step on the same sample turns the label into a polynomial transform of itself, which lowers the effective information exponent. For polynomial links this gives weak recovery, strong recovery and small test error with $n=\\tilde O(d)$ samples. For general links the complexity is $d^{(p_*-1)\\vee1}$, with $p_*$ the generative exponent."

- Read Sections 1 to 6 of the HTML version in full; the appendices (proofs of Props 6, 9 and Thms 1, 2) were not read.
- The venue "NeurIPS 2024" comes from the arXiv comment.
- The mechanism label `hidden-progress` was introduced for the same-sample effect in Dandi et al. 2024 (2402.03220). Here it appears as label transformation by two reused steps; I judged this to be the same change in the features.
- Algorithm 1 is a layer-wise scheme: normalised SGD on the first layer with an interpolation (negative momentum) step, then ridge regression on the second layer. The paper itself says it is open whether standard multi-pass SGD on all parameters gives the same efficiency.
- Label noise $\varsigma_i$ is i.i.d. but its distribution is not specified in the main text, hence `other`.
- The abstract says the sample complexity matches the information-theoretic limit up to polylogarithmic factors; the constant $C(q)$ depends on the degree exponentially.
