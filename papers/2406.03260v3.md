one_line: "Exact finite-width prior and posterior of deep linear networks with several outputs or convolutions as Gaussian mixtures over Wishart matrices, with large deviation limits."
summary: "The paper proves that the output prior of a Bayesian deep linear network is an exact mixture of Gaussians whose covariance is the Gram matrix times a product of independent Wishart matrices. The matrices have size $D$ for fully connected networks with $D$ outputs and size $N_0$ for convolutional networks, and the hidden widths enter only as degrees of freedom. With a Gaussian likelihood the posterior predictive is again a Gaussian mixture, with a mixing measure tilted by the data. Large deviation theory shows that in the lazy infinite-width limit the matrices concentrate on the identity, whereas in the mean-field parametrisation they concentrate on data-dependent solutions. The authors map the results to the earlier physics literature on kernel renormalisation."

- Read the HTML of v3 through Sec. 4.2 and the proof statements; the proofs in Appendix A were not checked. The `Thmtheorem` ids in the HTML are the propositions (Thmtheorem1 is Prop. 1, 5 is Prop. 5, 6 is Prop. 6, 8 is Prop. 8, 10 is Prop. 10; Thmtheorem2–4 are remarks).
- All five results are rigorous statements, so `status: thm`; the physics reading (kernel shape or local kernel renormalisation, feature learning) is the authors' interpretation in Sec. 1.3 and Sec. 4.
- Prop. 10 is the mean-field posterior at fixed $P$, which the authors cite as complementary to gradient-descent results for deep linear networks. The proportional limit $P\propto N$ is not treated rigorously here; the paper only checks that the saddle point recovers the earlier physics equation (Sec. 4.2), which is why `op-07` is listed as touched, not answered.
- The paper also gives a dictionary to Meijer G-function formulas for $D=1$ (Sec. 4.1); that mapping is not listed as a result. Dials for r1 and r2 are set to the lazy defaults because those results concern the prior only.
- The convolutional results assume 1D convolutions, periodic boundaries and unit stride.
