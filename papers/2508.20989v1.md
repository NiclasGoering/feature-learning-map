summary: "The paper uses the proportional-limit effective action of Bayesian one-hidden-layer networks with standard scaling to define observables of feature learning. The posterior similarity matrix of the features differs from the prior one only at order $1/N_1$, but summed over neurons this gives a finite shift of the squared distance between class manifolds. That distance is a nonmonotonic function of the temperature, a peak that vanishes at infinite width or with random labels, which the authors relate to the inversion seen under gradient descent. The first-layer weight covariance also gets a finite, data-dependent correction that grows with the load. All this occurs although the output is a Gaussian process with a trivially rescaled prior."

- Read the full main text of HTML v1 and, in the fix pass, the SM in the v1 PDF (Eqs. 9–55: distance for unbalanced data, weight covariance, linear check). `venue` is `preprint`.
- Sign slip in the paper: main Eq. (8) has $+\Lambda_2$ with $\Lambda_2\propto+(\tilde K^{-1}y)\Delta(\tilde K^{-1}y)$, while SM Eq. (49) has a minus; the SM sign is the consistent one (the linear check, SM Eqs. 52–55, reproduces Eq. 13 only with it). r3 follows the SM.
- SM Eq. (47) as printed is minus the action consistent with $Z=\int e^{-N_1S/2}$ (and Eq. 46 flips the sign of $\log Q$); the saddle is unaffected. With that sign fixed, $N_1S/2$ is $N$ times the Pacelli free energy at $\rho=\bar Q$, $\eta=T$, Wishart factor $(N_1-2)/N_1$.
- r1: the paper calls both corrections $O(1/N_1)$, which is entrywise; $\Delta_2$ is rank one with norm $O(P/N_1)$ (at $\beta\to\infty$, $\Delta_2=\frac{\lambda_1}{\bar Q}yy^\top/N_1$).
- `moves_mean` is yes because the results are stated at $T>0$, where the posterior mean (Eq. 5) depends on $\bar Q$; the Discussion's "trivially rescaled prior" refers to a data-dependent rescaling.
- r2 is marked as comparing with another rule because the paper interprets the temperature peak as the equilibrium counterpart of an inversion observed under GD (reference [31] in the paper, not ingested); no GD calculation is done here, so `gd_step_size` and `gd_time` are `not-stated`. A checker may prefer `compared_other_rule: "no"`.
- The paper drops the term $y^\top\Delta_1y$ in the distance when the assumption $y^\top Ky/P=O(1)$ holds, a numerical assumption checked in SM Fig. 3: it holds for Gaussian data, roughly for CIFAR-10 (slow growth), and fails for MNIST 0/1. The nonmonotonicity statement thus rests on this.
- Fig. 2 uses a linear activation and "1500 samples"; the caption calls the plotted $\Lambda_2$ "Eq. (8)", so it is evaluated from the formula, not sampled; `sampler` is `none`.
- Zero-mean activations only; the dependence on the activation in r2 is shown with erf. The paper states that the theory does not describe feature learning near the interpolation threshold.
