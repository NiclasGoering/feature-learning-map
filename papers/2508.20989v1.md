summary: "The paper uses the proportional-limit effective action of Bayesian one-hidden-layer networks with standard scaling to define observables of feature learning. The posterior similarity matrix of the features differs from the prior one only at order $1/N_1$, but summed over neurons this gives a finite shift of the squared distance between class manifolds. That distance is a nonmonotonic function of the temperature, a peak that vanishes at infinite width or with random labels, which the authors relate to the inversion seen under gradient descent. The first-layer weight covariance also gets a finite, data-dependent correction that grows with the load. All this occurs although the output is a Gaussian process with a trivially rescaled prior."

- Read the full main text of HTML v1; the Supplementary Material (Gaussian-equivalence details, unbalanced data, scaling of $y^\top K y/P$) was not read. `venue` is `preprint`.
- r2 is marked as comparing with another rule because the paper interprets the temperature peak as the equilibrium counterpart of an inversion observed under GD (reference [31] in the paper, not ingested); no GD calculation is done here, so `gd_step_size` and `gd_time` are `not-stated`. A checker may prefer `compared_other_rule: "no"`.
- The paper drops the term $y^\top\Delta_1y$ in the distance when the assumption $y^\top Ky/P=O(1)$ holds, a numerical assumption that is checked on CIFAR-10 only in the SM; for iid random data it is exact. The nonmonotonicity statement thus rests on this.
- Fig. 2 uses a linear activation and "1500 samples"; it is unclear from the main text whether $\Lambda_2$ was computed from the formula or from sampled networks, so `sampler` is `none`.
- Zero-mean activations only; the dependence on the activation in r2 is shown with erf. The paper states that the theory does not describe feature learning near the interpolation threshold.
