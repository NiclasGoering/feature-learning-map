summary: "The paper trains the first layer of a two-layer network by mean-field Langevin dynamics while the second layer is optimal at every instant, so learning becomes selection of a kernel. It proves convexity of the reduced objective, linear convergence and particle and time discretisation errors. For targets in a union of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, the trained network needs $O(d\\log d)$ samples while any linear estimator needs $\\Omega(d^m)$. For a single-index target the kernel and parameter alignment rise from $O(1/\\sqrt d)$ and $O(1/d)$ to order one. Label noise inflates the kernel's degrees of freedom, and a label-noise procedure that provably reduces it is analysed."

- Read the main text of v2 in full from the HTML version; the appendix proofs (B, C, D) were not read line by line, only their statements in the main text.
- Learning rule: the stationary minimiser of the entropy-regularised objective is treated as `bayes` (generalisation and alignment theorems, r2 and r3), and the dynamics and its convergence as `gd` (r1, r4, r5), following the vocabulary description of mean-field Langevin dynamics. Tags R3 and R6 follow the two width groups.
- Theorem 4.5 is stated for the Boolean cube with tanh units, Theorem 5.4 for Gaussian inputs, so r2 and r3 carry different data overrides. The temperature $\lambda$ is $1/\sqrt n$ in Thm 4.4 and $\sim1/(d\log d)$ in Thm 5.4, hence `fixed` and `to-zero`.
- The paper's constants are unspecified ($c_1,\dots,c_5$), so these results are graded `none`. The label-noise result needs a multi-output setting for $\tilde\sigma>0$; the paper notes the procedure works for a single output in experiments.
- New mechanism `kernel-dof-inflation`: Sec. 5.2 states that the noise term enters the objective as $-V$, which pushes towards a larger degrees of freedom. This differs from `kernel-grows-along-target`, which concerns alignment with the target.
