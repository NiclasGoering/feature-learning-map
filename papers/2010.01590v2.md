one_line: "Deep GPs and finite Bayesian networks are deep Wishart processes over Gram matrices; a deep inverse Wishart variant, fitted variationally, beats NNGP baselines."
summary: "The paper defines deep kernel processes, in which Gram matrices are alternately transformed by a nonlinear kernel and resampled from a Wishart or inverse Wishart law. Deep Gaussian processes with isotropic kernels, finite Bayesian networks, infinite networks and infinite networks with bottlenecks are all shown to be such processes. For variational inference the authors propose the deep inverse Wishart process, whose layer widths are learnable and which reduces to the NNGP when they diverge. A doubly stochastic inducing-point scheme operates directly on Gram matrices. On UCI regression and fully connected MNIST and CIFAR-10 it generally matches or beats NNGP and deep GP baselines."

- Read the full v2 text including Appendices A-L. For finite nonlinear networks the paper is explicit that the kernel chain (Eq. 51) "cannot be evaluated analytically"; only its mean (Eq. 52) is known. The Wishart chain holds for isotropic-kernel deep GPs (Eq. 8), linear networks and bottleneck networks (Eq. 57). r2 now says so.
- r4 (added) separates the deep inverse Wishart prior from the network equivalences: it is a new model whose only link to networks is its mean and the $\delta_\ell\to\infty$ NNGP limit.
- This is a method paper on variational inference, so `learning_rule: bayes` refers to approximate Bayesian inference; the entry is placed under R0 only because the limit of diverging width is the NNGP (r2). The statements in r1 are exact identities for finite widths and carry no regime tag.
- `status: thm` for r1, r2 and r4 marks exact distributional identities derived in the paper; they are not stated as numbered theorems.
- The 'exact' closure and the squared-exponential kernel (listed under activation `other`) refer to the DGP identity; the DIWP uses variational approximations (`approximations: other`).
- Table 1 in the HTML shows relu and squared-exponential results for the same nine UCI sets; the paper does not report a mean over sets, so r3 keeps to the qualitative claim made in the text ('usually gives better predictive performance').
