summary: "The paper rewrites the field-theory dual of a randomly initialised MLP so that it respects the layerwise permutation symmetry of the neurons. Each layer becomes a lattice site, the weight matrices become link variables, and integrating out the Gaussian weights leaves a $(0+1)$-dimensional lattice gauge theory whose interactions vanish as $N\\to\\infty$. For a tanh network, expanded as a formal power series, the tree-level propagator gives a recursion for the layer variance. Summing all cactus diagrams at the next order gives a recursion for corrections that the authors state are $O(1)$ in $1/N$, with a convergence argument in an appendix. Higher-point amplitudes are discussed only qualitatively; there is no training, no numerics and no renormalisation-group flow."

- RG record: Sense (c), neural-network field theory (the NN/QFT duality of 2109.13247, built on a layer-indexed lattice), but the paper claims no renormalisation, coarse-graining step or beta function. Only the Gaussian weights and biases are integrated out, exactly (Eqs. (16)-(17)); the only "flow" is the layer-to-layer map of the variance at initialisation, $\Delta_{\ell-1}\to\Delta_\ell$ at tree level (Eq. (48)) and $X_{\ell-1}\to X_\ell$ for the sum of all cactus diagrams at leading order $O(1)$ in $1/N$ (Eq. (57), restated as Eq. (70), resummed as Eq. (124)). The $O(1/N)$ corrections are only sampled by example diagrams (Eq. (58), Eqs. (60)-(64)); nothing beyond first order is computed. Maturity: diagrammatic derivation with an asymptotic convergence argument (Appendix B), no closed form, no numerics, and the authors call the amplitude part preliminary (Sec. 5).
- Read the full text from arXiv HTML v1 (Secs. 1-5, Appendices A and B). The algebra of Appendix B (Eqs. (89)-(123)) and the symmetry factors were not re-derived; the reference list was only skimmed. The paper has no figures besides Feynman diagrams and no experiments; the only numerical remark is footnote 9 of Sec. 2 (tanh, $L=100$, $\sigma_w^2=1.76$, $\sigma_b^2=0.05$: about 5% of preactivations lie outside the convergence radius of the tanh series).
- Vocab gap: `learning_rule` has no value for "initialisation only, no inference or training". I used `bayes` with tag R2 (the ensemble of random networks is the prior), as the entry 2106.10165v2 does for its initialisation chapters. The validator forbids `not-applicable` for `likelihood_scaled`, `temperature` and `closure` in a Bayes-only entry, so I set the first two to `not-stated` and `closure` to `one-loop`.

- Judgement calls: the network analysed is not exactly Eq. (1): the covariant derivative adds a lattice-spacing term, giving $h_\ell=W_\ell(h_{\ell-1}+a\,\phi(h_{\ell-1}))+a\,b_\ell$ with $a$ a free parameter (Eqs. (6), (10), (16)); the authors ask in Sec. 5 whether this is a faithful representation of an MLP or a ResNet-like variant. The width limit of r2 is set to `inf-kernel` because the paper says the interactions vanish at large $N$ after Eq. (22) and calls Eq. (48) tree-level.
- Inconsistencies in the paper: (i) after Eq. (22) the interactions vanish at large $N$ and the theory is free, yet Sec. 2.2 and Sec. 5 say the $O(1)$ loop corrections of Eq. (57) survive in the infinite-width limit; (ii) $c_{2k}$ in Eq. (75) carries a factor $a^2$ that Eq. (35) does not, while Eq. (73) multiplies it by $a^2$ again; (iii) the paper itself notes that Eq. (48) holds only within the radius of convergence of the tanh series (after Eq. (88)). Nothing in the paper checks the recursions numerically.
