summary: "For any architecture whose function-space prior converges weakly with width, the exact function-space posterior converges weakly to the posterior formed from the limiting prior and the same likelihood. The likelihood must depend on the parameters only through the training outputs and be continuous, bounded and width-independent; Gaussian and softmax likelihoods qualify. A corollary extends this to posterior moments such as the predictive mean and variance, and in an infinite-width, finite fan-out embedding every finite set of parameters except the top-layer bias keeps its prior limit. Exact rejection sampling for a three-hidden-layer erf network shows the posterior approaching the NNGP posterior as width grows."

- Workshop paper (4 pages of main text, references on p. 5, appendices pp. 6–11). The latest arXiv version, v2 (26 Nov 2020), was read in full, including Appendix A (Example 1) and the proofs in Appendix B.
- Assumption 1 (anchor `Thmassumption1`): the likelihood is $\ell_n=\ell$ for all $n$, continuous and bounded, and depends on $\theta_n$ only through $f_n(\mathcal{X})$. Proposition 1 also needs $\int\ell\,dP_f>0$. Convergence is weak convergence of the function-space posterior on the Borel product $\sigma$-algebra. The limiting prior need not be Gaussian, so "NNGP posterior" applies only when the prior limit is a GP.
- Grade A is given to every result because the limiting predictor is closed-form GP regression on the NNGP Gram matrix when the likelihood is Gaussian. For non-Gaussian likelihoods, such as softmax, the limit is a GP posterior that still needs standard approximate inference.
- Fig. 2 sits inside the Conclusion section in the HTML (anchor `S6.F2`), although the experiments are described in Sec. 5 (`S5`, Eq. (6) at `S5.E6`). The paper does not state the training-set size or the target function for the experiment.
- The NTK curve in Fig. 1 is a reference line, not a study of gradient descent, so r2 has `compared_other_rule: "no"` and the paper is listed under Bayes only.
- closure is set to `exact` and solver to `algebraic`, since there is no closure approximation and the limit is closed form. Both are judgement calls for an R0 paper that has no saddle point.
