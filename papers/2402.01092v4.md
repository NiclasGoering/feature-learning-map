one_line: "Dynamical mean-field theory of a gradient-descent random feature model gives power-law losses in time, width and data, with asymmetric compute-optimal scaling."
summary: "The paper analyses a linear model on a random projection of power-law kernel features, trained by gradient flow or SGD on a teacher with power-law coefficients. A dynamical mean-field theory gives the full learning curve in terms of correlation and response functions, solved exactly in Fourier space. Larger models train faster, and data and width corrections accumulate over time. The test loss decays with different exponents in training time and in model or data size, which makes the compute-optimal allocation asymmetric, and ensembling is less compute-efficient than widening. A linearised ResNet on CIFAR-5M follows the predicted compute exponent, while feature-learning networks scale better."

- `learning_rule: [gd]`: the object is gradient flow and SGD on a linear model. The DMFT equations are derived by physics methods and are not rigorous, hence `phys`; the compute-optimal fit (r3) uses an additive ansatz, hence grade D.
- The venue could not be confirmed from the page header or the arXiv metadata, so it is `preprint`.
- The `extends` relation to 2002.02561 rests on the sentence that the infinite-time limit recovers "these prior works" (Bordelon et al., Spigler et al., Canatar et al., Simon et al., Bahri et al., Hastie et al.); I chose the first of these. The paper also relates its early-time corrections to Bahri et al.
- Online SGD (Fig. 3) is covered by the theory but not given its own result; in that setting there is no data bottleneck.
