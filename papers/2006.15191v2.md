one_line: "The probability that SGD finds a given zero-error function tracks its NNGP posterior probability, which is biased towards low-error, low-complexity functions."
summary: "The paper estimates the probability that SGD and its variants converge on each function consistent with a training set, and compares it to the NNGP posterior probability of the same function. Functions are labellings of a small test set, and trained networks are fully connected, convolutional and LSTM models on binarised image, text and ionosphere data. The two probabilities agree within about an order of magnitude on the high-probability functions, which are the ones SGD finds, and an exact finite-width posterior on a Boolean toy problem agrees as well. The Bayesian posterior itself is exponentially biased towards low-error, low-complexity functions. Second-order differences depend on optimiser, batch size and learning rate, and the agreement weakens with label corruption."

- Read the main text, App. A (methods, GP/MSE Eq. 5, GP/EP), App. E (CSR) and App. F (direct sampling); App. B-D read through captions.
- A function is a labelling of a small test set (50-100 points) chosen so that probabilities can be estimated by counting; results are for these coarse-grained functions and a single fixed training set. Binary classification only. `architecture: other` is the LSTM.
- The Bayesian side is the NNGP posterior: exact GP regression with noise $\sigma^2=0.002$ for MSE runs (Fig. 1a, `temperature: fixed`), and a Heaviside-link GP with 0-1 likelihood approximated by EP for CE runs (`temperature: to-zero`, `closure: gaussian`). The marginal $P(S)$ is a sign-consistency (orthant) probability, not a value-interpolation density. Only App. F (r5) uses the exact finite-width posterior (rejection sampling).
- Init: FC layers $\sigma_w=1/\sqrt{w}$ (std), $\sigma_b=0$; the prior uses $\sigma_b=0.1\sigma_w$ (App. A.1.1-A.1.2), hence `nngp-scaling`. Learning rate 0.01, batch 32 (Keras defaults).
- r5 replaces the earlier stand-alone label-corruption result, now folded into r4. The SGD nets have $|S|=10^4\approx10N$; `p_vs_n: fixed` refers to the Bayes side ($N=\infty$).
