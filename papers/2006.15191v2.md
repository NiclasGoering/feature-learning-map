summary: "The paper estimates the probability that SGD and its variants converge on each function consistent with a training set, and compares it to the NNGP posterior probability of the same function. Functions are labellings of a small test set, and trained networks are fully connected, convolutional and LSTM models on binarised image, text and ionosphere data. The two probabilities agree within about an order of magnitude on the high-probability functions, which are the ones SGD finds. The Bayesian posterior itself is exponentially biased towards low-error, low-complexity functions. Second-order differences depend on optimiser, batch size and learning rate, and the agreement weakens with label corruption."

- Read the abstract, introduction, Secs. 2-4 (Fig. 1 in detail), Sec. 5-6 and the discussion; the appendix, other figures and related-work sections were skimmed only.
- A function is a labelling of a small test set (50-100 points) chosen so that probabilities can be estimated by counting; the paper stresses that results are for these coarse-grained functions and a single fixed training set. Binary classification only.
- The Bayesian side is an NNGP posterior (analytic for MSE; expectation propagation for cross-entropy). `inf-kernel`/R0 therefore describe the Bayesian side; the SGD networks have finite width 1024. The tag `temperature: to-zero` reflects conditioning on zero training error.
- `parametrisation: sp` and `gd_step_size: constant` are taken from Keras defaults stated in the text (learning rate 0.01, batch size 32); prior scale is not stated in the sections read.
- Open problem op-10 (GD against the posterior) is listed because the paper compares SGD and posterior function probabilities; op-10 names it as prior volume evidence.
