summary: "The paper considers networks trained with noisy gradient descent, weight decay and square loss on a large training set, whose equilibrium is a finite-width posterior. A saddle-point treatment of the cumulant expansion of the output prior shows that the mean predictor equals Gaussian-process regression on a target shifted by a self-consistent term built from higher cumulants. For a two-layer linear CNN with a teacher this gives a scalar equation that matches trained networks far from the GP limit. The same framework shows a sharp transition in the spectrum of the hidden-weight covariance, where an outlier aligned with the teacher feature appears below a critical number of channels. A quadratic fully connected network is also treated numerically."

- Read the full main text of HTML v1 (abstract to discussion); the appendices (cumulant derivations, Edgeworth review, App. I) were not read in detail. `venue` is `preprint`.
- Learning rule is `bayes` because the object is the Gibbs equilibrium of noisy GD with weight decay, as the paper states. The tags are R2 / `large-width-1/N` because corrections are organised by the cumulant scaling $1/C^{r/2-1}$, but the saddle-point resummation is non-perturbative. The regime is also large-$n$ at fixed $C$; the Gaussian closure label follows the later Seroussi et al. treatment of the same idea.
- The equivalent-kernel limit and the constant $q$ in Eq. (16) carry $O(1/n)$ uncertainty; the paper says $q$ can be treated as a fitting parameter, which is why grade B was kept (it is computed from empirical GP predictions) and not A.
- The critical channel number of Eq. (18) was taken from the paper (Fig. 2 gives $C_{crit}=473$ for its parameters) and not re-derived.
- Quadratic-network result (r4) is numerical only (App. I); the paper leaves an analytic solution for future work.
