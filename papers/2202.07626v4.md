summary: "The paper analyses a two-layer ReLU network with fixed second layer, trained by full-batch GD on the logistic loss from small random initialisation. Data come from an XOR-like mixture of four clusters, which no linear classifier separates, with a constant fraction of adversarially flipped labels. At initialisation most neurons have correlation of order $d^{-1/2}$ with a cluster mean, and one large GD step amplifies this to order one. Further steps grow the aligned neurons and make them orthogonal to the opposite cluster. The trained network classifies every clean training point correctly and every noisy one wrongly, with test error at most $\\eta+O(\\sqrt{1/n})$."

- Read the HTML version through the statements in Secs. 2 to 4 (Thm 3.1, Prop 3.2, Lemmas 4.1 to 4.6, Conditions 4.7 and 4.8). Proofs in Sec. 4 and the appendices were not checked.
- Only the first layer is trained (second layer fixed at its initialisation); logistic loss; full-batch GD with a constant step size, early stopped at $T=1+1/(4\alpha)$. Fig. 1 is a two-dimensional simulation.
- Width only needs to be a constant (about $\log(1/\delta)$), so I used `finite-exact`: the results are non-asymptotic with explicit logarithmic conditions on $d$, $n$ and $m$.
- New mechanism `random-feature-amplification`: the paper names and explains it in the introduction and Sec. 4 (Lemmas 4.2, 4.4, 4.9). It is not the same as `rank-one-spike`, which concerns a single spike in the first-layer matrix, here the amplified weights are four cluster directions.
- The data are a mixture of four log-concave clusters with variance $\sigma^2\le 1/(C^2 d)$ and an adversary flipping up to a fraction $\eta\le1/C$ of labels.
