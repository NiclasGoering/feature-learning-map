summary: "The paper studies full-batch gradient descent on a two-layer network with $m$ neurons and Gaussian multi-index data, with $n/d\\to\\delta$. Using discrete-time dynamical mean field theory it shows that the hard latent directions, those with no first-order signal, are not learned in $O(1)$ steps. It then proves a spectral phase transition: the Hessian at the current iterate has a negative outlier eigenvalue whose eigenvector overlaps the hard subspace if and only if $\\delta$ exceeds an explicit threshold. The threshold is computed by Monte Carlo DMFT for GeLU and a quadratic-like activation, matches simulations, and predicts grokking for $\\delta$ just above it."

- Read Secs 1 to 4 and the proof outline (Sec. 5); the proofs in Appendices B to D and extra figures (Appendix E) were not read.
- Learned layer: the DMFT variable is the first-layer matrix $\Theta$, and $a_j,b_j$ appear as fixed numbers in Assumption 1(d), so I entered `first-layer-only`. The width factor $1/m$ in the update suggests mean-field scaling; the paper does not name a parametrisation.
- Theorems 1 and 2 are stated for a convex loss and bounded $\sigma',\sigma''$ (Remark 2.2); the square loss needs the relaxation noted after Remark 2.1. Lemma 2.3 gives block-diagonal control of the Hessian for large $m$.
- Grade C for r2 to r4: thresholds are found by running the DMFT recursion with Monte Carlo samples ($9\times10^5$ per step) and a binary search in $\delta$. The large-time value is an extrapolation by a polynomial fit in $1/t$.
- The time $\Theta(\log d)$ for the second stage is a remark in Sec. 4.2 (saddle escape time), used for the `log-dimension` override in r5.
- Section 3.4 argues, without a theorem, that the threshold is strictly above the best spectral-method threshold $\delta_{\rm opt}$, so gradient descent is suboptimal in this class; I did not make a result for it.
