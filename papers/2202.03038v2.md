summary: "The paper studies the training-error landscape of deep networks in function space, after fixing a standard parametrisation that removes permutation and scale symmetries and puts each unit on a sphere (a toroidal space). It samples minima of different flatness with SGD, replicated SGD and adversarial initialisation, and measures flatness, geodesic barriers, optimised single-bend paths and distances. Flatter minima generalise better, lie closer together and have lower barriers, and most minima are joined by near-zero-error paths with one bend. Binary-weight networks show the same trends, and the results match earlier analytical pictures for shallow models."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-6); the appendices (extra figures, binary-network details, matching algorithm proof sketches) were not read. No venue is given on the arXiv page; the first-page keyword mentions ICML but I did not confirm a publication, so `preprint`.
- Purely empirical, GD-type training only (SGD, replicated SGD, adversarial initialisation, BinaryNet), so no Bayes dials and no regime tag. `width_limit: finite-exact` means fixed finite networks; r4 uses `high-dim` as the nearest group for the hidden-manifold experiments with varying overparametrisation, a judgement call.
- Flatness is measured by local energy under multiplicative Gaussian noise on the training error, not the loss; biases and batch-norm parameters are ignored in the symmetry removal (stated by the authors).
- Linear mode connectivity barriers for VGG remain around 20% training error even after alignment; only the optimised single-bend paths are near zero.
