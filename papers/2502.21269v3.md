summary: "The paper studies a two-layer network of width $m$ with unit-norm first-layer weights, trained by gradient flow on a single-index model with $n=\\alpha md$ samples. A dynamical mean field theory, derived for a Gaussian version of the risk, predicts three regimes for mean-field initialisation: feature learning at times of order one, an extended phase where second-layer weights grow, and overfitting for $t\\gtrsim m$, where the test error rises as the features are unlearned. The Gaussian complexity of the network, tracked through the second-layer norm, controls interpolation of noise. Two rigorous results bound the generalisation gap and justify the mean-field description up to a time that grows slowly with $m$."

- Read Secs 1 to 4 in full. The DMFT derivations and the appendix (Appendices C and G) were not read; the statements in r1, r2 and r5 are as stated in Sec. 2. The paper says that the separation of timescales and the scaling functions at $t\sim m$ rest on numerical solution plus analytic derivations, and the order-$m$ scaling is "not sufficient" to pin down numerically.
- The DMFT is exact for a Gaussian version of the risk; the Gaussian approximation is checked against SGD with $d=200$ (Figs 2, 4).
- The paper default is the mean-field initialisation; r5 overrides to lazy initialisation. For r2 the second-layer weights are held at $\gamma\sqrt m$.
- Width limit: $m\to\infty$ after $n,d\to\infty$ with $n\propto md$, so `inf-meanfield-propP`. This is my choice, not the paper's.
- The rigorous results r3 and r4 hold at finite $n,m,d$ with unspecified absolute constants and any fixed $k$, hence grade none.
