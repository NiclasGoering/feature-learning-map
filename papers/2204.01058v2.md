one_line: "Rigorous $1/n$ expansion of output cumulants of random fully connected networks; depth-to-width ratio $L/n$ sets fluctuations, correlations and gradient explosion at initialisation."
summary: "The paper studies fully connected networks with Gaussian weights and L hidden layers of width proportional to n. For polynomially bounded activations it proves sharp orders in 1/n for all joint cumulants of the output and its derivatives, and exact layer-to-layer recursions in which the k-th cumulant depends at leading order only on cumulants of order at most k. Solving the recursions at criticality shows that the depth-to-width ratio L/n acts as an effective depth, controlling single-neuron fluctuations and inter-neuron correlations. This is used to prove that the exploding and vanishing gradient problem for first-layer weights occurs if and only if L/n is large. The paper does not treat training or feature learning."

- Read the v2 HTML for the abstract, Secs. 1–4 (statements, remarks and the discussion of criticality); the proofs in Secs. 5–11 and the appendices were not read.
- The entry is about the network at initialisation, so the Bayes label and R2 follow the convention used for other prior-law results. The paper states it does not treat feature learning. A human may prefer `gd` for r4, which concerns gradients, but no training dynamics are analysed.
- `width_limit` lists `large-width-1/N` for fixed-depth results and `inf-depth` for the double scaling $L/n=\xi$ in r3 and r4; `depth` lists both slugs since $L$ is arbitrary. Conjecture 4.3 (existence of a double-scaling limit for all $k$) is not recorded as a result.
- The constants $C_{2k}$ and the grade `A`: the recursions in Cor. 3.4 are explicit in terms of Gaussian expectations of the activation at the infinite-width kernel, so the grade is A.
- Theorem 4.5 is stated for single non-zero inputs, widths all equal $n$, and the $K_*=0$ class (including tanh); for ReLU the paper says the analogous result is known from earlier work.
- Venue: no journal reference on arXiv, so `preprint`.
