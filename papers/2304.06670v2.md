summary: "The paper studies fully connected networks learning Boolean functions and image data in a Bayesian picture over functions. Changing the weight scale of tanh networks moves them from an ordered to a chaotic regime and weakens the prior's bias towards low-complexity functions. Where the bias is strong, it offsets the exponential growth in the number of functions with complexity, and SGD generalises well on simple targets. Where it is weak, SGD generalises poorly. Prior times a likelihood from the error spectrum predicts the SGD posterior, and MNIST and CIFAR-10 show the same trends."

- Read the abstract, introduction and the Results section (Figs. 1-3 and Eqs. (1)-(3)) and the start of the Discussion; the Supplementary Notes and remaining appendix figures were not read.
- The paper's own Bayesian side samples random parameters (finite width), so the main entry is `finite-exact` with tag R0 attached through r5, where the infinite-width GP posterior is compared to SGD (Fig. S15 reported in the main text; anchor refers to appendix A10). No stationary sampling at low temperature is used.
- Eq. (3) averages over the $l=5$ lowest-error functions per complexity and relies on a decoupling approximation. Averaging a ratio by a ratio of averages (Eq. (2)) needs a concentrated $P(S)$. Robustness to $l=1$ or $50$ is in Figs. S13-S14.
- Op-10 is listed because r5 and r3 compare SGD with posterior function probabilities; the paper's first-order claim is the one that op-10 questions.
- The Nature Communications venue and year are from the arXiv journal-ref; the HTML shows v2.
