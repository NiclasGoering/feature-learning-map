one_line: "A sharp conservation law for kernel ridge regression gives simple closed-form risk estimates in which kernel eigenmodes compete for a fixed budget of learnability."
summary: "The paper defines the learnability of a target function as the overlap between the target and the kernel ridge predictor, and proves that summed over any orthonormal basis it is at most the number of training samples, with equality at zero ridge. Using this law and a Gaussian-feature universality assumption, it derives closed-form estimators for test risk, bias, variance and predictor covariance in terms of per-mode learnabilities. Applications include a lower bound showing parity is exponentially hard for rotation-invariant kernels, an explanation of the deep bootstrap through regularisation-limited and data-limited regimes, and a smoothness estimator."

- Read the HTML of v6 through Sec. 6.2 and the start of 6.3; Sec. 6.4 (free Fermi gas analogy), the appendices and all proofs (App. H: rigorous parts; App. I: derivation) not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for the other kernel-regression entries: the theory is for kernel ridge regression, tied to wide networks through the NTK and kernel gradient flow.
- Thm 3.2 and Prop 3.1 are rigorous and exact at finite $n$. The eigenlearning equations in Sec. 4 are nonrigorous and rest on a Gaussian-feature universality assumption (Appendix I.4), hence `phys`. The parity bound (r3) uses only exact results plus Yang and Salman's eigenvalue ordering for fully-connected kernels, so is recorded as `thm`.
- Eq. (9), the omniscient risk estimator, is $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0(\sum_i(1-\mathcal L_i)^2v_i^2+\epsilon^2)$ with $\mathcal E_0=n/(n-\sum_i\mathcal L_i^2)$ (Eq. 8), read from the HTML.
- `same-result-as` Canatar et al. (2006.13198) is the paper's own statement (Introduction, Related Work); the estimators agree but are derived differently.
- r5 compares kernel regression on binarised MNIST with a ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 as a model of the deep bootstrap; the ResNet is the setting of Nakkiran et al., and only the KRR curve is the paper's own, so `compared_other_rule` is `no` (no Bayes-versus-GD comparison).
