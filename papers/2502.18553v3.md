one_line: "Pedagogical review of field theory for deep networks: GP regression with an RG flow of the ridge, 1/N perturbation, kernel scaling and kernel adaptation."
summary: "The review writes trained deep networks as statistical field theories over outputs and pre-activations and applies perturbation theory, mean-field decoupling, saddle points and replicas to them. In the infinite-width limit, dataset-averaged Gaussian process regression is treated by an equivalent kernel, an effective ridge and a Wilsonian RG in which unlearnable kernel modes renormalise the ridge, and power-law spectra give scaling-law exponents. At finite width it shows that $1/N$ corrections grow with the data size, then presents two non-perturbative closures for Langevin-trained two-layer networks: kernel scaling (one scalar order parameter) and kernel adaptation (layer-wise kernels). A short chapter builds a dynamical field theory that links NTK dynamics to NNGP equilibrium. Derivations are physics-level, with checks on Gaussian process regression for CIFAR-10 and MNIST and on linear and erf networks."

- RG record: sense (a), a Wilsonian RG over kernel eigenmodes (Sec. 2.4.4, which reviews Howard et al.), with a secondary strand of sense (e) in the kernel scaling of Sec. 3.4. Integrated out: dataset-averaged Gaussian-process modes $k>\Lambda'$ whose eigenvalue and target power lie below the ridge, one mode at a time, under a Gaussian-discrepancy assumption. What flows is the ridge, $\kappa^2_{\rm RG,\Lambda-1}=\kappa^2+\lambda_\Lambda$ (Eq. (2.44)), integrated to $\kappa^2_{\rm RG}=\kappa^2+\sum_{k>\Lambda'}\lambda_k$ (Eq. (2.45)), at first order in the coupling of the discarded mode (Eq. (2.42)) and stopped when $\lambda_{\Lambda'}\ll\kappa^2_{\rm RG}$ fails; in Sec. 3.4 the single kernel scale $\mathcal{Q}=1+C_{\rm MF}/N$ (Eqs. (3.28), (3.32)) is renormalised at mean-field level, while the $1/N$ perturbation theory of Sec. 3.2 is a strict low-order expansion that the review says has no RG justification. The RG step itself is first order, with no higher-order or non-perturbative RG attempted; the maturity is a derivation plus Gaussian-process-regression numerics (Fig. 2.1) for the ridge flow, and a literature survey for the other RG programmes (data coarse-graining, finite-width non-Gaussianity, depth).
- The review's own RG statements, for the boil-down. (i) Sec. 2.4.4 describes RG as a greedy algorithm for path integrals: integrate out a small set of easily marginalised (small-variance) modes, treat their couplings perturbatively, follow the flow of the effective action. (ii) The flow stays tractable while $\lambda_{\Lambda'}/\kappa^2_{\rm RG,\Lambda'}\ll1$; the cut-off is set by a learnability threshold $P\lambda_{\Lambda'}/\kappa^2_{\rm RG}=\epsilon$ (0.01 in Fig. 2.1), after which the equivalent kernel, possibly with a perturbative correction, is used. (iii) With non-Gaussian discrepancies the cited RG work obtains an input-dependent ridge. (iv) Sec. 3.4 calls kernel scaling "a simple renormalization of the ridge" and names the deep-linear version of Li and Sompolinsky the Backpropagating Kernel Renormalization, exact for $P\propto N_w\propto d\to\infty$ at fixed depth (cited result). (v) Sec. 5.1 adds that Roberts, Yaida and Hanin view the kernel recursion as a discrete RG transformation.
- Open, as the review states it: an RG-based justification for keeping only a subset of finite-width corrections (end of Sec. 3.2, which stresses that its perturbation theory has no partial resummation such as self-energy or one-loop terms); the other RG-and-networks programmes surveyed in Sec. 2.4.4 (coarse-graining of data by layers or diffusion, RG on the law of random networks, depth coarse-graining, Bayesian-inference RG flows, PCA-RG) have not yet analytically predicted the generalisation performance of networks; the difference between width and data scaling exponents (Sec. 2.6); the phase diagram of feature learning (end of Sec. 3.5).
