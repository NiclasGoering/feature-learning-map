summary: "The paper identifies Kadanoff's variational real-space renormalisation group for binary spin systems with unsupervised training of restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs). Setting the RG coupling operator equal to minus the RBM energy plus the data Hamiltonian makes the RG hidden-spin Hamiltonian equal the RBM's marginal hidden Hamiltonian, so a stack of RBMs is a sequence of RG steps. This is an identity of definitions that holds for any Boltzmann machine; the RG objective (free-energy difference) and the learning objective (KL divergence) remain distinct. For the 1D Ising model, exact decimation gives a hand-built deep network whose couplings flow to $J=0$. For the 2D Ising model just above criticality, a four-layer RBM stack trained by contrastive divergence shows local receptive fields that grow with depth, a qualitative resemblance to block-spin RG."

- RG record: Sense (d), learning as RG on data: Kadanoff's variational block-spin RG on binary (Ising) data, mapped onto stacked RBMs. What is integrated out: the visible spins $v$ at each step, leaving hidden spins $h$ with Hamiltonian $H^{RG}_\lambda[h]$ (Eq. (6)), one RBM layer per RG step. What flows: the couplings $K\to\tilde K$ through $T_\lambda$; the mapping is $T=-E+H[v]$ (Eq. (18)) with $H^{RG}_\lambda=H^{RBM}_\lambda$ (Eq. (21)), exact as an identity, and the only explicit flow is the exact 1D decimation $\tanh J^{(n+1)}=\tanh^2 J^{(n)}$ (Eq. (24)) with fixed point $J=0$ (Fig. 2C), all non-perturbative, with the 2D RG only variational. The paper stays at the one-step mapping (no expansion, no fixed-point or exponent analysis in 2D); maturity: an identity of definitions, one exactly solvable example and one qualitative numerical example (Fig. 3).
- Read in full: arXiv v1 HTML (the only version; Secs. I–V, Apps. A, B, Figs. 1–3, Eqs. (1)–(25)). arXiv lists no journal reference, so the venue is `preprint`. HTML anchors checked: S1.E9, S3.E18, S3.E21, S3.E22, S3.F2, S4.E24, S4.F3.
- What is exact: Eq. (18) defines the RBM energy from the RG operator and the data Hamiltonian, and Eqs. (19)–(21) then show that the RBM's hidden marginal carries the RG Hamiltonian; the paper says this does not depend on the form of $E$, so it holds for any Boltzmann machine. It does not identify the two training objectives: variational RG minimises $\Delta F$ (Eq. (8)) and the RBM minimises $D_{KL}$ (Eq. (16)), and the paper says these are distinct variational schemes. It is therefore not shown that contrastive-divergence training finds an RG transformation. Eq. (19) divides by the RBM partition function $\mathcal Z$ and the paper does not comment on how it relates to $Z$ of Eq. (2).
- Examples done: (i) 1D Ising, exact decimation, Eq. (24), with a hand-built deep network (Fig. 2B); nothing is trained, and the paper notes the network ignores half of the visible spins (Sec. IV.1). Result r3 carries `inf-depth` only because the flow $J^{(n)}\to0$ is stated for $n\to\infty$ layers. (ii) 2D Ising, periodic $40\times40$ lattice at $J/(k_BT)=0.408$ (the paper quotes 0.4352 for the transition, so just above $T_c$, not at criticality), RBM stack 1600-400-100-25, contrastive divergence, L1 strength $2\times10^{-4}$, 200 epochs, momentum 0.5, minibatch 100 (App. A), no convolutional structure. The evidence is visual only (receptive fields, three reconstructions at compression 64, Fig. 3): no measured flow of couplings, no comparison with a block-spin transformation, no run at the critical point, no test of the $\Delta F$ objective.
- Inconsistencies in the paper: Sec. IV.2 says 20,000 samples, App. A says 40,000. Sec. IV.2 calls the local block structure emergent, while App. A says the L1 strength was chosen so that all-to-all couplings are impossible, so locality is partly enforced by the penalty. Eq. (10) writes $\sum_i b_j h_j$ (index mismatch). `gd_time: constant` in r4 only records the fixed 200 epochs; no time scaling is analysed.
