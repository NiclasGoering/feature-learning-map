summary: "The paper studies binary classifiers of $n$-bit strings produced by random deep networks with Gaussian weights, treated through the infinite-width Gaussian-process approximation. It proves that the average Hamming distance to the closest string with a different classification is at least $\\sqrt{n/(2\\pi\\ln n)}$, and that the expected number of random bit flips needed to change the class grows linearly in $n$. A uniformly random classifier would give one and two respectively. Experiments with two hidden layers confirm both scalings and the prefactor, and trained MNIST networks keep the scaling for random inputs. The result supports the conjecture that the prior over functions is biased towards simple functions."

- Read the full text including Appendix A and the statements of Theorems 1-2; the proofs in Appendices B-C and Appendix D (greedy versus exact search) were not checked.
- The theorems are proved inside the Gaussian-process approximation (outputs jointly Gaussian across layers); the paper notes that this is exact only as $n_1,\ldots,n_L\to\infty$. The theorems are stated for any activation, but the bound $F'(1)\le1$ and so the explicit constant $\sqrt{n/(2\pi\ln n)}$ is for ReLU. For tanh the prefactor can shrink exponentially in depth.
- `learning_rule: [bayes]` because the object is the prior over functions (random initialisation) used in PAC-Bayes bounds; no likelihood or posterior appears. `p_vs_n` and `P` are `not-stated` since there is no training set in the theory.
- The abstract says "settle the conjecture" of Valle-Perez et al. about simple functions; the paper measures simplicity by insensitivity to input changes, not by Lempel-Ziv complexity, and says that not every simple function is likely under the prior.
- Experiments use the same width as input length ($n$ neurons per hidden layer), 2 hidden layers; no bias; weight variance $2/n_{in}$.
