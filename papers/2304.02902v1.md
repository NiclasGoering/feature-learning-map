summary: "Permutation and activation symmetries make the parameter posterior of a Bayesian MLP a union of $|\\mathcal{E}|$ equioutput copies of one reference set. For a finite symmetry group and a transformation-invariant prior, the posterior predictive density reduces to an integral over that set. A probabilistic bound then gives the number of independent chains that suffices to visit all functionally distinct modes, whatever the architecture. Experiments with NUTS and one sample per chain on tanh networks show equal or better test log density than a single long chain and deep ensembles. A post-hoc symmetry-removal algorithm exposes a three-mode posterior for a small network."

- Read the full main text and Table 1; the supplementary proofs (0.A) and algorithm details (0.B) were not checked. The arXiv page gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call: no regime fits a non-asymptotic Bayesian MLP posterior. `high-dim` is placed on the experiment result r3 only to satisfy the tag rule; the paper does not give the parameter-to-sample ratio.
- Prop. 1 and Cor. 1 hold only for a finite set of equioutput transformations (tanh); the paper states ReLU scalings are excluded and argues informally that the conclusion carries over.
- Prop. 2 assumes chains reach modes independently with known probabilities. The 1274-chain example uses assumed values $\nu=3$, $\pi=(0.57,0.35,0.08)$, not estimated ones. `mixing: low` is a judgement call (NUTS, one sample per chain, no diagnostics).
- Table 1 has 9 datasets and 2 networks (18 settings); the text says equal or better in all but two experiments, and I did not recount which.
