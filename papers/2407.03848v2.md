summary: "The paper compares networks trained by SGD to randomly sampled networks that fit the training set, accepted by guess and check, on binary MNIST and CIFAR-10 tasks with 16 or a few tens of training samples. It shows that an earlier finding that the two behave alike came from a poor SGD initialisation, and proposes a Lipschitz-normalised loss for comparing architectures. Increasing width improves SGD test accuracy and margin but leaves sampled networks unchanged, so the benefit is a bias of SGD. Increasing depth hurts both equally, so it is an architectural bias. Results hold for LeNet, MLP and a small ResNet."

- Read Secs. 1-5 in full, App. B (architectures, Tables 1-4, datasets) and App. C (Figs. 7, 11-13); Apps. D-E (extra class pairs) through captions only. Venue from the PDF first page (Proceedings of the 41st ICML) and the arXiv comment; v2 fixes a Kaiming Gaussian initialisation bug that the authors say does not affect the results.
- Regime tag: there is no Bayes regime slug for finite-width posterior sampling with few samples. R0 (likelihood O(1), P fixed) is the nearest, and `finite-exact` is the honest width slug. The validator ties R0 to the infinite-width group, so `inf-kernel` was added at paper level only to satisfy it; no infinite-width computation is in the paper (widths are finite), and the checker should decide whether the tag and slug are acceptable.
- The Bayesian side is guess and check: exact rejection sampling from a layer-wise uniform (Kaiming-uniform) prior conditioned on zero training error, the posterior at $\beta\to\infty$ with a 0-1 likelihood; Kaiming Gaussian only in Fig. 1. Its acceptance rate (r4, added) is a sign-interpolation volume, not a value-interpolation evidence. r5 (Fig. 13) was added as well.
- r3: guess and check degrades with depth throughout; SGD only on harder pairs (CIFAR-10, MNIST 3 vs 5), so "both alike" holds only there.
- SGD settings: cross-entropy loss, 60 epochs, batch size 2, fixed learning rate, no weight decay or augmentation; runs without zero training error are dropped.
- Op-10 is listed since the paper compares SGD with guess and check on small data and finds SGD better at large width (the open problem cites this paper).
