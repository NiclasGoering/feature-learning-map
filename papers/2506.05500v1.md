summary: "The paper extends the generative exponent of single-index models to Gaussian multi-index models with a fixed number $r$ of indices. A leap decomposition of the index space defines the generative leap exponent $k^\\star$, which never exceeds the information leap exponent. A low-degree lower bound shows that $n\\gtrsim d^{k^\\star/2}$ samples are needed. A sequential spectral U-statistic over Hermite tensors recovers the index space with $n\\sim d^{1\\vee k^\\star/2}$ samples and needs no prior knowledge of the model. Case studies give $k^\\star$ for Gaussian parity, intersections of half-spaces, polynomials and deep ReLU networks with biases."

- Read the HTML version through Sec. 5 and the statements of Props 1 to 8. Proofs in the appendices were not checked.
- Not a gradient-descent algorithm: the estimators are spectral and the lower bound is for low-degree tests. `learning_rule: [gd]` is a judgement call, since the paper positions the exponent against the information-leap exponent that governs SGD on two-layer networks. GD scaling fields are `not-stated`.
- The paper does not state a SQ lower bound for the multi-index case and notes a mismatch between SQ and LDP there (Sec. 3). The LDP bound is for a detection task dominated by index estimation.
- The constant $C(\mathsf P,K)$ in Thm 3 depends on the model and on $r$; the paper says the dependence on $r$ is not fine-grained (Sec. 4.2). Hence grade none.
- Anchors in this HTML version carry no section prefix (for example `Thmtheorem1`).
