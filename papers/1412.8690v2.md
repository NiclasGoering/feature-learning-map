one_line: "Unbounded-width one-hidden-layer nets with an $\\ell_1$-type output norm give a convex problem whose generalisation adapts to low-dimensional structure; the RKHS norm does not."
summary: "The paper studies single-hidden-layer networks with positively homogeneous activations and unboundedly many units, regularised by a total-variation norm on the output weights, which makes the problem convex. It computes approximation errors in this variation space and in the RKHS of the corresponding random-feature kernel. For targets depending on low-dimensional projections of the input, the variation norm does not grow with dimension, whereas the RKHS norm does. This yields generalisation bounds free of the dimension in the exponent, and with $\\ell_1$ input-weight penalties allows variable selection with exponentially many variables. The remaining step, adding one unit, is a non-convex subproblem for which no polynomial-time algorithm with these guarantees is found."

- Read abstract, introduction, Secs. 2 to 5.2 and 5.4 to 7 in the HTML text; the proofs in Appendices C and D (spherical harmonics) were not checked.
- Bach states no learning rule. I used `learning_rule: [gd]` as the nearest bin, since the estimator is the global minimiser of a penalised empirical risk; the paper's own algorithm is Frank-Wolfe on the convex problem, with an unsolved non-convex subproblem (Sec. 6).
- No bounds are given for the exact setting of r1 at $\alpha=0$ apart from the stated variant in Table 2; the exponents and the $\sqrt d$ factor depend on the norm pair $(p,\alpha)$, so the statement is for $p=2$, $\alpha\ge1$.
- The paper also proves a polynomial-time sufficient condition (Sec. 5.5) and then states no strong enough convex relaxation was found; I did not make this a result.
