summary: "For squared loss, the training error obeys $\\dot e=-M(t)e$ with $M=JJ^*$. Expanding in the moving eigenbasis of $M$ gives exact mode equations, and grouping modes into logarithmic spectral shells shows that coupling inside a shell cancels in the quadratic energy. Assuming that the net inter-shell exchange reduces to a boundary flux, the shell energies follow a transport-dissipation equation, with lazy training as zero transport. A scale-free drift gives a self-similar solution with a moving frontier and a power-law loss, and finite-sample spectral mismatch gives double descent. Stochastic Lanczos estimates on Pythia checkpoints show a unimodal shell-energy profile whose peak moves to smaller eigenvalues."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-9 up to the experiments in Sec. 7.4); the cross-model consistency subsection (7.5), the appendices and the Lanczos pseudocode were not read.
- Only the exact identities (Prop 1, Eq. 2) are rigorous. Renormalisability (Def 4.1), the continuum approximation and the scale-free drift $v=c_0\lambda/t$ are assumptions; the paper says the drift is "selected" by log-log straightness of observables and points to a separate derivation (Zhang 2026) under strong conditions. Results r2 to r4 are therefore recorded as `phys`, with `grade: none` because $c_0$ and $p$ are free parameters.
- The paper states that the energy transport conserves total quadratic energy and is not itself a loss-reducing step; the training loss is monotone because only dissipation decreases it.
- For language models the error is defined as the negative log-probability of the true token, with a local quadratic approximation of the KL; the Pythia estimates therefore rest on this approximation.
- The text uses the acronym GRSD for its scale-free solution and refers to an earlier conjecture by the same author (Zhang 2025) about spectral evolution; neither is recorded as a result.
