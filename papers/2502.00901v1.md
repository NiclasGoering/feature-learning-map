summary: "The paper shows that a deep attention network with tied low-rank query and key weights is a sequence multi-index (SMI) model, and that an SMI model is a multi-index model on flattened sequences. For Gaussian inputs with $N/D\\to\\alpha$ and finite sequence length, rank and depth, it gives the Bayes-optimal error and the state evolution of approximate message passing. It also gives the weak-recovery thresholds from the stability of the zero-overlap fixed point. For a two-layer attention target, the last layer is learned first and the first layer only at a larger $\\alpha$. SGD and a trained transformer on TREC show a similar sequential pattern."

- Read the full main text (Secs. 1-4) and theorem statements; the appendix proofs and GAMP derivations were not read.
- Thm 2.1 needs a unique extremiser of the sup-inf problem; the paper also gives a replica derivation (App. D.5). Status is `thm`, with the proof outlined in App. B from Aubin et al. 2018. Thm 2.3 is stated for even SMI models such as the tied attention model.
- All numerical thresholds ($\alpha_1\approx0.14$, $\alpha_2\approx0.79$) are for one example ($L=2$, $M=2$, $c=1$). The $L=3$ result (Fig. 3 left) is a conjecture of a common threshold for the two shallow layers, not a theorem.
- Statistical limits are for Gaussian i.i.d. inputs, finite sequence length and no fully connected layers; the paper lists both as limitations. Value matrices are fixed to identity.
- r5 override: the HTML keeps Fig. 3 as a two-panel figure; the right panel is the TREC experiment.
- Query/key weights are tied and low rank, so `depth: [one-hidden-layer, deep]` covers the single-layer special case and the deep case.
