As $m\\to\\infty$ the particle gradient flow converges to a Wasserstein gradient flow. For positively 2-homogeneous or partially 1-homogeneous models, with an initial measure whose support separates two spheres or two boundary sets, any limit of this flow is a global minimiser. This applies to ReLU and sigmoid networks and to deconvolution, with asymptotic, non-quantitative results. Experiments show that a small amount of over-parametrisation suffices in dimension up to 100."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-5) of the arXiv HTML; the appendices with the proofs (A to D) were not read. Venue from the NeurIPS 2018 footer on page 1. Anchors checked against the HTML; Fig. 3 sits under Sec. 4.3.
- Both convergence theorems assume that the Wasserstein gradient flow converges in $W_2$; the paper says it cannot exclude non-convergence under interesting assumptions (App. C.5). They also need a Sard-type regularity assumption (technical, checked for smooth enough functions) and, for ReLU, a reparametrisation by the signed square, since the ReLU map is not differentiable (Prop 4.3). The results are qualitative and give no rate or width.
- The separation condition on the initial support can only hold in the many-particle limit. The paper compares with the noise-based result of Mei, Montanari and Nguyen, which relies on mixing rather than homogeneity.
- r1 is placed under `gd` / mean-field at fixed data law (population risk or empirical risk). The theory is for gradient flow, the experiments use mini-batch SGD with particles initialised on a small sphere (Fig. 2). The architecture list includes `other` because the same theory covers sparse deconvolution.
- The thread `lazy-vs-rich` is used because the paper contrasts particle training with a convex method on fixed particles; it does not use the words lazy or rich.
