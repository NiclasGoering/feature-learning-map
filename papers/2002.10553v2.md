summary: "The paper shows that training a two-layer ReLU network with squared loss and weight decay can be written as a finite convex program with a block $\\ell_1$ penalty, by semi-infinite duality and the enumeration of hyperplane arrangements of the data. The number of variables is polynomial in the sample size for fixed data rank, and an optimal network is read off from the solution. The weak-regularisation limit is a gauge function of a convex hull, the neural gauge. Linear convolutional networks become semi-definite or nuclear-norm problems, and circular ones become $\\ell_1$-regularised linear models in a Fourier feature space. Small experiments compare SGD with the convex program."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 4 and 6 to 7, and the statement of Thm 1 and Lemmas 2 to 4; the proofs and appendices were not read line by line. In the HTML, Lemmas 2 to 4 and Thm 5, 6 carry the anchors `Thmtheorem2` to `Thmtheorem6`.
- The convex program has complexity exponential in the data rank $r$ (for fixed $n$ and full-rank data, exponential in $d$), and the paper notes this cannot be improved unless P=NP even for $m=2$. This is why the title's "polynomial-time" is for fixed rank $r$.
- Only squared loss is in Thm 1; Thms 5 and 6 (App. A.13) extend the duality to other convex losses.
- No learning rule is in the paper; `learning_rule: [gd]` is the closest bin because the object is the global optimum of the weight-decay training problem, which the paper compares with SGD in Sec. 6. Please re-check.
- `method`, `solver`: duality and convex programming, both `other` in the vocabulary.
