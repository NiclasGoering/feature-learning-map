one_line: "Perturbative 1/N Bayesian inference for deep shaped-nonlinearity networks: kernel regime for N much larger than L and P, then data-dependent kernel corrections controlled by LP/N."
summary: "The paper treats Bayesian inference with deep MLPs whose activation is shaped as $\\phi(t)=t+\\psi t^3/L$, with $P<N_0$ and $N,L,P$ large. A random-graph model of prior moments gives the partition function to first order in $1/N$ at any temperature. For $N\\gg L,P$ inference is a kernel method whose feature map embeds data on a sphere, hyperbola or plane set by $\\psi$. At zero temperature the $LP/N$ correction is a data-dependent kernel reweighting by the sample covariance. For deep linear networks with noisy power-law data, evidence and generalisation are optimal at zero temperature and improve with $LP/N$."

- Read Secs. 1-2, 4.2, 5 (Prop. 5.1 and proof, Prop. 5.2) and 6 in full, including the derivations 6.3.1-6.3.2 and 6.5.1-6.5.2; the combinatorial derivations in Secs. 3-4.1 and App. A were skimmed.
- Two normalisation slips in the paper: the weight variance is $1+\eta/L$ in (2.10) and fn. 3 but $1+2\eta/L$ in Prop. 4.1 and Sec. 6.1 (the kernel prefactor $e^{2\eta}$ in (2.13) needs the latter); the loss (2.11) carries $\frac1{2P}$, but the proof of Prop. 5.1 uses $e^{-\Vert t\Vert^2/2\beta}$, i.e. likelihood noise variance $1/\beta$. Here $\eta$ is a weight-variance shift, not label noise.
- r2 variance: Sec. 6.3.2 gives $-\partial_\tau^2\log Z=\Vert\hat x_\perp\Vert^2\{1+\frac LN[\Vert\hat\theta_*\Vert^2-P+2c_{\psi,\eta}(\ldots)]\}$. For $\psi=0$ this is the first-order expansion of the deep-linear kernel-scale renormalisation. r4 `moves_mean` is `yes` because $m_1$ in Sec. 6.5.2 has an $O(L/N)$ term at finite $\beta$.
- The paper works "at a physics level of rigor", so all results are `phys`. Closure `one-loop` stands for the first-order $1/N$ truncation.
- Data model: inputs after the feature map are iid with covariance $\Sigma$; the power-law spectrum and label-direction ansatz are described as "rough"; evidence statements rely on self-averaging assumptions (Sec. 6.2).
- Perturbation theory is valid only inside the stated window ($\alpha<2$, $k=o(P^{1/\alpha})$); at the boundaries the sign of the correction changes (Fig. 2).
- Mechanism typing is a judgement: the paper calls the $LP/N$ correction "feature learning" and a "data-dependent kernel", which fits T2 here.
