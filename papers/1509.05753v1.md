summary: "The paper studies a perceptron with binary synapses, learning random patterns or a teacher perceptron, with $P=\\alpha N$. Equilibrium analysis says typical solutions are isolated and hard to find, yet simple algorithms succeed. A replica computation of a large-deviation free energy that rewards solutions with many neighbouring solutions (the local entropy) reveals subdominant, extremely dense clusters. Solutions in these clusters generalise better than typical ones and agree with solutions found by simple algorithms. The same picture is checked on MNIST with a binary multilayer network, and the local entropy is turned into an optimisation scheme."

- Read the PRL text from the arXiv PDF. The arXiv HTML page contains only the Supplemental Material, so no HTML anchors exist; the Supplement (replica details, 13 order parameters, multilayer network) was not read in full.
- Classification vs generalisation: the data are random labels (classification case, $\alpha_c=0.833$) or a teacher perceptron ($\alpha_{TS}=1.245$). The target is therefore `[teacher-network, other]` at paper level, with a teacher-only override in r2.
- `learning_rule: [bayes]` is a judgement call: the theory is a zero-temperature Gibbs measure over solutions (equilibrium, reweighted by local entropy). The algorithms studied (SBPI, reinforced BP, CP+R, EdMC) are heuristic, not gradient-based, so `gd` was not used.
- Claims about multilevel synapses, sparse patterns and the unconstrained free energy $F_U$ are stated without derivation (deferred to follow-up work), so none is recorded as a result. The MNIST result (1.25% error) is numerical and was left out.
- The text also discusses a first-order transition at $\alpha_{TS}$ and a change of structure at $\alpha_U\simeq0.77$ (RS breaks down); neither is recorded as a result.
