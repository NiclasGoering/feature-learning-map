one_line: "Field theory of the ResNet prior: NNGP kernel as saddle, a first-order-in-$1/N$ response function, and an optimal residual scale $\\rho^*\\propto1/\\sqrt L$."
summary: "The paper writes the prior of a residual network with Gaussian weights as a field theory in layer-wise kernel variables and their conjugates, with an action that scales with the width $N$. Its saddle point is the NNGP kernel, which accumulates across layers because of the skip connections. Expanding to Gaussian order gives the propagator and hence a response function of the output kernel to the input kernel, the first-order finite-size term. This response is maximal at a residual-branch scale $\\rho^*$ that a saturation argument places at $\\propto1/\\sqrt L$ for large depth, with weak dependence on other hyperparameters. The theory holds at initialisation; Langevin-trained networks are studied numerically."

- RG record: (a) Sense (b): effective theory of a finite-width network at initialisation, as a recursion in depth; the paper never uses renormalisation language and does no coarse-graining. (b) Integrated out: the Gaussian weights and biases (Eqs. (9)-(10)), then the pre-activations $h^{(l)}$, leaving the kernel variables $C^{(l)}$, $\tilde C^{(l)}$ with action $\mathcal S$ (Eqs. (21)-(22)). (c) What flows with depth: the residual and network kernels, $C_*^{(l)}$ and $K^{(l)}=K^{(l-1)}+C_*^{(l)}$ (Eqs. (29)-(30), exact at $N\to\infty$, no fixed point, kernels only grow), and their linearisation, the response recursion (Eqs. (47), (49), (51), (53)); the single tuned parameter is the residual scale $\rho$ (optimum Eqs. (65)-(66)); order: saddle plus Gaussian fluctuations (propagator $\mathcal G=-(\mathcal S^{(2)})^{-1}$, Eqs. (44)-(46); kernel variance Eqs. (128)-(129)), i.e. first order in $1/N$. (d) It does not go beyond first order (Sec. IV, Limitations: higher orders are noted but not computed), and its maturity is a physics-level derivation plus numerics at $N=500$, with the analytic optimum a heuristic saturation estimate and the trained-network check only numerical.
- Vocab gap: `learning_rule`: wanted "prior / initialisation only" (no posterior, no training dynamics in the theory); used `bayes` because the paper frames the object as the Bayesian network prior and Sec. III.4 treats the Langevin stationary law as the posterior.
- Vocab gap: `method.method`: wanted "field theory (action, propagator, Gaussian fluctuations)"; used `saddle-point-ld` and `perturbative-1/N`. `quantity`: wanted "response function / signal propagation"; used `kernel-representation` and `optimal-hyperparameter`. `sampler`: wanted "direct draws from the prior"; used `other`. `closure`: wanted "tree-level propagator (Gaussian fluctuations)"; used `one-loop`.
- Vocab gap: `parametrisation`: wanted "residual-branch scale $\rho$ as a hyperparameter with standard $1/N$ widths"; used `sp` (the $1/\sqrt L$ choice is not named depth-$\mu$P in the paper). `target`, `label_noise`, `amplitude`, `temperature`: the theory has no target or likelihood, so `arbitrary` or `not-stated` are used.
- Read the full v3 HTML (main text, Apps. A-F); the multi-input derivations in App. A (Eqs. (72)-(85)) were skimmed. Equation numbers are as printed. Inconsistencies in the paper: Eq. (49) writes $\phi'^{(k-1)}$ inside an average over $K^{(l-1)}$ and Eq. (59) sums $\eta^{(l)}$ where $\eta^{(k)}$ is meant; the formula after Eq. (130) sums only the diagonal $\mathcal G_{11}^{(kk)}$ although Eq. (38) has off-diagonal layer entries.
- Scaling of the "$O(1/N)$" claim: $\Delta=N\mathcal G_{12}$ is $O(1)$ and, by Eqs. (55)-(59), equals the infinite-width derivative $\partial K/\partial K^{(0)}$; the $1/N$ is carried by the connected correlator $\langle C\tilde C\rangle$ and by $\mathcal G_{11}$, which the paper never evaluates. No finite-width correction to the kernel or predictor is computed or tested. Eq. (65) uses only the infinite-width kernel $K^{(L)}$ with linearised $\phi$ and the assumption $\mathcal V/2=\sqrt{K^{(L)}}$, "only an estimate".
- Judgement calls: `D: to-infinity` and `joint` follow the paper's $\nu_0=d_{\rm in}/N$ fixed ratio in the action, although the numerics use $d_{\rm in}=100$ or $784$ with $N=500$. r2 adds `inf-depth` for the large-$L$ law of Eq. (66); numerics go to $L=200$. r4 has `mixing: high` because its evidence rests on Langevin training until convergence without diagnostics; the noise level is not stated, so the dial's $T\le10^{-2}$ criterion cannot be checked. r4's type T2 is the closest type with label `not-analysed`; the paper offers no mechanism. No relation to 2405.10761 is added: v3 cites it only as outlook (Sec. IV, Outlook).
