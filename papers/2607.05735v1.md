summary: "For fully connected Bayesian networks at the critical mean-field scaling, the paper studies the reduced entropy, the intensive prior cost of fitting a target to a given mean-squared error. It proves that low reduced entropy implies a polynomial-width network that agrees with the infinite-width learned function at every input, by keeping an active low-dimensional part and resampling the lazy bulk from the prior. A cloning argument gives the converse. Together these show that infinite-width and polynomial-width Gibbs learners learn exactly the same Boolean-cube targets from polynomial samples."

- Read the abstract, introduction, setup, Secs. 3-7 statements and parts of the proofs in the HTML v1; the appendices (regularity package, lazy-swap bounds, cloning) were skimmed only.
- The paper treats only the critical scaling $\gamma=1$ (hidden μP, readout $1/N$); the over-rich $\gamma>1$ and finite-width regimes are deferred to a companion paper. All polynomial exponents depend on depth, so `depth` lists both categories; the paper uses $L$ weight matrices and $L-1$ hidden layers with $L\ge2$.
- New mechanism `active-lazy-spectral-split` is added for the active/lazy decomposition (Sec. 1 "The engine: subsampling", Lemma 5.1, Fig. 2). It is not covered by `adaptive-kernel`, which says the whole kernel adapts.
- Learner is the smooth empirical Gibbs posterior at intensive inverse temperature $\bar\beta=\beta/N$, hence `likelihood_scaled: yes`. The activation is bounded, Lipschitz and non-polynomial; `data_class: boolean-parity` is the closest vocabulary entry for Boolean-cube targets, the theorems are not specific to parities.
