summary: "The paper treats the depth of a feed-forward ReLU network trained by gradient descent as a renormalisation-group coordinate. Its object is a lifted tensor-product metric built from each layer's forward pullback and backward push-forward Gram metrics, which is isotropic in the neutral phase after trace normalisation. A first-order discrete Dyson expansion and its continuous Callan–Symanzik flow give tensorial and spectral beta functions driven only by anisotropic insertions. Gradient steps are argued to create these insertions as synchronous symmetric traceless metric strains, and a Wilsonian layer-to-layer recursion sorts them into relevant and irrelevant ones. Heavy-tailed spectra follow from a scale-free counting hypothesis; there are no numerics, and the full temporal mean-field theory is left to future work."

- RG record: Sense (b), RG over depth; it is not sense (e), because there is no Bayesian posterior, no $P\propto N$ regime, no saddle point and no 1/N expansion, and the paper never mentions kernel renormalisation (2012.04030, 2209.04882 and 2605.29684 are not cited). What is coarse-grained: blocks of layers of the layerwise forward-pullback and backward-push-forward Gram metrics (lifted channel $\mathcal C(r)=\Sigma(r)\otimes T(r)$), with the isotropic trace divided out so that only the target-active symmetric traceless strain is kept. What flows, in depth $r$ with $\beta=r\,\partial_r$: the trace-normalised channel by Eq. (7) and Eq. (8), the isolated-mode spectral beta functions Eq. (9) and Eq. (39), and the strain by the recursion Eq. (31), Eq. (32) and the Callan–Symanzik-type Eq. (33). The order is first order throughout (first-order Dyson Eq. (5), strains to $O(\eta)$ in Eqs. (23)–(24), first-order eigenvalue perturbation for isolated modes) and the paper does not go beyond it; maturity is an analytic scaling argument under stated assumptions, with no theorem, figure or numerics, power laws resting on a hypothesis (Eq. (12)), and the full temporal DMFT left to future work.

II, "Scope and assumptions"), scalar readout with a sample-independent covector $b_L$ (whether $b_L$ is trained is not stated; Eq. (16) updates every $W^{(r)}$, hence `learned_layers: [all]`). V.5) uses mini-batch noise with a Gaussian log-multiplier. Fixed-point existence is only constructed under a rank-one ansatz in App. A.4, where the condition reduces to one scalar equation (Eq. (60) in the HTML, (A20) in the PDF).
- Vocab gap: `width_limit` has no value for "depth treated as a continuum, width not specified"; I used `inf-depth` and set N, P, D to `not-stated`. `method` has no value for a Wilsonian / Callan–Symanzik depth RG with Dyson expansion (used `other`); `approximations` has no value for fixed-ReLU-chamber, isolated-mode and scale-free-counting assumptions (used `linearisation` plus `other`). `mechanism_label` has no label for target-aligned symmetric traceless metric strain accumulating in layerwise Gram metrics; I used the broader `kernel-evolves-in-training` (T6) for every result rather than add a special case. `status`: r5 is `conjecture` because the paper calls the power law a "scale-free counting hypothesis", while r1–r4 are `phys` (derived under stated approximations, no proof).
- Checks and omissions: the title speaks of a phase transition, but the paper only identifies an instability of the target-isotropic fixed point and fixed-point conditions; no order parameter or transition point is computed. Eq. (16) carries a factor $2\eta/(MN)$ while the same update in Eq. (49) (HTML numbering) carries $2\eta/M$; App. A.2 refers to "Section VI.A" for the isolated-mode assumption, which is stated in Sec. V.1. The Kesten compatibility argument for $\alpha=2$ (Eqs. (18)–(21)) and the weight-Gram imprint (Sec. V.4) have no result slot (limit of five); the paper calls the former "a minimal phenomenological compatibility argument", not a derivation of a universal exponent. The arXiv comment calls the paper a working paper under technical review.
