one_line: "Field theory of NNGP regression with a stochastic input kernel: data variability gives a non-Gaussian action whose first-order correction pulls the predictive mean towards zero."
summary: "In the infinite-width limit the network output is a Gaussian process whose kernel depends on the input overlap matrix. Treating the overlap entries as correlated Gaussian random variables, the paper computes the disorder-averaged predictive mean diagrammatically, without replicas, in the form of a homogeneous-kernel result plus first-order corrections in the data variance. The corrections always reduce the magnitude of the mean, and a closed-form limit for infinite training data shows that the homogeneous-kernel prediction is over-confident. Deep nonlinear networks have an optimal prior weight variance slightly in the supercritical regime, and the first-order theory is tested on synthetic binary patterns and on MNIST."

- Read the PDF of v2 (9 Nov 2023) in full: main text, Appendices 1-6, Supplement section headings. The arXiv HTML exists; its equation numbers run three ahead of the PDF after Eq. (37): PDF (39) is HTML (42), PDF (56) is HTML (59). `source.loc` uses PDF numbers, anchors the HTML ids.
- Sections: III B 1 linear, III B 2 one-hidden-layer erf, III B 3 multilayer (Fig. 4), III B 4 non-symmetric tasks and MNIST (Figs. 5-6). Eq. (39) is the second-order delta-method expansion of the average of $K_{*\alpha}K^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}y_\beta$ over Gaussian kernel entries; the sign towards zero is shown in closed form only for the symmetric task (Eqs. 52, 56).
- The statement "the first-order correction always pushes the mean towards zero" is from the discussion. The paper treats variability as small and says a self-consistent (saddle-point) treatment would be needed for large variability.
- Empirical comparisons are exact GP regression on resampled training sets; no posterior sampling. The paper mentions possible extensions to finite-width kernel corrections (feature learning), but does not do them.
- Published as Phys. Rev. Research 8 (2026), doi 10.1103/r1s3-lr3g, under the shorter title "Data variability in neural network Bayesian inference"; the entry follows arXiv v2. Grade A: Eq. (39) needs only the mean and covariance of the kernel entries, no self-consistent equations.
