summary: "The paper uses the $D$-output Bayesian effective action of one-hidden-layer networks at $P/N_1\\to\\alpha_1$, taken from the supplement of the single-output effective-action paper. The infinite-width NNGP kernel is renormalised by a $D\\times D$ order parameter matrix $Q$, which changes the kernel's shape for $D>1$ and reduces to a scalar for $D=1$. $Q/\\lambda_1$ equals the average overlap between readout weight vectors of different outputs, so output-output correlations vanish only at infinite width. Langevin sampling on synthetic data, MNIST and CIFAR-10 agrees with the predicted generalisation loss and with the predicted correlations, for example between vehicle classes. Adam-trained ReLU networks do not beat the best infinite-width kernel."

- Read v1 including the supplemental material (SM I-V: derivation, readout overlaps, Langevin details, perturbative solution, Adam pipeline). No journal reference in the arXiv metadata, so `venue` is `preprint`. The matrix action, Eq. (1), is quoted from 2209.04882 (its SM multi-output action, PDF Eq. 76, which normalises the loss by the number of outputs).
- Derivation again uses Gaussian equivalence justified informally by generalised central limit theorems; hence `phys`. The paper says finite-width effects at small $N_1$ are not controlled.
- `moves_mean`: the paper does not discuss it, but SM Eq. (17) settles it: at $\beta\to\infty$, $(Q\otimes K)^{-1}(Q\otimes k)=I\otimes K^{-1}k$, so $\Gamma_a=k^\top K^{-1}y_a$ does not depend on $Q$; at $T=0.01$ $Q$ enters only through the output-coupled ridge $TQ^{-1}$. Hence `yes` for results at $T>0$.
- `mixing: high`: Euler unadjusted Langevin at $T=0.01$, $\epsilon=10^{-3}$, about $5\cdot10^7$ steps, with a visual thermalisation cut and blocking plus Gamma-method error bars (SM III); `diagnostics: other`.
- The student activation of the Bayesian runs is given only in the synthetic-data paragraph ("For all simulations ... Erf", SM III A); `erf` is kept for r2 and r3. The paper's $D$ is the number of outputs, not the input dimension ($N_0$).
