summary: "The paper extends the Bayesian effective action of one-hidden-layer networks at $P/N_1\\to\\alpha_1$ to $D$ outputs. The infinite-width NNGP kernel is renormalised by a $D\\times D$ order parameter matrix $Q$, which changes the kernel's shape for $D>1$ and reduces to a scalar for $D=1$. $Q/\\lambda_1$ equals the average overlap between readout weight vectors of different outputs, so output-output correlations vanish only at infinite width. Langevin sampling on synthetic data, MNIST and CIFAR-10 agrees with the predicted generalisation loss and with the predicted correlations, for example between vehicle classes. Adam-trained ReLU networks do not beat the best infinite-width kernel."

- Read the full main text of HTML v1; the supplemental material (derivations, perturbative solution, Adam pipeline, low-dimensional data) was not read, so the details of r4 and the Adam protocol rest on the main text. No journal reference in the arXiv metadata, so `venue` is `preprint`.
- Derivation again uses Gaussian equivalence justified informally by generalised central limit theorems; hence `phys`. The paper says finite-width effects at small $N_1$ are not controlled.
- `moves_mean` is `unknown` throughout: the paper does not discuss whether the matrix $Q$ changes the mean predictor. At zero temperature a Kronecker form $Q\otimes K$ would leave the mean unchanged, but the experiments are at $T=0.01$, and this was not checked.
- `mixing: high` is by analogy with the same group's sampling protocol (Langevin at $T=0.01$); the sampling details of this paper are in the supplement.
