summary: "The paper studies a two-layer ReLU network learning a degree-$p$ polynomial of $r$ relevant directions on Gaussian inputs. The algorithm takes one large gradient step on the first layer with weight decay, resets the biases and then trains the second layer by ridge regression. The first step makes the first-layer weights span the principal subspace, which gives risk guarantees from $n\\asymp d^2r+dr^p$ samples and width $r^p$, below the $d^p$ needed in the kernel regime. Retraining only the head on a new task with the same subspace needs $r^p$ samples, independent of $d$. A correlational-query lower bound shows that dropping the non-degeneracy assumption costs $d^{p/2}$."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 6 and the proof sketches; the appendix proofs were not read. The arXiv record has no journal reference, hence `preprint`.
- Algorithm 1 is not plain gradient descent: the targets are first detrended (linear part removed), the first-layer step has size $\eta_1=\tilde O(\sqrt d)$ with weight decay $\lambda_1=\eta_1^{-1}$ so that $W^{(1)}=-\eta_1\nabla_W\mathcal L$, biases are then redrawn, and only the head is trained after that. `learned_layers: [all]` records that both layers are trained, in two stages.
- Sec. 3 says that resampling the data after the first step would lower the sample complexity to $d^2r+r^p$ and that this follows from Thm 3, but the Thm 3 printed in Sec. 3 is the transfer statement; the resampling variant is not stated as a theorem in the text I read.
- The mechanism label `relevant-subspace-alignment` is new. It differs from `rank-one-spike` in that the step is not rank one for $r>1$; the paper itself explains it through the leading term $Hw$ of the gradient in Sec. 5.1.
- `op-06` is listed because the paper gives a gradient-descent sample complexity for targets with low-dimensional structure; the paper has no Bayesian analogue.
