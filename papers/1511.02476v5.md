summary: "The review casts inference as a planted statistical ensemble in the teacher–student setting. Bayes-optimal inference sits on the Nishimori line, where there is no static glass phase and belief propagation (BP) is asymptotically exact. Information-theoretic thresholds appear as phase transitions; when the transition is first order, a hard phase opens in which BP and Monte Carlo stay in a metastable state. Worked examples are the planted spin glass, the stochastic block model and generalised linear estimation (compressed sensing, binary perceptron) with approximate message passing, state evolution and spatial coupling. The perceptron is treated only as a special case of linear estimation."

This is a review of inference in general, not of neural networks; the perceptron appears only as a special case of generalised linear estimation (Sec. VI D). Most table fields therefore describe linear estimation or are `not-stated`, and results r1–r4 override the data and architecture fields because they concern graphs and spin systems.
- Results are the five most relevant statements for the map; many other results (Bethe free energy, TAP equations, non-backtracking spectral method, matrix factorisation) are not covered.
- Regime tag R6 is used as the Bayes-optimal teacher–student tag. The depth, width and activation fields are chosen to fit the schema and have no deep meaning here.
- Mechanism labels: the mechanism types T0–T6 describe feature learning, which the review does not address. The first-order and hard-phase results use T5 `mixed-phase` as the closest existing label (coexisting fixed points); the Nishimori and second-order results are `not-analysed`. Please check whether T5 is a fair match.
- Open problem op-03 is touched through the absence of a static glass phase on the Nishimori line and the planted-versus-random role of initialisation, not answered. The statement in r4 about the conjectured hardness is the authors' own conjecture, qualified by the XOR-SAT counterexample.
