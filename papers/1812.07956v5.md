one_line: "Lazy training is a scaling effect: at large output scale $\\alpha$, gradient flow of any differentiable model stays near its linearisation; lazy CNNs underperform."
summary: "The paper studies a differentiable model $h(w)$ trained on a loss of the rescaled output $\\alpha h(w)$. It proves that for large $\\alpha$ and $h(w_0)=0$ the parameters barely move and the gradient flow follows that of the linearised model, with finite-horizon bounds, a square-loss criterion, and uniform-in-time linear convergence for strongly convex losses. In the over-parameterised case this reaches a global minimiser; in the under-parameterised case only a local one. Two-layer teacher-student runs and CIFAR-10 CNNs with large output scale show that lazy models generalise worse and that practical CNNs are far from this regime."

- Read the full main text (Secs. 1-3) of the arXiv HTML; the appendix proofs (Lemmas B.1, B.2) were not read. Venue from the NeurIPS 2019 footer on page 1 and the arXiv journal reference.
- Placement: lazy training is a scale effect ($\alpha\to\infty$) for any model, so the paper sits at `inf-kernel` with $N$ fixed. For two-layer networks the paper relates the scale to width via $\alpha(m)$ and discusses $\alpha=1/\sqrt m$ (lazy) against $\alpha=1/m$ (mean-field), hence r4 lists both groups. The deep CNN runs in r5 are at fixed width and large scale.
- The theory needs the "centred" model ($h(w_0)=0$, obtained by subtracting the initial output or by symmetrised initialisation) for all uniform results; Thm 2.4 allows small $\|h(w_0)\|$ instead. The statements are for gradient flow with exact gradients; the paper cites known approximation results for GD and SGD.
- r3 uses `no-advantage-over-kernel` because the paper's point is that the lazy flow can stop at a local minimum that is worse than the global one; the claim that the result implies lazy training gives no feature gain is the paper's reading, not a theorem.
- The paper notes that the lazy regime says nothing about whether the population loss goes to its minimum as $p\to\infty$; this needs the tangent kernel to converge to a universal kernel, and the convergence may be slow in high dimension (Fig. 1(c)).
- In r5, `parametrisation: sp` is an assumption from the use of standard Xavier initialisation in a VGG-11 model; the paper does not name a parametrisation.
