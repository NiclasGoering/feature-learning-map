one_line: "A random interpolating network from a flat weight prior generalises with sample complexity near the teacher's parameter count, since weight redundancy favours narrow teachers."
summary: "The paper analyses posterior sampling with a 0-1 likelihood, also called guess and check, from a flat prior over network weights. It shows that the sample complexity is $-\\log\\tilde p$, where $\\tilde p$ is the prior probability of drawing a function equal to the teacher. For quantized fully connected and convolutional networks, counting weight settings that reproduce a narrow teacher bounds this by roughly the teacher's parameter count times the bits per weight, with only weak dependence on student width when neurons are scaled. A margin assumption gives a similar bound for continuous two-layer leaky-ReLU networks. The volume of bad interpolators is exponentially small in the sample size."

- Regime: there is no regime slug that fits exactly. R6 with `finite-exact` (non-asymptotic bounds), plus `high-dim` in r3/r4 where $d_0\gg d_1^\star$, is a judgement call: sample size scales with the teacher's parameter count rather than the student's.
- r4 is a short empirical remark in the Discussion; depth results are stated qualitatively and no figure is cited, so the loc is a section pointer. The dimension and number of runs were not read.
- Lemma 3.2 is described by the authors as a special case of a PAC-Bayes bound, with a self-contained proof that gives rate $1/\epsilon$ for single posterior draws.
- Op-10 is listed because the paper relates posterior sampling to SGD (Sec. 7), as the open problem does.
