one_line: "Field-theory learning curves for Gaussian-process (NNGP/NTK) regression: equivalence kernel plus a first $1/\\eta$ correction, with kernel truncation into effective noise as the 'renormalisation'."
summary: "The paper casts Gaussian-process regression, which describes wide networks trained with the NTK or with noisy gradient descent and weight decay, as a field theory over functions. Averaging over datasets with replicas and a Poisson-averaged dataset size $\\eta$ gives the equivalence-kernel learning curve at leading order and a first correction in $1/\\eta$ from Feynman diagrams, in closed form for data uniform on a hypersphere. For noiseless NTK regression the expansion is not controlled, so the paper trims the kernel's Taylor series and moves the removed high-degree modes into an effective label noise, which it calls a renormalisation. Learning curves for depth-4 ReLU NTK regression match the prediction, which also limits learnable polynomial degree and is used to choose initialisation scales. Finite-width corrections are left as a future extension."

- RG record: (a) Sense (a), Wilsonian RG over kernel eigenmodes in a one-step form: Sec. VI (described as in the spirit of RG) trims the dot-product kernel $K=\sum_q b_q(x\cdot x')^q$ at order $r$ into a "renormalized NTK" $K^{(r)}$, while the $1/\eta$ expansion of Secs. IV–V is separate Feynman-diagram perturbation theory at infinite width with no renormalisation. (b) Integrated out: harmonics of degree $l>r$ (eigenvalues $\lambda_l=O(d^{-l})$, Eq. (25)), i.e. Taylor terms $q>r$, whose diagonal part $\sigma_r^2=\sum_{q>r}b_q$ is added to the label noise while the eigenvalues $\lambda_l$, $l\le r$, change (Sec. VI, unnumbered). (c) Nothing flows: there is no flow equation, beta function or fixed point; $r$ is a discrete cutoff chosen by a trade-off ($r=3$, Sec. VII), the replacement is a scaling estimate $O(\sqrt N d^{-(r+1)/2}/K_{x,x})$ (Sec. VI, App. K, using Eq. (66)), and its purpose is to make the $1/\eta$ expansion controlled at $\sigma^2=0$ (end of Sec. V). (d) Beyond first order: no, the only perturbative output is the leading EK term (Eq. (20)) plus one correction $g^*_{SL,\eta}$ (Eqs. (22)–(24), App. I, Eq. (57)), and no $1/N$ finite-width term or RG at finite width is computed (Sec. IX lists both as future work); maturity is a physics-level derivation plus numerics (Fig. 2, Table 1), with equation numbers as in the arXiv HTML (the PDF numbers are 2 lower in the main text).

- Placement: `learning_rule: [bayes, gd]` because the same GP-regression learning curve serves the NNSP correspondence (noisy GD with weight decay, equilibrium posterior) and the NTK correspondence (vanishing learning rate, noiseless); r4 and r5 are NTK-regime and so GD-only. Tag R0 only: width is infinite throughout and the paper's "perturbative" expansion is in $1/\eta$, the Poisson-averaged dataset size, not in $1/N$ of the width, so R2 does not apply. The main-text curves (Fig. 2) are exact GP regression with the NTK, no network trained (Sec. VII); networks are trained only in Sec. VIII (full-batch GD, learning rate 1.0, footnote 1 mentions width $2^{14}$). The NNSP case (App. C, one-hidden-layer NNGP kernel with finite $\sigma^2$) is not recorded as a separate result.
- Inconsistencies: App. K points to the right panel of Fig. 1 for numerical support of the trimming, but Fig. 1 is the membrane picture (the check is in App. B); Sec. VI says the trimmed kernel has zero eigenvalues for $l>r$ while App. K says $l\ge r$; Sec. III.1 refers to "App. II". In Table 1 the optimised network's test loss (0.078) is below both its prediction (0.110) and its GP average (0.141), which the paper calls fair agreement. The trimming accuracy is a scaling argument backed by numerics, not a proof, so r3 is `phys`.
- The paper cites neither 2002.02561 nor 2106.04110, so `relations` is empty. Like 2002.02561 it gives fixed-target kernel-regression learning curves by eigenmode (modes learned once $\eta\sim\sigma^2/\lambda_l$, $\lambda_l\sim d^{-l}$ on the sphere), but through a $1/\eta$ expansion about the EK result with a trimmed kernel for the ridgeless case, not a self-consistent effective ridge. The finite-width quartic corrections that 2106.04110 treats are what Sec. IX leaves as an extension. None of the three has a flowing coupling.
