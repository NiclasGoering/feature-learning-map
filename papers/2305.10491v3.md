summary: "The note defines Bayesian renormalisation by running dynamical Bayesian inference backwards, discarding data, with $\\tau=1/T$ as the RG time. The late-time Gaussian posterior has covariance $\\tau\\mathcal I^{-1}$, so the Fisher metric fixes which models are distinguishable and acts as an emergent RG scale in place of a momentum cutoff. Pushing the posterior forward through the model map $G$ gives a heat kernel and a Fokker–Planck flow of exact-RG form (Eqs. (43)–(47)), presented as a refinement of diffusion learning. An explicit hard-cutoff scheme, Eq. (55), removes parameters whose Fisher diagonal lies below $\\Lambda$ and is tried on one MNIST autoencoder. The derivation is formal, with no theorem, and nothing is said about network kernels or feature learning."

- RG record: (a) Sense (d), learning as inverse RG on data and information theory: coarse-graining is the discarding of observed data, indexed by $\tau=1/T$, with the Fisher metric as emergent RG scale; the explicit scheme of Eq. (55) is a Wilson-shell-style cutoff on Fisher-sorted weights, so it touches sense (a) but acts on weights, not on data or kernel eigenmodes. (b) Formally, models beyond Fisher distance $\sim\sqrt{\tau}$ of the MAP are averaged into the predictive, Eqs. (38)–(39); in the implementation, encoder weights with Fisher diagonal $\mathcal I_{ii}\le\Lambda$ are removed, Eq. (55). (c) What flows is the posterior predictive $p_\tau$: the Gaussian posterior (38) gives the predictive (39), then the heat kernel (43) with $K_\tau^{-1}$ from (44), written as Fokker–Planck (45) and SDE (46) and mapped to exact RG in (47), at leading order in $\tau$ (late-time Gaussian posterior, Eq. (35)); no flow of network couplings is derived. (d) Nothing beyond leading order, no theorem and no beta function; maturity is formal derivation plus analogy plus one single-run autoencoder experiment (Figs. 2, 3, 5), whose scheme (55) is not derived from (45)–(46).
- What is derived, by equation: Sec. 2 reviews known results (Polchinski and Wegner–Morris flows as Fokker–Planck diffusion, Eqs. (3)–(14); heat kernel and conditional-expectation semigroup, Eqs. (20)–(24); refs. [10], [12], [18], [41]). New here are Eqs. (38)–(39) (backward inference), (43)–(46) (pushforward by $G$, Fokker–Planck and SDE) and the identification (47). Eqs. (33)–(36) come from ref. [4] and (35) is asserted "at sufficiently late $T$"; footnote 10 extends (38) to all $\tau$ because the prior is "irrelevant". Eq. (45) is "read" from the form (43), and $V_\tau$ is not constructed. Eq. (44) pushes the inverse Fisher metric forward with the Jacobian of $G$, so the Gaussian form (43) needs $G$ to be nearly linear on the posterior width; the paper does not say so. There is no theorem, proposition or lemma. The claim that the scheme "coincides with ordinary renormalization" for physical systems (abstract, Sec. 3 intro) is supported by citations ([35], [36], [41], [77]), not shown here.
- Examples done: only one, the MNIST autoencoder of Sec. 4 (added in v3): input 784, latent 20, 15680 encoder weights, Adam (learning rate 0.001), MSE loss (53), Fisher of the encoder from NNGeometry [75] after training, decoder untouched, $\Lambda$ from 0 to 2.375 in steps of 0.125 (Table 2). No toy model for dynamical Bayes and no physical field theory is worked out. Not stated: training epochs or batch size, whether Fig. 2 is train or test loss, whether removed weights are set to zero, empirical or true Fisher, seeds or error bars. In Fig. 2 magnitude pruning is as flat as Fisher pruning up to about 10000 removed weights and has lower loss beyond about 12000, so the figure shows a gain over random pruning but not over magnitude pruning; the text says the Fisher loss exceeds the magnitude loss "shortly" after $\Lambda\sim0.1$. Fig. 5 shows about 10000 of 15680 diagonal entries in the lowest bin (below about 0.1).
- Neural networks and kernels: the paper makes no statement about width limits, NNGP or NTK kernels, feature learning or kernel adaptation; the word kernel appears only for ERG, heat and diffusion kernels. Networks enter only through the dictionary of Table 1 and the autoencoder of Sec. 4, so every mechanism is `not-analysed`. Entries r1 to r3 are written at the level of a general parametric family (no network); r4 is the only network result, overridden to GD because the autoencoder is trained with Adam and the Fisher matrix is only a post-hoc diagnostic.
