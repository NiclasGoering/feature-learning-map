summary: "The paper derives, by the replica method, the typical generalisation error of kernel ridge regression for any kernel, data distribution and noisy target, as a function of the kernel spectrum and the target's weights on it. It shows that modes with larger eigenvalues are learned faster, introduces a cumulative power measure of how well the target aligns with the kernel, and finds that noise or inexpressible target parts make the learning curve non-monotonic. For rotation-invariant kernels on high-dimensional spheres it gives one band-limited formula per learning stage. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10 and NTK-trained networks agree."

- Read the HTML of v6 through Sec. "Dot Product Kernels, NTK and Wide Neural Networks" and the Discussion's opening; Methods and Supplementary Notes (replica derivation, bias-variance details, Notes 1-6) not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for 2002.02561: the theory is for kernel ridge regression, and connects to networks through NTK/NNGP equivalence with gradient descent in the infinite-width limit.
- The thermodynamic limit is only "$P\to\infty$" with finite-$P$ accuracy tested numerically; the paper says the formula describes average learning curves well even for a few samples. Eq. (4) is a replica-symmetric saddle-point result, hence `phys`.
- Grade `B` for r1: it needs the kernel eigenvalues and target weights, which for real data come from the empirical (uncentered) kernel PCA of the dataset.
- Zero-eigenvalue target modes, which act as noise (Supplementary Notes 3-4), and the double-descent boundary Eq. (8) are not recorded as separate results.
