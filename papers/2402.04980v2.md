summary: "The paper models a two-layer network trained by one large full-batch gradient step on the first layer, followed by ridge regression on the readout, as a spiked random-features (sRF) model: a Gaussian bulk plus a rank-one spike correlated with the single-index target. It shows by replica calculation that the sRF is equivalent to a conditional Gaussian model, non-linear in the projection of the input on the spike, and derives self-consistent equations for the test error with $n$, $p$, $d$ proportional. Simulations at $d=p=2000$ agree. The error beats random features and the best linear predictor, and upper and lower bounds are given in terms of a two-function basis."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 4 and the conclusion; the appendices with the derivations (A: spike statistics, C: replica computation, D: non-uniform readout, E: bounds) were not read in detail. The paper says the replica derivation is non-rigorous, hence `phys`.
- Training protocol: layerwise, one full-batch step with $n_0=\alpha_0 d$ samples and $\eta=\tilde\eta d$, then ridge regression of the readout on fresh data. Activation $\sigma$ is odd; readout starts uniform at $\mathbf 1_p/\sqrt p$.
- The sRF is assumed to describe the trained network; the evidence is numerical (Figs. 1, 2, 4). The mapping of the network to $(c,r,\gamma)$ in Result 3.1 is also a result stated without proof in the main text.
- The related-work section says the intermediate step size $\eta=\Theta(d^s)$, $1/2<s<1$, has a finite-spike approximation, while here the full scaling is used; I kept `gd_step_size` as large-single-step only.
- Result 3.3's equations take the joint law $\nu$ of the singular values of $W$ and its overlaps as input (Assumption 3.2), so grade A rather than B: no empirical Gram matrix is needed.
