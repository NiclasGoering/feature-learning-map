one_line: "In L2-regularised deep linear networks, lowering weight decay adds data singular directions one by one, through second-order (one hidden layer) or first-order transitions."
summary: "The paper treats deep linear networks with squared loss and L2 weight decay as a toy model for learned features, with the weight-decay strength as an external field. Under an alignment condition on the data covariances, the loss reduces to independent Landau-type potentials in the singular values of the end-to-end matrix. One hidden layer gives a cascade of second-order transitions at $\\beta_j=\\eta_j$; two or more hidden layers give first-order transitions with explicit critical strengths. The complete Hessian spectrum at each rank-restricted minimum and the critical slowing down of gradient flow are derived. Numerics on non-aligned data and on tanh and MNIST networks show a similar pattern."

- Read the main text (Secs. I-V) and Tables 1-3; the appendices (Hessian derivations, exact dynamical solution, numerical details) were not read in detail.
- The paper has no numbered theorems; the results are analytic derivations under the alignment condition (Eq. 17: $\Sigma_{xx}$ and $\Sigma_{yx}$ share singular vectors) and $N_{data}\to\infty$. They are recorded as `phys` rather than `thm`; a checker may decide differently.
- Statements about the first-order case rely on numerics (annealing and reversed annealing) for hysteresis; the exact $\beta^{(det)}$ and $\beta^*$ formulas are for aligned data only and are valid for $n\ge3$ layers (two or more hidden layers).
- Dependence on noise is deliberately excluded (zero temperature gradient flow); the paper says it is open whether the transitions survive as thermodynamic phase transitions in the large-model limit.
