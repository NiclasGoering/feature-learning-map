summary: "The paper views the $n$ units of a wide network as interacting particles and studies their empirical distribution under gradient descent. A law of large numbers gives a deterministic function-space flow driven by a positive semidefinite kernel built from the current particle law, which converges to the target if the particle law converges. A central limit theorem shows fluctuations of order $n^{-1/2}$ that heal at times beyond $\\log n$, so the approximation error is $O(1/n)$. For SGD it gives noise scalings and rules for step and batch size. Experiments fit a 3-spin function on the sphere in dimensions up to 25."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-5) of the arXiv HTML; the appendix on the long-time behaviour with noise and the proofs were not read. The venue is not stated on the first page of v3, so `preprint`.
- Prop 3.4 (global convergence) assumes that the particle law converges to a probability measure. The paper says it cannot exclude loss of mass at infinity and suggests adding a regulariser. The initial law must meet Assumption 3.1 (a separating manifold in the weights and exponential moments). Props 3.9 and 4.4 additionally assume the limiting unit law has full support, which the paper argues is reasonable but does not prove.
- The paper states in Sec. 1.1 that its results apply to deep networks when only the final layer grows large at fixed depth; `depth` lists both for that reason, while experiments use one hidden layer (and a radial-basis network in Sec. 5.1).
- Noise scaling: the abstract says the error scales universally as $O(n^{-1})$; r2 is the $O(n^{-1/2})$ fluctuation before healing.
- The quantity $\mathcal E[\mu]$ is convex in the particle law, so no sampling is involved; `temperature` is not applicable except for a brief noisy warm-up in the Gaussian-kernel experiment (thermal noise for half of the run).
