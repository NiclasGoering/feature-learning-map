summary: "The paper recasts the field theory of randomly initialised single-hidden-layer networks, proposed by Halverson, Maiti and Stoner, as a renormalisation problem solved with the non-perturbative Wetterich–Morris flow of the effective action. The network output is a field over input space; the infinite-width limit is a free field with the exact two-point function, and finite width adds quartic and sextic couplings. In a passive flow the data resolution is the cutoff, and truncated flows for the mass and couplings are given in the local-potential approximation and in a momentum-dependent scheme. In an active flow the weight standard deviation is the running scale, which gives a pure scaling law for the quartic and sextic couplings, checked on simulated networks at $d_{\\rm in}=1$. Training is not studied."

- RG record: sense (c), a neural-network field theory (the NN-QFT correspondence of Halverson, Maiti and Stoner) with a non-perturbative Wetterich–Morris flow, for a single-hidden-layer Gauss-net at random initialisation, with no training. Integrated out are the data-space Fourier modes of the output field above a running scale $k$, with the exact IR two-point function (2.20) known and the UV action unknown; the passive RG takes the data resolution $1/a_0$ as cutoff, the active RG sets $k^2=2\sigma_W^2/d_{\rm in}$, Eq. (4.4). What flows is the effective action truncated at sixtic order (running $u_2$, $u_4$, $u_6$, plus $\kappa$, $Z$, $\eta_k$ in the broken phase) under the Wetterich equation (3.3): symmetric local-potential flows Eqs. (3.16), (3.20), (3.21) and beta functions (3.24)–(3.26); broken phase Eqs. (3.41)–(3.43) with Proposition 1, Eq. (3.44); a momentum-dependent closure of Blaizot–Mendez-Galain–Wschebor type, Proposition 2, Eqs. (3.51)–(3.53); and the active flow Eqs. (4.11)–(4.14), a pure scaling law $\sigma_W\,du_4/d\sigma_W=(4-d_{\rm in})u_4$. Order and maturity: non-perturbative in the couplings but truncated at $\phi^6$ ($u_4\sim1/N$, $u_6\sim1/N^2$), so it reaches second order in $1/N$; mostly analytic derivation with approximations, and numerics only check the $u_4$-versus-$\sigma_W$ scaling at $d_{\rm in}=1$ (Figs. 8, 9, 13) and integrate the passive flows once from arbitrary initial values (Fig. 7).

- Inconsistency: Eq. (4.14) at $d_{\rm in}=1$ gives $u_4\propto\sigma_W^{+3}$, whereas the panels of Figs. 8 and 9 print "theory: $\log_{10}|u_4|=-3.00\,\log_{10}\sigma_W+\dots$" and the text of Appendix A says $u_4$ decreases as $\sigma_W$ increases; the paper does not comment on the sign. The plotted theory line takes its intercept from the measured $\log_{10}|u_{4,0}|$ at $\sigma_W=1$, so only the slope is compared. The pure-scaling form follows from keeping the shape (4.3) of $\Gamma^{(2)}_k+r_k$ fixed and neglecting the vertex momentum dependence; Remark 3 says the result is expected exact only for IR reference scales, and Eq. (4.10) says the approximation breaks down at $\bar u_4\sim2/I_2$.
- Judgement calls: Propositions 1 and 2 are marked `phys`, not `thm`, because their proofs (Appendix B.1, B.3) are calculations inside the truncated flow, not statements about the network. r3 to r5 have `grade: none` because the UV initial couplings of the passive flows are not inferred from networks; Fig. 7 uses arbitrary values $u_2=1$, $u_4=-0.5$, $u_6=0.01$. `moves_mean: "no"` records that the prior output field is centred and nothing is trained.
