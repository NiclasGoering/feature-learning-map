one_line: "Mean-field Langevin dynamics converges exponentially to the entropy-regularised minimiser under a uniform log-Sobolev inequality, and annealed noise $\\alpha/\\log t$ reaches the unregularised minimum."
summary: "The paper studies noisy particle gradient descent, whose many-particle limit is mean-field Langevin dynamics, for the entropy-regularised minimisation of a convex functional of a measure. Assuming a uniform log-Sobolev inequality along the dynamics, it proves exponential convergence in objective value, relative entropy and Wasserstein distance, via an entropy-sandwich lemma. With noise decaying like $\\alpha/\\log t$ for large enough $\\alpha$, it shows convergence in value to the unregularised global minimum. The assumptions are checked for wide two-layer networks with bounded features. Experiments on kernel MMD minimisation show annealing beats noiseless particle descent."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-6) of the arXiv HTML; the proofs in the appendix (Lemmas A.1, A.2) were not read. Anchors checked against the HTML. Venue from the TMLR header ("Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (08/2022)").
- Learning-rule convention follows INGEST.md: the fixed-point statement (Prop 2.3) is `bayes`, the dynamics and annealing are `gd`. No result compares the two. Regime tag R3 is used as in the other mean-field Langevin entries.
- The paper states that its guarantees apply to the mean-field limit, not to finite-particle NPGD. Theorem 4.1 needs $\rho_\tau\ge C_0e^{-\alpha^*/\tau}$; Prop 5.1 gives this for bounded features only. The analysis does not give a finite-$m$ or discrete-step rate.
- r2 `gd_time: other`: time scale is $\tau_t=\alpha/\log t$, polynomial rate in $t$ to the regularisation path. In r5 the target is a kernel MMD to a random empirical measure on the torus, not a neural-network task; `input` and `target` are `other`, and the experiment is a numerical check of the dynamics.
- Chizat and Bach's (2018) noiseless convergence is mentioned only as related work; the limitations paragraph says the noise-based analysis misses structure that helps noiseless training.
