one_line: "Non-asymptotic, dimension-free deterministic equivalent for random feature ridge regression, used to derive scaling-law exponents and the minimal number of features for the minimax rate."
summary: "The paper proves that the test error of random feature ridge regression is, with high probability, within a multiplicative $\\tilde O(n^{-1/2}+p^{-1/2})$ of a closed-form expression that depends only on the eigenvalues of the feature map. The proof assumes a concentration property of the eigenfunctions and does not depend on the data dimension. Under power-law source and capacity conditions, the equivalent gives the error exponent as a function of the number of features and the regularisation, with all crossovers. The minimax-optimal rate needs fewer features than previously known lower bounds required. Simulations on a spiked-feature teacher and on Fashion-MNIST agree with the theory."

- Read the HTML of v3 for the main text (Secs. 1 to 4); the appendices (proofs, derivation of the exponents, Appendix E on the link with gradient-flow and SGD scaling laws) were not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a placement choice, as for other kernel-regression entries: the object is ridge regression on fixed random features, with no training dynamics. `finite-exact` because the bounds are non-asymptotic in $n$ and $p$, with no input-dimension limit.
- Theorem 3.3 needs Assumption 3.1 (concentration of the eigenfunctions), which the paper says excludes many nonlinear feature maps. The simulations (spiked tanh features, erf features on Fashion-MNIST) are used as evidence beyond it.
- The exponents in Thm 4.1 are stated to follow from Thm 3.3 inside a range of $(\ell,q)$ where the approximation error vanishes; the derivation is in the appendix.
- Cor. 3.4 and Cor. 3.5 are numbered as in the text and their anchors follow the HTML counter in Sec. 3; r4 cites both.
