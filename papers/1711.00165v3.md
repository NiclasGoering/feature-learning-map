summary: "A deep fully connected network with iid parameters and weight variance scaled inversely with width is, by taking hidden widths to infinity in succession, a Gaussian process whose covariance follows a layer-by-layer recursion in the pre-activation kernel. This allows exact Bayesian regression with a deep network prior, and the paper gives an efficient numerical scheme for the kernel for general nonlinearities. On MNIST and CIFAR-10, treated as regression on one-hot labels, the NNGP is competitive with SGD-trained networks and often better, and its predictive variance correlates with test error. Trained networks approach the NNGP as width grows. Kernel performance is linked to the fixed-point analysis of signal propagation."

- Read the HTML of v3 (3 Mar 2018) in full except the appendices on kernel implementation details and extra plots (Appendix A to E skimmed).
- The infinite-width argument takes widths to infinity in succession and uses the central limit theorem at each step. The paper does not prove it, and later work (Matthews et al. 2018, queued in this map) gives the rigorous version. For this reason r1 is `phys`.
- r2 compares an exact Bayesian predictor with Adam-trained networks whose learning rate and initial variances were tuned by a hyperparameter tuner, and the label is regression on one-hot targets. `gd_step_size: other` since a tuned Adam learning rate is not covered by the vocabulary.
- Section 3.2 relates the kernel recursion to the signal-propagation fixed points (ordered/chaotic phases) of Poole et al. and Schoenholz et al. It is not recorded as a result.
