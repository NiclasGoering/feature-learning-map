summary: "The paper computes the Gibbs posterior of deep linear networks with $L$ hidden layers of width $N$ in the limit $N,P\\to\\infty$ at fixed $\\alpha=P/N$, with no assumption on inputs or targets. Integrating out weight matrices one at a time from the readout (back-propagating kernel renormalization) rescales the remaining kernel by a scalar order parameter set by a saddle-point equation. At zero temperature the mean predictor equals the infinite-width GP predictor and only the variance is multiplied by $u_0^L$, which yields phase diagrams of the generalisation error against width, depth and prior scale. Finite temperature, multiple outputs and a heuristic extension to ReLU networks are also treated. Predictions are compared with unadjusted Langevin sampling on synthetic data and MNIST."

- Read v2 (18 Sep 2021, same as the PRX version) in full, using the HTML for anchors; equation numbers were checked against the v2 PDF. The supplementary material (SM I–VIII), which holds the monotonicity analyses behind r2, is not part of the arXiv version and was not read. r2 cites the main-text statements.
- Depth: the theory holds for any $L\ge1$, and Figs. 3, 6, 8, 10–14 use $L=1$, so `one-hidden-layer` is listed alongside `deep`. In r2, `inf-depth` refers to the $L\to\infty$ fixed point of the fixed-$\alpha$ equation (Eqs. 29–30), taken after the thermodynamic limit.
- Parametrisation: the forward pass has explicit $1/\sqrt{N}$ factors and $O(1)$ weights with prior variance $\sigma^2$ (the regulariser is $T\sigma^{-2}\Theta^\top\Theta/2$ in Eq. 2). It is recorded as `ntk`, but the paper does not name it, and for the Gibbs measure it is equivalent to SP with variance $\sigma^2/N$.
- Narrow networks ($\alpha>1$) at $T=0$ use pseudo-inverses (Eqs. 14–16). The $u_0$ equation and the predictor statistics hold for all $\alpha$ as long as $\alpha_0<1$. For $\alpha_0>1$ the predictor is the least-squares one and its variance vanishes (Eq. 32).
- The ReLU extension is called "heuristic" in the abstract and an "ansatz" in Sec. VI. Its support is numerical only, so r4 has status `num`.
- Numerics: unadjusted Langevin (Eq. 129) at $T=10^{-3}$ ($10^{-4}$ for Figs. 8–9). The number of chains, the run lengths and any mixing diagnostics are not reported, and equilibrium is judged by agreement with theory. Figs. 6 and 14 use the finite-$T$ theory.
- Grade B because $u_0$ and the predictor need $r_0$ and $K_0^{-1}$ from the empirical training Gram matrix.
