summary: "The paper traces the distribution of preactivations through a multilayer perceptron at large but finite width $n$, keeping Gaussian-process kernel, a self-energy correction and a four-point vertex, with recursive equations resembling renormalisation-group flow. This yields a weakly non-Gaussian prior over outputs at order $1/n$ for any activation. A perturbative scheme around Gaussian-process regression then gives the Bayesian posterior mean at finite width. For deep linear networks the leading correction vanishes. For a two-layer quadratic network on MNIST, finite width improves accuracy at small training sets, which the author reads as regularisation."

- Read the HTML v2 main text (Secs. I-VI); the Wick-contraction derivations and the proof in Appendices A-D were not checked.
- Weight and bias variances follow Neal; the formalism needs a fixed number of outputs $n_L$ and finite depth/width, perturbative when depth/width is small (Sec. V).
- The posterior is computed with a Gaussian-process (noise-free or fixed-variance) likelihood as in the textbook GP treatment; temperature is not discussed, hence `not-stated`.
- The remark about deep linear networks (r3) is a single sentence in Sec. V with no derivation; `grade: none` for that reason.
- The closing "Dreams" section speculates about renormalisation-group fixed points and the lazy-to-feature-learning transition; not claimed as results.
