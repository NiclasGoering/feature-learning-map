one_line: "Layer-by-layer recursion for the $1/n$ non-Gaussian prior of finite-width MLPs with arbitrary activation, and a perturbative scheme for Bayesian inference with it."
summary: "The paper traces the distribution of preactivations through a multilayer perceptron at large but finite width $n$, keeping Gaussian-process kernel, a self-energy correction and a four-point vertex, with recursive equations resembling renormalisation-group flow. This yields a weakly non-Gaussian prior over outputs at order $1/n$ for any activation. A perturbative scheme around Gaussian-process regression then gives the Bayesian posterior mean at finite width. For deep linear networks the leading correction vanishes. For a two-layer quadratic network on MNIST, finite width improves accuracy at small training sets, which the author reads as regularisation."

- Read the main text of v2 (HTML and PDF), App. C (examples, experiments) and App. D (NGPM', NGPM''); the Wick-contraction derivations and the proof in Appendices A-B were not checked. HTML numbers equations sequentially: Eq. (22) of the HTML is Eq. (5.5) of the PDF.
- Inference is noise-free: Eq. (Bayes) conditions on the exact outputs and (GPM) uses $\tilde K_{RR}^{-1}$, hence `temperature: to-zero` and `label_noise: none`. The formalism needs a fixed number of outputs $n_L$ (fn. 1, fn. 5) and small depth/width (Sec. V).
- r2: (NGPM'') is the first-order variation of the GP mean under $\tilde K\to\tilde K+\epsilon A$, with $A$ = self-energy + $\frac12V\phi\phi$ (along the targets) $-\frac{n_L}2VK^{-1}$ $-$ a fluctuation pairing of $V$. Hence T2 `kernel-grows-along-target`, although the paper does not speak of features.
- r3 (deep linear) is one sentence in Sec. V. Checked with the deep-linear vertex (4.3): every term of $A$ has the form $K_{\alpha R}M$ and cancels in the predictor at zero noise. `grade: none` kept.
- Fig. 1 compares single-input prior output histograms over $10^6$ weight draws with the theory; Fig. 2 evaluates the formula (NGPM'') on MNIST, no posterior is sampled. The closing "Dreams" section is speculation, not a result.
