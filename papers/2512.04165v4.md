one_line: "Heuristic variational scaling arguments predict which feature-learning pattern a Bayesian network adopts and the sample complexity scale, from pen-and-paper energies."
summary: "The paper lower-bounds the sample size needed for a Bayesian network to align with its target by the negative log prior probability of that alignment, a Chernoff-type rate function. It approximates the rate function variationally over per-layer feature-learning patterns (Gaussian process, Gaussian amplification, specialisation, magnetisation) and uses heuristic rules for how a spike propagates to the next layer's kernel. Minimising the resulting energies gives scaling laws for two-layer fully connected and convolutional networks, three-layer networks and softmax attention. Predictions are checked against ensembles of Langevin-trained networks and, for two layers, against a large-deviation computation."

- Read the main text of v4 in full from the HTML version; Appendices A, B, C, E, F were read through the parts that state the bound, the pattern energies and the experimental setup (not every derivation line).
- The paper calls its results heuristic. r1 is derived in App. A by an elementary argument and is marked `thm`; the rest is `phys`. The abstract mentions $P_*=\sqrt L$ for attention while Sec. 6.2 gives $\sqrt{HLd^3}$; I used Sec. 6.2.
- Sampler `ula` and mixing `low` are judgement calls: the paper says networks are trained by Langevin dynamics at ridge $\kappa=O(1)$ and reports no mixing diagnostics.
- Width group: only the proportional-in-$d$ setting is tested; the mean-field-scaling extension in App. E.1.1 is not given its own result and is not tagged R3.
- Only five results are recorded. App. E.1.2 also gives $P_*\propto d^{3/4}$ for non-overlapping-patch CNNs, matching an earlier mean-field result that the paper cites; it is not a separate result here.
