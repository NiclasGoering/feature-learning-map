one_line: "Layer-wise GD on a three-layer network learns hierarchical Gaussian targets with fewer samples than kernels or two-layer nets, by reducing effective dimension layer by layer."
summary: "The paper introduces single- and multi-index Gaussian hierarchical targets (SIGHT, MIGHT), where the label depends on a low-dimensional non-linear feature of a lower-dimensional linear projection of Gaussian inputs. For a three-layer network trained layer by layer, it proves that the first layer recovers the projection from about $d^{1+\\varepsilon_1}$ samples, one pre-conditioned step on the second layer then recovers the non-linear feature from about $d^{k\\varepsilon_1}$ samples, and a ridge readout fits the target. Kernels and two-layer networks are argued to need more samples. A weaker statement is proved for deeper targets, a compositional information exponent is conjectured to govern hierarchical learning, and simulations show joint backpropagation behaves similarly."

- Read the HTML v4 in full, including App. A, B.1, C, D, E. The ID given in the request was credited to "Fu et al."; the arXiv paper is by Dandi, Pesce, Zdeborová and Krzakala, and Fu et al. 2025 is only a cited related work. Venue taken from the arXiv journal reference.
- Training protocol of Thm 1 is idealised: W1 neuron-wise spherical SGD for polylog steps on correlation loss, then W2 re-initialised to zero and given one pre-conditioned (sample-covariance) step, then ridge on w3. `learned_layers: [all]` records that all are trained, but by this staged procedure, not jointly; hence `parametrisation: other`, and step-size/time fields are given per stage.
- Assumptions needed by Thm 1: information exponent 1 for $g^\star$ and exactly 2 for $P_k$ (Ass. 1), $a^\star_i=1$, activation conditions (Ass. 2), and $\mathbb{E}[g^\star He_j]=0$ for $1<j\le k$ (Ass. 3, technical). Widths $p_1=p_2\sim d^{k\varepsilon_1+\delta'}$ are assumed for convenience. Grade A is a judgement: the thresholds are explicit exponents in $\varepsilon_1,k$, but they are upper bounds with $\tilde O$ and without constants.
- The advantage over kernels and two-layer nets is argued heuristically in Sec. 2 and by simulation (Fig. 4); the two-layer lower bound is not proved (App. A.1 says an unconditional lower bound is open), so it is not a separate result. MIGHT with $r>1$ is only partly covered (App. D; specialisation needs several steps). The largest simulated $d$ was not checked.
- `target: [other]` because SIGHT/MIGHT compose a polynomial of a linear projection with a link; `op-06` is listed since GD gets an analytic sample complexity on a hierarchical target while no Bayesian analogue is claimed.
