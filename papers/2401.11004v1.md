summary: "The paper tests the Bayesian effective action for fully connected one-hidden-layer networks at $P/N_1\\to\\alpha_1$ against numerical experiments. Networks with erf activation are sampled with a discretised Langevin dynamics, with thermalisation checks, blocking-based error bars and control of the finite step size. The predicted generalisation loss agrees with the sampled one when varying temperature, width, training-set size and the readout prior, apart from small deviations at small width. The theory also gives a test accuracy for binary classification, whose dependence on both priors is shown as a prediction only. The authors conclude that a global rescaling of the infinite-width kernel is a main renormalisation mechanism in standard-scaled shallow networks."

- Read the full HTML of v1, including the supplement (accuracy derivation, Sec. III; Monte Carlo, Sec. IV). There is no journal reference in the arXiv metadata, so `venue` is `preprint`. Apart from the test-accuracy formula (SM Eqs. 21-34) and a finite-$T$ saddle solver, no new theory is derived, so the entry stays `empirical` (`theory-experiments` is defensible).
- Mixing: unadjusted Euler Langevin at $T=0.01$–$0.1$ (plus a few higher-$T$ checks), with thermalisation windows, blocking error bars, integrated autocorrelation times $\tau_{int}\gg1$ and step sizes reduced until finite-$\epsilon$ bias is invisible. This sits between the definitions of `low` and `high`; `low` was chosen because the paper reports the autocorrelation analysis, but a checker may prefer `high`.
- The paper notes large step-size systematics at $T\sim0.5$ and negligible ones at $T\sim0.01$; a figure with temperatures up to 0.1 is the main range shown.
- Chains: runs of up to $5\cdot10^7$ updates per setting (Fig. 5 shows a single Monte Carlo run). `moves_mean` is `yes` for r1-r3: SM Eq. (32) writes the mean as a function of $\bar Q$, and at $T>0$ it is the NNGP mean with ridge $\lambda_1T/\bar Q$; it cancels only at $T=0$. r4 is left `unknown` (no mechanism).
- All simulations use $\lambda_0=1$; $\lambda_0$ is varied only in the theory-only Fig. 4. The experiments ($P=1000$ against $N_0=784$, $1024$) lie far outside the $P=O(\sqrt{N_0})$ range that the Breuer-Major argument guarantees for erf; the paper does not discuss this.
- `op-03` (mixing and phase coverage) is listed because the paper documents a careful finite-step and autocorrelation protocol for unadjusted Langevin.
