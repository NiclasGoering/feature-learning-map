Networks with erf activation are sampled with a discretised Langevin dynamics, with thermalisation checks, blocking-based error bars and control of the finite step size. The predicted generalisation loss agrees with the sampled one when varying temperature, width, training-set size and the two Gaussian priors, apart from small deviations at small width. The theory also gives a test accuracy for binary classification. The authors conclude that a global rescaling of the infinite-width kernel is a main renormalisation mechanism in standard-scaled shallow networks."

- Read the full HTML of v1, including the Monte Carlo appendix (Sec. IV). There is no journal reference in the arXiv metadata, so `venue` is `preprint`. No theory beyond the cited effective action is derived, so the entry is `empirical`.
- Mixing: unadjusted Euler Langevin at $T=0.01$–$0.1$ (plus a few higher-$T$ checks), with thermalisation windows, blocking error bars, integrated autocorrelation times $\tau_{int}\gg1$ and step sizes reduced until finite-$\epsilon$ bias is invisible. This sits between the definitions of `low` and `high`; `low` was chosen because the paper reports the autocorrelation analysis, but a checker may prefer `high`.
- The paper notes large step-size systematics at $T\sim0.5$ and negligible ones at $T\sim0.01$; a figure with temperatures up to 0.1 is the main range shown.
- Chains: runs of up to $5\cdot10^7$ updates per setting (Fig. 5); `moves_mean` is `unknown` because the paper only discusses generalisation loss, not the mean predictor.
- `op-03` (mixing and phase coverage) is listed because the paper documents a careful finite-step and autocorrelation protocol for unadjusted Langevin.
