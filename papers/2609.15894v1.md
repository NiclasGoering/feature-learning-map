summary: "The paper introduces Quenched Ensemble Sampling (QES), which keeps the monotonically decreasing energy path of nested sampling but replaces the hard constraint by a soft microcanonical factor $(E-U)_+^\\nu$, so gradient-based kernels such as MALA can be used in high dimension. An exact augmentation shows that the marginal over $x$ is the posterior. On a spike-slab model with a first-order transition, QES estimates the marginal likelihood correctly while tempered SMC is about 2.3 nats low. On small tanh networks QES matches NUTS and MCLMC in predictive accuracy and supports model comparison between activations. On a lattice $\\phi^6$ theory it crosses a first-order transition and estimates the partition function."

- Read the main text, including Secs. 3 and 4 and Tables 3–5; appendices (convergence criteria, ESS conventions) not checked.
- Only the BNN experiments concern neural networks; the other experiments are a spike-slab model and a lattice field theory, so the setting, data and width fields describe the BNN part. Regime tag R1 and `finite-proportional` are the closest schema group and are chosen only because Bayes entries need a regime tag.
- Mixing `critical` because the benchmarks are built around first-order transitions with coexisting phases.
- The paper scans the BNN posteriors for phase transitions (Sec. 4.2, last paragraph, Fig. 4) and finds no evidence of one; this is now r5. The resolution is limited by the Gamma($\nu+1$) smoothing of $P(E)$ (a few nats, $\nu=2$).
- r3: NUTS has the highest ESS per gradient (Table 3: 4,375–11,631 against 1,189–3,184 for QES), and on glass, sonar and winered the test NLL weakly favours the activation the evidence rejects (Table 4 caption). Laplace-GGN factors are two to four times larger and pick the opposite activation on glass and winered.
- r1 is an exact identity shown in-line (Gamma integral after Eq. 8 and before Eq. 11), hence `thm`; the adaptive level schedule makes the SMC estimator biased at finite particle number (Sec. 2.1, App. C.2). The tempering convention is the map's: $\beta$ on the likelihood only, prior untempered (Eq. 1).
- op-03 is touched through the phase-coverage question: QES is a proposed route across first-order transitions.
