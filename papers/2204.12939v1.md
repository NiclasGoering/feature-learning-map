summary: "The paper treats sequential Bayesian updating of a fixed parametric model as a flow in the amount of data $T$. For independent data and small update batches every observable obeys $\\partial_T\\bar O=-\\mathrm{Var}(O,D)$. At late times with a Gaussian posterior, saturating the Cramér-Rao bound gives a posterior covariance $\\propto 1/T$, and hidden variables add a driving or dissipation term. These results are derived at leading order, with corrections solved numerically for one parameter, and checked on 1D Ising coupling inference. Network experiments only fit loss against training-set size to power laws or exponentials, so nothing is derived about network posteriors, kernels or feature learning, and the link to renormalisation is an analogy."

- RG record: sense (d), learning as RG on data, by analogy only (Secs. 1, 2.7, 6). Nothing is integrated out: the flow variable is the data count $T=N\tau$, hidden parameters are held fixed like slow modes (Sec. 2.7, footnote), and the paper stresses that the flow gains information where RG loses it. What flows are posterior moments, $\partial_T\bar O=-\mathrm{Var}(O,D)$ (Eq. (2.25)), at leading order in the continuum limit $N\gg N_k\gg1$ with no assumption on the posterior shape, closed at late $T$ by a Gaussian posterior to Eq. (2.38), solved by $\mathcal I_*^{ij}/T$ (Eq. (2.39)), with hidden variables adding $-\beta\bar C^{11}$ (Eq. (2.57)). Beyond leading order only numerically (Eqs. (2.47), (2.50), Tables 1-2); no Wilsonian or Polchinski-type equation is derived (named as future work in Sec. 6), so maturity is derivation plus numerics for the Bayes flow and analogy only for RG.
- What is derived: Eq. (2.16) follows from Bayes' rule under three assumptions, independent draws (Eq. (2.7)), a plug-in of the running maximum-likelihood estimate (Eq. (2.8)) and $N\gg N_k\gg1$ at leading order in $1/N_k$. Its solution, Eq. (3.7), is the standard large-sample posterior $\propto e^{-TD_{KL}}$, and for Gaussian data Eq. (3.8) is, as the paper says, the Normal-Inverse-Wishart posterior of a conjugate prior. The $1/T$ law is a particular solution checked by substitution (Eq. (2.40)); the paper assumes Cramér-Rao saturation and a Gaussian late-time posterior and does not show that general solutions approach it. The hidden-variable solution (2.59) needs Eq. (2.55) and drops $O(\beta^2)$; the $1/T^{1+\nu}$ interpolation in Sec. 2.7 is phenomenological. Section 3.2 only restates Gaussian-process data as a multivariate Gaussian on finite partitions.
- Neural networks and kernels: no statement is made about network posteriors, NNGP or NTK kernels, kernel adaptation or feature learning. Section 5 trains a one-hidden-layer FF net (128 units) and CNNs with Adam (learning rate 0.001, epochs scaled as 1/size so total training is matched; batch size, initialisation and the FF hidden activation are not stated) and fits loss against training-set size with $aT^{-b}+c$ over about one decade of $T$. After training the network is tested on the 10,000-image test set (Sec. 5); the paper does not say explicitly that the plotted loss is this test loss. The link between the Bayesian covariance flow and the loss rests on the stated assumption that the loss is quadratic near the final inference; the paper calls the comparison qualitative. r5 is marked `compared_other_rule: "yes"` and `[bayes, gd]` because the paper sets the Bayes-flow prediction against gradient-trained networks, but no Bayesian posterior over network weights is computed or sampled.

(A.1) lacks; Sec. 2.7 writes $1/T^{1+\nu}$ while the text under Table 3 writes $1/T^{1-\nu}$; Eq. (4.5) is a periodic chain but Eq.
