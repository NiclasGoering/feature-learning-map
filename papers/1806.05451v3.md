summary: "The paper studies a teacher-student committee machine with Gaussian inputs, a fixed number $K$ of hidden units and $m/n=\\alpha$ samples per input dimension. Under a technical assumption it proves the replica formula for the free entropy by adaptive interpolation, and derives the Bayes-optimal generalization error from it. An AMP algorithm and its state evolution are given. For $K=2$ the hidden units specialize beyond a threshold in $\\alpha$. For large $K$ with $\\alpha=\\Theta(K)$ the specialization is a first-order transition, and AMP stays on the non-specialized plateau, which suggests a large computational gap."

- Read the full HTML text of v3 (main text, discussion and proof outline); proofs in App. A and the AMP derivation in App. F, G were only skimmed. Anchors checked against the arXiv HTML.
- Theorem 3.1 needs the unproven Assumption 1 (Sec. 5.3, footnote 2 says later work removed it), so `status: thm` is conditional on it. The hypotheses (H1)-(H3) are bounded prior support and a smooth bounded activation, relaxed in Remark 3.2 to include the sign activation; `activation` lists both.
- The student uses the teacher's architecture and prior, so this is Bayes-optimal learning; the paper does not study over-parametrized students. The regime tag R6 is used for the Bayes-optimal teacher-student setting with $K=\Theta(1)$.
- Specialization values in r2 come from numerically solving the state-evolution fixed points, hence `num`; the large-$K$ analysis in r3 and r4 is replica calculation (App. D), hence `phys`. Gaussian-weight values in r2: $\alpha_{\rm spec}^G(K=2)\simeq2.04$; binary-weight values $\alpha^B_{\rm spec}\simeq1.58$, $\alpha^B_{\rm perf}\simeq1.99$ for AMP and $\alpha^B_{\rm IT}\simeq1.54$ information-theoretically.
- The AMP optimality claim is a conjecture of the community, not proved here. The case $K/n=\Theta(1)$ is named as open.
