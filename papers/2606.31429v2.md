one_line: "For spherical mean-field Langevin dynamics on Gaussian index models, the low-temperature stationary law forms a multi-spike structure that yields minimax $d/N$ and $Md/N$ rates."
summary: "The paper defines feature learning through a base-fiber decomposition: training selects a hidden-layer distribution, and estimation happens in the induced feature space. For a spherical mean-field network trained by Langevin dynamics, its long-time limit is an entropy-regularised minimiser. For Gaussian multi-index targets it concentrates near the hidden directions with high probability, with a sharp change at temperature of order one. For single-index targets the concentration is on the sphere or on projective space, by parity of the information exponent. The learned feature space aligns with the signal, giving rates $d/N$ and $Md/N$ up to logarithms, and the learned kernel stays away from the initial one."

- Read the main text of v2 (Secs. 1-4) in full from the HTML version; proofs in the appendices (about 1100 lines) were not read, only their statements as restated in the main text (including Theorem 3, the frame-condition refinement, which is not recorded as a separate result).
- The estimator is the long-time limit of mean-field Langevin dynamics, an entropy-regularised minimiser, so `learning_rule` is `bayes`. The paper says the stationary law may be read as a posterior from a uniform prior (remark after Thm 2) and that convergence time is $\Omega(\exp(N))$.
- Width group: the network is a mean-field measure (infinite width), but the sample size $N$ grows with $d$ ($N\gtrsim d\log d$), so I used `high-dim` and tag R6, not the fixed-$P$ mean-field group. Here $N$ in the paper is the number of samples, not width. Only the hidden layer is trained (`first-layer-only`).
- Constants such as $c_0$, $C$ are not specified, so grades are `none`. Label noise is bounded and centred, not Gaussian, so `other`.
- Anchor ids in the HTML are `ThmProposition1`, `ThmTheorem1` and so on, with Proposition 3 appearing as `ThmProposition0` in the HTML.
