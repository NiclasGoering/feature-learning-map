It derives a closed form for the population loss and shows that regular $k$-gons of the weight columns are critical points, with local learning coefficients from singular learning theory. The free energy formula predicts a posterior transition from 5-gons to 6-gons near $n\\approx600$ samples, which NUTS sampling confirms. SGD trajectories pass through plateaus at the same critical points, with the estimated local learning coefficient rising as the loss falls. The authors conjecture that dynamical transitions have Bayesian antecedents."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-6) and the statement of Lemma 3.1; the appendices (proofs of critical points, free energy computation in App. C.2, local learning coefficient estimation in App. K) were not read.
- Regime tag: the validator requires one for `bayes`. The analysis is for a fixed small model with the sample size $n$ varied (singular learning theory), which fits none of R0-R6; R6 with `high-dim` is a forced fit. Same issue for `finite-exact`, the honest width group.
- Conjectures in Sec. 3.2 (the $c$-gon is a critical point for $c>8$ divisible by 4, and none exists otherwise) are not recorded as a separate result. The $4$-gon is not an analytic point of $H$ (App. J).
- The free energy formula is asymptotic in $n$ while the transition is read off at finite $n$; the constants $c_\alpha$ are absorbed. The paper itself says that below $n\approx400$ the MCMC classification becomes uncertain, so the agreement for small $n$ should not be read as evidence.
- The label `competing-codes` is a reuse: the $k$-gons are different geometric encodings of superposed features; the paper does not use the word code.
