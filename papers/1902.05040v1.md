summary: "The paper asks which functions an infinite-width ReLU network with one hidden layer can represent when the squared Euclidean norm of its weights, excluding hidden biases, is bounded. For univariate functions the minimal norm is $\\max(\\int|f''|,|f'(-\\infty)+f'(\\infty)|)$, so norm-regularised training is a total-variation penalty on the derivative. Hence the minimal-norm interpolant of a sample is a linear spline, and any regularised fit has a piecewise-linear minimiser with breakpoints at the data. In higher dimensions an exact formula is derived only for convex functions. A small experiment shows trained networks reach the optimal norm with smoother functions."

- Read the full paper, including the proof of Thm 3.1 and the multivariate discussion of Sec. 5; I did not read Appendix proofs (A, B, C, D) line by line.
- The paper has no learning rule of its own. It treats the global minimiser of a weight-norm-regularised loss, so I used `learning_rule: [gd]` as the closest bin (norm regularisation as the implicit bias of training). Please re-check.
- `width_limit: inf-meanfield-fixedP` is the closest bin for infinite width with a convex (measure-valued) norm; there is no μP or mean-field scaling in the paper.
- The label `ridge-atoms` ("sparse ridge atoms", T4) is used because the minimiser needs at most $N+1$ units (Rosset et al., as cited), with the cost a variation norm. The paper does not use the word "atoms".
- The paper conjectures a nuclear-norm-of-Hessian formula and then argues the naive version cannot be right (Sec. 5), so no conjecture result is recorded.
- Only an exact formula for univariate functions is given; the title question is answered for $d=1$ only. The multivariate case is the subject of 1910.01635 (not read here).
