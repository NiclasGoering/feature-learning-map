- RG record: (d) learning as RG on data, a claimed correspondence rather than coarse-graining of the network: the continuous-field RG of the input density by the scale transformation $x_i\mapsto Z(\lambda)^{1/2}x_i/\lambda$ (Eq. (30)) is matched with simulated-annealing training of a fully connected network, with training time $t$ playing the role of $\lambda$ (Sec. 3.2.1). Nothing is integrated out; what flows are the couplings $\theta_r$ of the energy $\sum_r\theta_r(\sum_i x_i^2)^r$, $\theta_r\mapsto\theta_r Z(\lambda)^r\lambda^{-2r}$ (Eq. (32)) with fixed points Eq. (33), and on the network side the feature-layer couplings $\tilde\theta_r^t$ fixed by partition-function invariance (Eqs. (49), (50)); the RG equation is Eq. (36), Eq. (37) at $\lambda\to\infty$. Thm 3.1 (stated as Thm 1.1 in the introduction) gives $\tilde\theta_r^t\to\theta_r^*$ as an exact $t\to\infty$ statement, but it assumes the fixed points exist and that connected correlation functions are invariant (Eq. (45)), and $Z(\lambda)$ is left free, so no fixed point is computed. No expansion in any small parameter, so "beyond first order" does not apply; maturity is a derivation resting on assumptions, with no numerics.
- Read the whole paper in the arXiv HTML (v1 is the only version; the companion is cited as [1, 2], where [2] is arXiv 2212.00005 and [1] the journal version "on Ising model" of the same work). Optimal-parameter condition: the loss, Eqs. (3) and (48), is the squared distance of the output to the network's own output at $W^*$ plus $\tau\|W-W^*\|^2$, so the minimiser $W^*$ is unique by construction and the targets are exactly realisable. This is why I entered `target: teacher-network` (same architecture, planted $W^*$); the paper does not use the word teacher. Training is the annealed diffusion Eq. (6), studied as a dynamics, hence `learning_rule: [gd]` as in 2212.00005.
- The class is narrower than the title: after the change of variables of Eqs. (17)–(25) the energy is $\sum_r\theta_r(\sum_i x_i^2)^r$ with $\theta_r>0$ (even polynomial, radially symmetric), and the conclusion says so. Thm 1.1 and Thm 3.1 are the same result; Thm 1.1 omits the $g(t)$ term of Eq. (40) in its partition-function condition, and Thm 3.1 allows $\theta_r^*\ge0$ while the integrability bound in Eqs. (77)–(80) uses $\tilde\theta_R^*>0$. The training points $x_k$ enter only through the sums in Eq. (39); the data density enters only through the couplings $\theta_r$ and the assumed fixed points.
