one_line: "No finite checklist of convergence diagnostics can certify MCMC convergence; the worst undetected error decays as a Kolmogorov width, and slow-mode diagnostics become nearly optimal."
summary: "Using kernel mean embeddings, the paper represents each convergence diagnostic as a direction in a Hilbert space and the chain's non-convergence as a vector. Any finite set of diagnostics can pass while that vector is nonzero, so more chains, longer runs or nonlinear diagnostics cannot certify convergence. Under a source condition the worst undetected discrepancy after $m$ best diagnostics is $R\\lambda_{m+1}^{r}$, and for reversible samplers the slow transition modes give nearly optimal diagnostics at late times. An anytime-valid bound and a limit on certifiable effective sample size follow. Experiments use MALA on mixtures and five regression posteriors."

- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call: no regime fits generic MCMC theory. `high-dim` is placed on r1 only to satisfy the tag rule. Setting fields are `not-stated` for the same reason; `depth: one-hidden-layer` is a placeholder (the field is required and the paper has no network).
- Theorem 2.1 is exact over the source ellipsoid; the paper notes its worst case need not be realised by a probability law (App. A.2). Anchor for Thms 2.1-2.2 is the section id because I could not map the theorem ids reliably.
- Experiments: MALA on a two-component Gaussian mixture (four chains miss one mode though $\hat R=1.002$), a synthetic circle and five regression posteriors; I did not check which regression datasets.
