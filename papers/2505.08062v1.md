one_line: "Functional large-deviation principle for the layer covariance process of Gaussian networks; posterior LDP is lazy in standard scaling, data-dependent under mean-field scaling."
summary: "The layer-wise random covariance functions of a fully connected Gaussian network form a Markov process in the trace-class operators, and the paper proves a large-deviation principle for it with speed equal to the width, first in trace norm and then in continuous functions. A Varadhan-type lemma turns this into posterior LDPs under a Gaussian likelihood. With the standard likelihood the posterior rate function equals the prior one, so the training data has no effect. With the mean-field rescaled likelihood the rate gains a data term, which the authors read as feature learning."

- Read Secs. 1-4 and the statements of Sec. 5 in the HTML v1; proofs in Secs. 6-7 and the appendix were not checked line by line.
- Pure theory, no experiments. The paper-level defaults describe the lazy case (r1, r2); r3 overrides to the mean-field likelihood. The mean-field prior scale is called "pathological" by the authors since outputs vanish at the prior level, but the posterior of the covariance is still non-trivial.
- Theorems need the growth bound $\sigma(x)^2\le A(1+|x|^r)$ with $r<2$ and, for the continuous-function version, a Lipschitz condition (H4); the authors expect the borderline $r=2$ to hold.
- Anchors `S3.Thmdefinition3` etc. are the HTML ids of the theorem-like environments (arXiv's renderer numbers them as "definition"); worth a quick check that Thm 3.3, Prop 4.2 and 4.3 sit on them.
