summary: "The paper shows that unadjusted Langevin Monte Carlo trains depth-two networks of any width on any bounded data, for squared and logistic losses with smooth bounded activations. A Frobenius-norm penalty above a critical value independent of the width makes the Gibbs measure satisfy a Poincaré inequality, because the loss is a Villani function. A recent non-asymptotic result then gives convergence of the iterates in $q$-Rényi divergence. By stability arguments, this yields a bound on the expected excess population risk. Experiments with tanh nets of width 16 to 1024 on a sine target and a comparison with AdamW support the setting."

- Read Secs. 1-5 and the statements of the lemmas and theorems; the appendix proofs (A.1 Rényi convergence, B.3 risk bound) were not checked.
- LMC is treated as a training dynamics whose law is shown to converge to a Gibbs measure, so `learning_rule: gd` (Langevin studied as dynamics) with Bayes dials `not-applicable`. A reader may prefer `bayes`; then a regime tag would be needed and none fits.
- Only the first layer is trained (outer weights $a$ fixed). The exact-width independence of the critical penalty holds with $\|a\|_2$ fixed; the experiments hold $\|a\|_2=2$ to keep the critical value fixed.
- The theorems need bounded smooth activations (tanh, sigmoid) and a penalty above $\lambda_c$; the paper proves no result without the penalty. Results hold at any finite width, hence `finite-exact`.
- Step size and step count in Thm 4.1-4.2 are given only in $\tilde\Theta$ form with problem-dependent constants (Rényi divergence of the initialisation, Poincaré constant), so `gd_time: other`.
