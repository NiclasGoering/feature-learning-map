summary: "The paper studies how the scale $\\alpha$ of initialisation moves gradient flow on homogeneous models between a kernel regime, where the tangent kernel stays fixed, and a rich regime, where it adapts. For depth-2 and depth-$D$ diagonal linear networks, the interpolating solution is the exact minimiser of an explicit potential $Q_\\alpha$ that moves from the $\\ell_2$ norm at large $\\alpha$ to the $\\ell_1$ norm at small $\\alpha$. Reaching the rich limit needs exponentially small $\\alpha$, and sparse regression generalises only there. For matrix factorisation, width $k$ and scale $\\sigma$ enter through $\\sigma\\sqrt k$. Experiments on ReLU networks show the same transition."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-7) of the arXiv HTML; the appendix proofs (Thm 5 and Corollary G for non-commuting measurements, derivation of the implicit biases, experimental details) were not read. The first page of v3 gives no venue, so `preprint`. Anchors checked against the HTML.
- Setting: the paper's diagonal linear model $\langle\beta_w,x\rangle$ with $\beta_w=w_+^2-w_-^2$ has 2d units, and its depth-2 form is a one-hidden-layer network; depth-$D$ versions are `deep`. All Thm 1-3 results need the unbiased initialisation $F(\alpha w_0)=0$ and interpolation $X\beta^\infty=y$ (assumed, not proved). They are exact at any $\alpha>0$, hence `finite-exact` for the default width limit.
- r4 sits in an intermediate scaling between kernel and rich behaviour as width grows. Both `inf-kernel` and `inf-meanfield-fixedP` are listed because the paper's result covers both ends of the $\sigma\sqrt k$ axis; Thm 4 itself requires commuting measurements, and non-commuting cases are supported only by experiments on matrix completion (Fig. 5) and by the kernel-side Thm 5.
- The statement of r2 shortens Thm 2: the exponential threshold is a sufficient condition, and the paper only shows necessity for a universal $\ell_1$ approximation (Lemma 2 in App. D).
- Fig. 6 uses a synthetic regression task, MNIST (5000 hidden units, cross-entropy) and a VGG11-like network on CIFAR-10. The paper states that the experiments use the depth-adjusted scale $\alpha^D$ on the synthetic data.
