summary: "The paper takes the trained weights of a deep network to follow a Gibbs law $e^{-\\beta C}$, the long-time limit of Langevin training. Promoting each neuron to an extra variable, tied to its inputs by a soft constraint of strength $\\beta_v$, rewrites the cost as a sum of identical nearest-layer terms with approximate translation symmetry across depth, which recovers the network as $\\beta_v\\to\\infty$. For a toy chain with one neuron per layer, one data point, a Heaviside activation and binary weights, decimating every second layer gives an exact three-coupling flow whose fixed points form a plane, so no transition occurs. Scaling laws are then illustrated at a fictitious fixed point that needs imaginary couplings. One Monte Carlo run compares the network with its dynamic-neuron version."

- RG record: (a) sense (b), RG over depth: real-space decimation (ratio $p=2$) of a chain whose Hamiltonian, after neurons are promoted to dynamic variables (Eqs. (14)-(16)), is invariant under layer translation; the object is a Gibbs ensemble $e^{-\beta H}$ of the weights (Eqs. (5)-(7)), not a field theory and not a training dynamics. (b) Integrated out: the weights, biases and dynamic neurons of every second layer, halving the depth (Eqs. (21)-(25)). (c) What flows: the three couplings $(A,B,C)$ of $-\beta H=\sum_m(Ah\sigma+Bh+C\sigma+D)$ (Eq. (26)) under the map Eq. (27), fixed-point condition Eq. (28), for $N=1$, one data point, Heaviside activation, $W,b\in\{-1,1\}$, $h\in\{0,1\}$; fixed points are the plane $e^{A^*}=1$ (Eq. (29), flows converge, no transition) and a fictitious $(e^{A^*},e^{B^*},e^{C^*})=(3,-2/3,-1/3)$ (Eq. (30)) with linearisation $2\cdot\mathbb 1$ (Eq. (34)), $y_A=y_B=y_C=1$ and scaling laws Eqs. (37)-(40). (d) The decimation of the toy model is an exact finite sum inside the soft-constraint model, with no expansion in a small parameter and no loop or $1/N$ corrections; maturity is derivation for the toy case plus one Monte Carlo comparison (Fig. 7), while the paper states that the approach extends to general width, depth, data and activation (Sec. IV) without computing it, and notes that decimation then becomes hard because the number of couplings grows.
- Read the arXiv HTML v2 in full (Secs. I-VI, Apps. A-B) and checked Fig. 7 in the PDF. v2 is the version matching the journal. `learning_rule` is a judgement call: the paper starts from GD (Eq. (3)) and Langevin (Eq. (4)) but then assumes the long-time Gibbs law (Eq. (5), footnote 4) and analyses only its partition function, so it is placed under `bayes` by the INGEST rule for a Langevin run to equilibrium. The weights carry a flat or binary measure with no prior-likelihood split and no predictor, hence mechanism `not-analysed` and `moves_mean: unknown`. Eq. (41) in App. A proposes GD on $H_{\rm new}$ as an implementation and is not analysed.
- Setting choices: the paper's $L$ is the number of data points and $M$ the depth; in the map's scaling block $L$ is depth and $P$ the samples. Dials follow the toy example and Fig. 7 ($\beta=\beta_v=1$, one data point, one neuron per layer); the general formulation in Sec. III holds for any $N$, $M$, number of data (r1 override). `closure: exact` records that the decimation sums (Eqs. (21), (22)) are finite sums; the Gaussian replacement of the Dirac delta (Eq. (12)) is recorded under `approximations: other` (the authors say in footnote 5 it may not be strictly valid). The equivalence DNA $=$ DNN holds only for $\beta_v\to\infty$ (Eq. (13)); Fig. 7 uses $\beta_v=1$.
- Inconsistencies and limits in the paper: Eq. (21) writes the summed terms as $H_{m+1}$ and $H_m$, while Eq. (23) (defined through $W^{(m-1)}$, $h^{(m-1)}$) implies $H_{m-1}$ and $H_m$. With $d=1$ and $y_B=1$ the exponent in Eq. (40) is zero, and the paper describes these values as trivial for this simple system. The only fixed points with real couplings are the plane of Eq. (29); the critical analysis in Sec. V rests entirely on the fictitious point of Eq. (30) with negative $e^{B^*},e^{C^*}$. The Monte Carlo of Fig. 7 gives no algorithm, number of samples or equilibration check (`mixing: low` is a placeholder for this). App. B (Eq. (43)) argues for a spin-glass-like limit under sigmoid, linearisation and regularisation assumptions; it is illustrative and not recorded as a result.
