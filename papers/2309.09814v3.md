summary: "Deep kernel machines treat the layer Gram matrices of an infinite-width deep GP as parameters, optimised under an objective that keeps the likelihood term and adds KL penalties towards the NNGP kernel. The paper builds a convolutional kernel on Gram matrices with spatial structure and develops an inter-domain inducing-point scheme that avoids inverting huge image-sized matrices. It studies the regularisation strength, normalisation, likelihood and top layer, finding that a small penalty is best and that infinite penalty (the NNGP) is worse. The largest ResNet-style model reaches 99.3% on MNIST, 92.7% on CIFAR-10 and 72% on CIFAR-100, state of the art for kernel methods."

- Read v3 HTML in full for Secs. 1–6; the appendices (derivations of the kernel convolution, normalisation, likelihoods) were not checked line by line.
- The regime tag R3 and `inf-meanfield-fixedP` follow from the DKM being the infinite-width limit with the likelihood scaled so that representations stay flexible; the paper takes this from Yang et al. rather than deriving it, and does not state a parametrisation, hence `not-stated`. In r2 the endpoint $\nu=\infty$ is the NNGP limit, so `inf-kernel` is added there.
- The paper trains Gram-matrix parameters with Adam on an ELBO-type objective; this is variational inference, not a gradient-descent theory, so the learning rule is `bayes`.
- `label_noise: not-stated`: the Gaussian-likelihood variant is only relegated to the appendix, and the headline experiments use a categorical likelihood.
- op-04 is listed as touched: the entry gives empirical learning curves for a rich-regime Bayesian method on real data but no closed-form theory.
- Abstract says 'around 99%' on MNIST; the body gives 99.3%.
