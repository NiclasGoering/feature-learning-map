summary: "The paper compares the renormalisation group (RG) with deep learning, treating depth as scale. It takes the multiscale entanglement renormalisation ansatz (MERA), a hierarchy of local maps that rebuilds a lattice state scale by scale, and replaces its quantum isometries by stochastic maps, giving a generative hierarchical Bayesian network (CORA) whose layers invert RG coarse-graining steps. Marginals over any $L$ sites are evaluated exactly, without sampling, in time $e^L\\log N$. If the data law is fixed by local correlations, the local maps are fitted by maximising the likelihood of local reduced states; for general states, layers are trained in sequence against coarse-grained data. The paper is a short algorithm proposal with no theorem, numerics or neural-network analysis."

- RG record: sense (d), learning as RG on data (coarse-graining of a hierarchical generative model); the paper is not Gaussian, not a field theory and says nothing about neural networks. What is coarse-grained: blocks of $2^d$ lattice sites carrying random variables, mapped to coarser lattices $\Gamma_j$ by stochastic maps whose inverses $\pi_j: S(\Gamma_j)\to S(\Gamma_{j-1})$ form the generative layers (Sec. 2, unnumbered displayed equations, Figs. 1-2). What flows: no flow equation is written; the hierarchy of effective states over $\sim\log N$ scales (Sec. 1) is represented by the layers $\pi_j$, trained scale by scale (Sec. 3, 3.1), and marginals are evaluated exactly within the ansatz, with no perturbative or asymptotic order. Maturity: a short algorithm proposal built on an analogy with the RG, with no equation numbers, theorem, numerics or experiment, and no step beyond the first description of the method.
- Read: the full HTML v4 and the 7-page PDF (nothing skimmed; the paper has three sections, one subsection, three figures and no numbered equations). `source.loc` therefore cites sections and figures and the two unnumbered displayed equations of Sec. 2; anchors are HTML ids (`S2.Ex2` is the second displayed equation, `S2.p10`, `S3.p1`, `S3.p4` are paragraphs, `S3.SS1` is Sec. 3.1). `status: phys` is used for all five results only because the vocabulary has no value for a construction or proposal; nothing is proved and nothing is tested.
- Judgement calls: `learning_rule: gd` because the parameters are fitted by optimising a likelihood cost (the paper leaves the optimiser open: "the optimization procedure must be slightly modified"), which is neither a posterior nor a gradient-flow analysis; `gd` avoids forcing a Bayes regime tag, and `gd_step_size` and `gd_time` are `not-stated`. `depth: deep` because the model has $O(\log N)$ layers; `width_limit: finite-exact` because the cost claims are exact counts with no limit taken. `N` in the paper is the number of lattice sites, not a width. Mechanism is `not-analysed` throughout: the remarks on emergent order parameters and "higher-order concepts" (Sec. 1) are an analogy, not an analysed change of features, so no mechanism file is added. Thread `hierarchical-data` is used because the model is a scale-by-scale generative hierarchy, although the data themselves are generic lattice states.
- Venue is `preprint`: neither the arXiv record nor the first page of the PDF names a venue or journal reference. Relations are empty: the paper (2013) predates every entry in the map and cites only older sources (Wilson, Vidal, Landon-Cardinal and Poulin and others), none of which is in the map.
