summary: "The paper runs full-batch Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for modern networks (ResNet-20-FRN on CIFAR, CNN-LSTM on IMDB), parallelised over hundreds of TPUs, as a reference for the Bayesian neural network posterior. A single long chain mixes well in function space, though not in weight space. At temperature one the Bayesian model average beats SGD and deep ensembles in accuracy and log likelihood; on IMDB $T=1$ is the best temperature, and with the earlier authors' SGHMC code the CIFAR-10 cold posterior effect appears only with data augmentation. Short chains over a range of prior variances all beat SGD. The BNN generalises poorly under covariate shift, which a very cold posterior repairs for a small MLP on MNIST, and deep ensembles approximate its predictive distribution about as well as SGLD and better than variational inference."

- Read the main text of v1 (Secs. 1-10) and App. A (hyper-parameters), App. F (robustness, MNIST at $T=10^{-3}$) and App. G (cold posteriors, Table 7). The arXiv record gives no venue; the paper appeared at ICML 2021 (PMLR 139).
- The CIFAR-10 augmentation study (Table 7) uses the SGHMC-CLR-Prec code of Wenzel et al., not the paper's full-batch HMC; the HMC temperature sweep is on IMDB only (single chain, 40 samples per $T$). The prior variance of that sweep is $1/40$ in Sec. 7 but $1/50$ in App. A, and the Fig. 8 caption says a deep ensemble of 10 where the text says 50. Eq. (4) tempers likelihood and prior together ($T_{\rm post}$).
- Regime tag R6 is a judgement call, as no regime fits a non-asymptotic posterior over a ResNet. `high-dim` is put on r1 only to satisfy the tag rule.
- Mixing is `low`: HMC with Metropolis correction, up to $10^5$ leapfrog steps per sample, T=1, and multi-chain R-hat. The paper itself says mixing cannot be certified; weight-space R-hat values are large for some parameters, and only function-space mixing is claimed. The results that compare with SGD, ensembles and SGLD are marked `compared_other_rule: "yes"` and so use `[bayes, gd]` through overrides.
- The prior-robustness result (Figs. 9, Table 2; variance, mixture of Gaussians, logistic priors) is mentioned in r2 but has no separate result slot. The architecture is `other` for the CNN-LSTM. The Swish activation is recorded as `other`.
- The UCI regression networks are one-hidden-layer with 50 units (Fig. 5), so `depth` lists both values; no result isolates them.
