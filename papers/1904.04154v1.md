summary: "The paper studies the temperature-adjusted posterior $\\propto e^{-E/T}\\mathrm{prior}$ of neural-network classifiers on small stratified MNIST subsets. A replica-exchange Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler explores many temperatures at once. For a network with three logistic hidden layers the test error has a clear minimum near or above $T=1$, below that of standard optimisation, and the best $T$ depends on network and data set. Training error rises gradually with $T$ with no sharp transition, and a low-capacity variant shows no such minimum. Thermodynamic integration gives the model evidence, which favours a one-hidden-layer network over a three-hidden-layer one for 500 images."

- Read the full paper; the algorithm boxes and the thermodynamic-integration details were skimmed.
- Regime tag R1 and `finite-proportional` are the closest schema group for a Bayes paper at finite width and small data; the paper does not analyse proportional scaling. They are chosen only because Bayes entries need a regime tag.
- r1 compares sampling with standard optimisation (a GD-type baseline), so its learning rule is overridden to `[bayes, gd]`.
- The prior is uniform in a box of width set from standard initialisation scale (factor 50 or 1000 wider), not Gaussian.
- Replica exchange makes mixing at low $T$ a question; the paper gives no diagnostics, so mixing is entered as `low` on the basis of the exchange scheme only.
