summary: "A two-layer network takes one gradient step on the first layer with step size growing as $n^\\alpha$, then fits the readout by ridge regression on fresh data, with $n$, $d$ and width proportional and a single-index target. For $\\alpha$ in $((\\ell-1)/(2\\ell),\\ell/(2\\ell+2))$ the feature matrix equals the untrained one plus $\\ell$ rank-one spikes along $(X\\beta)^{\\circ k}$, which carry the first $\\ell$ polynomial components of the target. Equivalence theorems replace the trained features by this form, and Gaussian equivalence on the untrained part gives limiting training and test errors. At $\\ell=1$ only the linear part is learned; at $\\ell=2$ the quadratic part is fit as well."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 6 with the theorem statements and proof ideas; the appendices (J to N) were not read. Result r4 mixes a theorem (Thm 4.5 and Thm K.1 give the constancy within regimes) and simulations (Fig. 3); I recorded it as `num` because the figure is the evidence cited for the staircase picture.
- Protocol: a first half of the data drives one gradient step on $W$ with the readout fixed at its Gaussian initialisation; the second half fits the readout by ridge regression. Conditions 2.1 to 2.4 require $\mathbb E\sigma(Z)=0$ and bounded derivatives; the activation need not be odd.
- Thm 4.2 (training loss) and Thm 4.5 (test error) are stated for $\ell=1,2$ only; the spectrum results hold for all $\ell$.
- The paper shows that when $c_{\star,1}=0$ the one-step update changes nothing at this sample size, and cites other work that single-index targets with higher information exponent need more than order $d$ samples.
- The arXiv record has no journal reference, hence `preprint`.
