one_line: "A restricted Boltzmann machine learns real-space RG filters by maximising mutual information between a block and its environment; tested on 2D Ising and dimer samples."
summary: "The paper trains a restricted Boltzmann machine to find the real-space coarse-graining of a classical lattice system from Monte Carlo samples, without knowing the Hamiltonian. Filters map a small visible block to hidden spins and are chosen by stochastic gradient ascent on a Monte Carlo estimate of the mutual information between the hidden spins and the surrounding environment. On two-dimensional Ising samples it recovers Kadanoff block spins, and on dimer samples it finds local electric-field variables and ignores added noise. Iterated, it reproduces the Ising RG flow and gives $\\nu\\approx1.0\\pm0.15$. Analytic support is limited to a one-dimensional Ising comparison of three filters and heuristic arguments that the criterion gives short-range effective Hamiltonians."

- RG record: sense (d), learning as RG on data: an information-theoretic real-space RG (RSMI) in which an RBM learns the block-to-hidden-spin filters of a classical lattice model from Monte Carlo samples by maximising mutual information with the environment. What is integrated out: the visible-block variables not carried by the hidden spins $\mathcal H$ (Eqs. (1), (6)). What flows: the learned filters, retrained at every step, and the effective temperature read off from correlations or the information proxy $A_\Lambda$ (Fig. 5); no flow equation is derived, the exact-RG statement is Eqs. (17)–(18), and the claim that information-optimal filters give a compact $\tilde H$ is argued only (Eq. (19), Eqs. (41)–(44)), checked analytically for the 1D Ising chain (Eqs. (30)–(40)) and numerically in 2D. Order: no perturbative expansion of any kind, nothing beyond first order; maturity is an algorithm with numerics plus a heuristic argument.
- Read in full: the arXiv v2 HTML, main text plus the Supplemental Materials (Eqs. (1)–(44), Figs. 1–11). Theoretical (physics-level argument, no theorem or proof anywhere): the MI objective and its tractable estimator (Eqs. (1), (6)–(15)); exactness of RG for any filter (Eqs. (17)–(18)); the intuitive equivalence of MI maximisation with a compact-$\tilde H$ RG scheme (Eq. (19) and the section "Relation of Mutual Information RG procedure to conventional RG schemes"); the 1D Ising three-filter comparison (Eqs. (20)–(40)); MI saturation implying a nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian (Eqs. (41)–(44)); a boundary-coupling argument for 2D Ising blocks (no equation); the finite-size scaling form Eq. (16). Numerical: the Ising and dimer filters (Figs. 2, 4, 10), convergence (Fig. 6), the RG flow and exponent (Figs. 5, 8, 9), the contrastive-divergence comparison (Fig. 11).
- Learning-theory relevance, judged honestly: essentially none. There is no generalisation error, sample complexity, training-dynamics analysis or feature-learning mechanism; the RBM is trained by SGD on an unsupervised objective and the filters are reported as outcomes, so every mechanism is `not-analysed` (T6 as the closest type, `moves_mean: unknown` because there is no predictor). It bears on the map only as an information-theoretic route to learning an RG map, and through its claim that KL-trained RBMs do not do RG. No mechanism file was added.
- Inconsistencies in the paper: Eq. (13) replaces the log of an average by an average of the log with no error estimate; Eq. (28) has a relative sign that differs from Eq. (26); Eq. (34) has $\langle\Delta E\rangle$ where Eq. (33) has $\langle-\Delta E\rangle$; Eq. (37) writes arctanh where tanh would be expected; Eq. (10) sums $v_j$ over $i$. The 1D result compares three ansätze and is not a proof that decimation is the global maximiser. The text's pointers to supplement figures are off (convergence figure called Fig. 5, data collapse called Fig. 4, CD-RBM filters called Fig. 6; printed numbers are Figs. 6, 9, 11), and `source.loc` follows the printed captions.

(15). `diagnostics: other` is the saturation of $A_\Lambda$ (Fig. 6). `D: fixed`: the visible block is the network input, sizes up to $8\times8$.
