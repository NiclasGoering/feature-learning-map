one_line: "Derives data-adaptive kernel predictors for infinite-width μP networks at fixed $P$: Bayesian posteriors via a min-max kernel saddle, weight-decayed gradient flow via DMFT."
summary: "For deep μP networks with output scale $\\gamma_0\\sqrt N$ and a fixed number $P$ of training points, the paper derives infinite-width predictors that are kernel regression with data-adapted kernels. The Bayesian posterior, with likelihood scaled by $\\beta\\gamma_0^2N$, gives the aNBK predictor from a min-max saddle over layer and dual kernels, solved by importance sampling over non-Gaussian single-site densities. Gradient flow with weight decay converges instead to the aNTK predictor built from the final adaptive NTK of the training DMFT, a different kernel. For deep linear networks the saddle is algebraic and kernel–label overlaps grow exponentially with layer index. On two-class CIFAR-10 and MNIST subsets with $P\\le10^3$ both theories match trained networks and beat lazy NNGP predictors at large $P$."

- Read arXiv v2 (10 Sep 2025) in full from the HTML version, Appendices A–H included, and checked the equation numbering against the v2 PDF. The proceedings page lists the paper in ICML 2025 (PMLR 267, pp. 32617–32648); the arXiv record has no journal reference.
- Paper-level `learning_rule`, `depth`, `order_of_limits` and the GD step/time fields cover the whole paper, and each result narrows them with overrides. The GD-only result r2 sets the Bayes dials to `not-applicable`, and the Bayes-only results r1 and r3 do the same for GD step size and time. Figure 3 sits inside Sec. 4 in the HTML (anchor `S4.F3`), although Sec. 5 discusses it.
- Solver `other` for aNTK: the DMFT field dynamics are integrated in time with $2\times10^4$ Monte Carlo field samples, implemented only for $L=1$ (App. G.3). It is graded C by analogy with inner Monte Carlo. The aNBK solver alternates gradient ascent on the dual kernels with descent on the kernels, estimating $\mathcal{Z}_\ell$ by importance sampling with $B=2\times10^4$ samples (App. G.2). For $L=5$ the theory–Langevin layer alignments are 73–97% (Fig. 4). Fig. 7 was produced by warm-starting the solver from perturbed empirical Langevin kernels (App. A.1).
- Mixing is set to `high`: Langevin runs use optax `noisy_sgd` with $\beta=50$ ($T=0.02$, slightly above the vocabulary's $10^{-2}$), no Metropolis correction, no autocorrelation or R-hat diagnostics, and equilibrium judged by agreement with the theory. `label_noise: gaussian` means the Gaussian likelihood implied by squared loss at inverse temperature $\beta\gamma_0^2N$; no noise is added to the ±1 labels.
- R5: $\gamma_0$ multiplies $yy^\top$ in the action, so the small- and large-$\gamma_0$ limits of Eq. (17) are recorded as amplitude limits. App. A.5 gives the $O(\gamma_0^2)$ expansion, which the paper says agrees with Zavatone-Veth et al. (2022a). Eqs. (49)–(51) are printed without the $\beta^{-1}$ term. The large-$L$ asymptotic is recorded as `L: to-infinity` for r3 but not as width_limit `inf-depth`. Stated widths vary: $N=5000$ (Figs. 1, 3), $N=1024$/1028 (Figs. 2, 5; App. G.4).
