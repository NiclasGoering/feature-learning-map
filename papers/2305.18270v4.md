summary: "The paper trains the first layer of a two-layer network on Gaussian multi-index targets with full-batch gradient steps on fresh batches, then fits the second layer by ridge regression. With one giant step, $n=\\Theta(d)$ aligns the weights with a single direction, the linear part of the target, and learning more directions needs $n=\\Theta(d^\\ell)$ with $\\ell$ the leap index; with $n=O(d^{\\ell-\\delta})$ nothing is learned. With a few steps and $n=\\Theta(d)$, nested subspaces are learned along a staircase. For bounded width, the risk is bounded below by what the learned subspace can express, and for $n,p=\\Theta(d)$ one step does no better than kernels off the learned direction."

- Read the abstract, Secs. 1 to 3 including all theorem statements and the discussion of related work; the appendices (proofs, numerics) were skimmed only. The arXiv record gives the journal reference as "JMLR 25 (2004)", which is a typo for 2024 (the paper's header shows volume 25, 2024).
- Training protocol: layerwise, with fresh batches for each first-layer step and an independent batch for second-layer ridge regression (Assumption 2); the second layer is frozen at initialisation during first-layer steps. Initialisation is symmetrised so that the network output is zero at start.
- Theorems 4, 5 and 7 are stated in the regime $p=O(1)$ (fixed width); Conjecture 3.5 is proved only for $\kappa_1=\kappa_2=1$ via Thm 10 and Cor 11, which use $n,p=\Theta(d)$. Thm 10 is given informally in the main text; the full statement is in App. C.
- `rank-one-spike` for r5 follows the wording in the related-work section that Ba et al. show an approximately rank-one change; Thm 5 for $\ell=1$ gives a one-dimensional learned space. The label `relevant-subspace-alignment` is the one defined by 2206.15144v1 in this same pull request.
- `op-06` is listed because the paper gives gradient-descent sample complexities as a function of the leap index; it has no Bayesian counterpart.
