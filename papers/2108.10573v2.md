summary: "The paper defines the staircase property for functions on the Boolean hypercube: every nonzero Fourier monomial of degree at least two contains a nonzero monomial with one variable fewer. For a regular, sparse, random network with quadratic activations, zero initialisation and layer-wise stochastic coordinate descent, it proves polynomial time and sample complexity for sparse staircase polynomials. Each trained neuron multiplies the outputs of two earlier active neurons, so the network climbs the staircase one degree per step. Experiments with a ReLU ResNet show that a staircase of degree 10 is learned while the single degree-10 monomial is not. Extensions to biased inputs and general orthonormal bases are discussed."

- Read Secs 1 to 4 in full. Appendix B (formal theorem) and the proofs were not read line by line.
- Theorem 2.2 trains a sparse random network with quadratic activations, zero initialisation and perturbations to escape saddles, layer by layer with block coordinate descent. This is "regular" in the paper's sense but far from standard training; the experiments use ReLU ResNets with Gaussian initialisation instead. The paper default records the theorem's setting, with `relu` as an extra activation for the experiments.
- The paper calls the architecture a regular network; I entered `resnet` and `other` because the neurons receive both input and earlier-layer connections.
- The number of layers and the width are only polynomially bounded; depth is set to deep, since the construction needs about as many layers as the target degree.
- Open problem op-06 names Abbe et al. 2021 as a gradient-descent result on staircase targets with no Bayesian analogue.
