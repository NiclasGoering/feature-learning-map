summary: "The paper measures the average sensitivity of the Boolean functions represented by random Transformers and LSTMs, and during training on random labels and sparse targets. Random Transformers with uniform or Gaussian weights concentrate on low-sensitivity functions much more than LSTMs; with Xavier initialisation both are low, with Transformers lower. Both models learn functions of increasing sensitivity, and Transformers end at lower sensitivity. On sparse parities, majorities, dictators and random juntas with up to 30% label noise, Transformers generalise near perfectly while LSTMs interpolate and stay near chance. The authors state that the sensitivity bias does not directly explain the sparse-function results."

- Read the main text; Appendices B-H (other complexity measures, phase-transition and grokking experiments, generalisation bounds from sensitivity) were not read.
- `learning_rule: [gd]` is a judgement call: the random-model results are about the distribution at initialisation, which the paper treats as a prior over functions, but no posterior or Bayes-optimal quantity is computed. This avoids a regime tag that would not fit.
- Target for r4 is `[polynomial, other]` as a rough label for majority, dictator and juntas; none of the vocabulary values matches exactly. Label noise is random flipping of training labels, recorded as `other`. The sparse majority result also covers dictator (single relevant bit).
- The paper warns that Transformers fail on larger $n$ and $k$ (for $n=100,k=5$ no good generalisation), so r3 does not claim polynomial sample complexity.
