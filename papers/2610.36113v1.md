summary: "The paper treats grokking of a bias-free two-layer quadratic network on modular addition ($p=97$, $N=500$, AdamW with weight decay) as a critical transition. Over 30 seeds, the total squared weight norm is the order parameter, cross-seed variances are proxies for susceptibility and heat capacity, and epoch and weight decay play temperature and field. Hidden neurons are ordered along the Fiedler vector of the loss-Hessian coupling matrix in per-neuron norm coordinates and merged pairwise into block spins. A weighted regression on 65 trained configurations gives a local two-coupling map whose eigenvalues give two relevant exponents and, through standard scaling relations with $d=1$, five critical exponents that agree with direct power-law fits within 1–10%. The map is fitted rather than derived; a finite-size-scaling check across seven moduli gives $\\nu$ within 3%."

- RG record: Sense (b) only weakly and none of (a)–(e) fits well: it is an empirical real-space block-spin RG on the hidden neurons of one trained two-layer network, with grokking read as a continuous critical point in epoch and weight decay. Coarse-grained: neuron pairs adjacent in a Fiedler ordering of the loss-Hessian couplings $|J_{ij}|$ (Eqs. (5)–(6)) are merged into block spins with $b=2$ (Eq. (8)); no integral over fluctuations is done (Eq. (7) is only recalled). What flows: $(K_1,K_2)$, the mean first and second off-diagonal coupling bands (Eq. (9)), under a locally fitted affine one-step map $K'=c+TK$ (Eqs. (10)–(11)) with $y_t=0.6923$, $y_h=0.6515$ (Eq. (12)), no fixed point located; five exponents follow from the textbook relations (Eq. (14), App. D Eq. (36)) assuming homogeneity (Eq. (13)) and $d=1$. Order: second-order Taylor expansion of the loss plus a linearised map, nothing beyond first order; maturity is numerics plus analogy, since the relations are textbook, $y_t$, $y_h$ and the direct exponents are fitted without error bars, and the 1–10% agreement (Table 1) compares two empirical fits whose scaling identities hold by construction only on the RG side.
- Derived versus fitted (for the checker). Derived: the closed form Eq. (6) for $J_{ij}$, the variational Fiedler ordering (Eq. (21)), and the scaling relations from Eq. (13) (App. D). Fitted: $\mathbf{T}$ by Gaussian-windowed least squares on 65 pairs $(\mathbf{K}_n,\mathbf{K}'_n)$ (Eq. (10); weights 0.21 at $e=140,150$ and 0.05 at $\lambda=14,16$; condition number 208, App. E); the thermal/magnetic labelling of eigenvectors by alignment with $\partial\mathbf{K}/\partial e$ and $\partial\mathbf{K}/\partial\lambda$ (App. E, Fig. 10); every direct exponent by a log–log fit on a one-sided window (Table 1); $\nu$ by finite-size fit over seven moduli. Assumed: $d=1$, homogeneity (Eq. (13)), epoch as inverse temperature ($\beta_{\rm eff}\propto e$, App. F), weight decay as field $h=-(\lambda-\lambda_c)/2$, and cross-seed variances as susceptibility and heat capacity. The paper itself says the ensemble is not Gibbs (quenched seeds and splits) and that AdamW's decoupled decay does not literally add $-hS$ to the loss. No uncertainty is reported on $\mathbf{T}$, $y_t$, $y_h$ or any direct exponent.
- Points a checker may want to look at. $\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf{T}=-0.045$, so $\Lambda_\pm\approx\pm|\det\mathbf{T}|^{1/2}$ (paper's own remark, Sec. 3.3); the negative thermal eigenvalue is attributed by the authors to the two-band truncation, and they use a two-step $b=4$ map for $y_t$. App. C (Eq. (23)) linearises about a fixed point $\mathbf{K}^*$, whereas the fit expands about the grokking centre with an intercept $c$ and $\mathbf{K}^*$ is never computed. In Fig. 2B $\chi_S$ peaks at $e=118$ and $C_H$ keeps rising through $e_c=145$ and peaks later, so the analysis is aligned at the maximum test-accuracy slope rather than at a fluctuation peak, and the $\alpha$, $\gamma$, $\beta$ windows are $[107,133]$, $[153,171]$, $[180,256]$. In Fig. 5 the straight-line fits sit on visibly curved log–log data for $\beta$ and, less so, $\alpha$. Appendix B notes that true weight rescaling would multiply every off-diagonal $J_{ij}$ by 9 (cubic output scaling of a quadratic neuron); Eq. (6) is the gain-coordinate form and the factor cancels in $\mathbf{T}$. The Fiedler ordering is recomputed for every configuration, so neuron identity is not tracked across epochs or seeds. The abstract speaks of deep networks; the model is a single-hidden-layer network.
- Comparison with the map's grokking papers. 2310.03789 (Rubin et al.) is a Bayesian/Langevin-equilibrium saddle-point theory of a quadratic two-layer net on modular addition with a first-order transition (T5); this paper is a numerical AdamW study with a continuous-critical-point ansatz and no analytic theory. It cites Rubin et al. only as related work and neither tests for coexistence or hysteresis nor contradicts it, so no relation is recorded. 2407.20199 (Mallinar et al.) is cited as grokking in non-neural models; its Fourier and block-circulant structure is not examined here. The two approaches are not reconciled in the paper.
