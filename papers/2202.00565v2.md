summary: "A two-layer fully-connected network is trained by SGD to discriminate synthetic translation-invariant images that differ in correlation length. When the pixels have non-Gaussian higher-order statistics, half the hidden neurons develop localised receptive fields that tile the input and resemble the filters of a convolutional network. On Gaussian controls with the same covariance this does not happen. A Gaussian-equivalence theory fits the early test error and fails when the weights localise. A single-neuron gradient-flow model shows that localisation is driven by the fourth-order input cumulant, whose CP decomposition factors localise as the non-Gaussianity grows."

- Read the main text, Methods (training details, Gaussian equivalence, reduced model) and the figure captions; the Supporting Information A.1-A.6 were only skimmed by section title.
- The paper is mostly numerical, and `width_limit: high-dim` is a judgement call (there is no teacher-student limit). The second layer is fixed at $1/K$ in the main text; a plastic second layer is in Fig. S1. SGD uses MSE; cross-entropy and ReLU are said to give the same receptive fields (Sec. A.6).
- New mechanism `cumulant-factor-localisation`: the paper names the fourth-order cumulant and its CP decomposition as what drives localisation, and no existing label covers it.
- Statements r3: the single-neuron model uses a cubic polynomial activation, drops the fifth-order term and fixes the bias at $b=-1$ (Methods). The paper says a full analysis for generic fourth-order tensors is beyond its scope.
- `data.input` is `[structured-gaussian, other]`: a pointwise saturating nonlinearity of a translation-invariant Gaussian field with two correlation lengths. Anchors use arXiv's unnumbered section prefix `Sx2` for the Results section.
