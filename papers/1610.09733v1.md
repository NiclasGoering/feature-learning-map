summary: "The paper treats the eigenmodes of the inverse covariance matrix $K$ of $N$ variables as momentum shells and eliminates the low-variance modes below a moving cutoff, for a near-Gaussian distribution with a weak quartic coupling $g$. At tree level the rescaled coupling $\\tilde g$ flows with an exponent set by the eigenvalue density, $\\alpha-2$ for $\\rho\\propto\\lambda^{\\alpha-1}$, which reproduces the $D<4$ rule for lattice systems. Integrating out the last kept mode gives a one-loop flow with a Wilson–Fisher-like fixed point. The authors propose a data-analysis recipe and apply it to 160 retinal neurons and to NYSE returns: the retinal fourth moments stay well above the Gaussian value, and the stock data separate sampling-noise modes from non-Gaussian ones."

- RG record: Sense (a): Wilsonian momentum-shell RG over eigenmodes of the inverse covariance $K$ of $N$ variables, with a data-analysis flavour of (d), applied to retina and stock-return data. Integrated out: the modes with the largest eigenvalues of $K$ (the smallest-variance principal components), by lowering the cutoff $\Lambda$ of Eqs. (6)-(7), with the rescaling $z_\Lambda$ of Eq. (10) keeping the mean variance fixed. Flows: only the rescaled quartic coupling $\tilde g$ (Eq. (13)), at tree level by Eqs. (14)-(15) ($d\ln\tilde g/d\ln\Lambda=\alpha-2$ for $\rho\propto\lambda^{\alpha-1}$) and at one loop ($O(\tilde g^2)$) by Eqs. (23)-(25) after the slowly-varying approximation of Eqs. (21)-(22); $K$ and its eigenvectors do not flow (the mass-like term in Eq. (19) is only noted). Nothing beyond one loop and no exponents are computed; maturity is a perturbative derivation plus two data analyses (Figs. 1-2) that give qualitative, not quantitative, evidence for a fixed point.
