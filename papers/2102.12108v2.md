summary: "Deep kernel learning places a Gaussian process on a neural-network feature extractor and trains the network weights on the marginal likelihood. The paper shows this can overfit worse than a deterministic network: at the optimum the data-fit term is fixed at $-N/2$, so the complexity penalty dominates and is lowered by correlating all data points. Experiments on a 1D toy problem, UCI regression, UTKFace and CIFAR-10 show that stochastic minibatching, not a better marginal likelihood, makes the method work, and that larger batches hurt. A fully Bayesian treatment of the network weights, by HMC or SGLD, removes the overfitting and gives better uncertainty metrics."

- Read the main text of v2 (Secs. 1-8) from the HTML, including all tables; the appendices (proof of Prop. 1 in App. A, details in Apps. B-C) were not read. The arXiv record gives no venue, so `preprint`.
- This is a Gaussian-process / empirical-Bayes paper, not a posterior-over-weights theory. `learning_rule` is `[bayes, gd]` because the marginal likelihood is trained by gradient methods and the fully Bayesian variant is sampled; r5 is marked as comparing the two. Regime tag R6 is a judgement call to satisfy the Bayes tag rule; `high-dim` sits on r4 only.
- New mechanism `kernel-overcorrelation` (T3) is taken from Sec. 5.1 (Eq. (5) and Remark 3) and the near-unity correlation functions in Fig. 1(b). The paper notes the same effect was concurrently called feature collapse by van Amersfoort et al. 2021. The type T3 is the closest of T0-T6 (the whole kernel adapts), but the mechanism comes from type-II maximum likelihood training, not from a posterior or a dynamics.
- r2's fit statements are read from Fig. 1 only; the paper reports no numerical correlation values. Eq. (5) and Prop. 1 assume a learnable signal variance, which the toy and UCI experiments use.
