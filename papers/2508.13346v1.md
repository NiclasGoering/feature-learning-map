summary: "A short note proves a linear-algebraic lower bound on the expected dimension of a (possibly random) subspace that approximates $N$ unit vectors of a Hilbert space with small average squared error. The bound grows with $N$ and shrinks with the pairwise overlaps of the vectors, and is tight for an orthonormal basis. Because a kernel method on $n$ examples picks its output from an $n$-dimensional subspace, it follows that kernel methods need $n\\ge(1-\\epsilon)N$ samples against $N$ orthonormal targets. For parities on the hypercube this gives $n\\ge(1-\\epsilon)2^d$, or $(1-\\epsilon)\\binom dk$ for $k$-sparse parities, even with non-adaptive membership queries."
