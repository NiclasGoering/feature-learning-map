summary: "The paper re-examines the marginal likelihood (Bayesian evidence) as a tool for hyperparameter learning and model selection. It shows, on Gaussian density estimation, a Fourier model, Gaussian processes and 25 CNN and ResNet architectures, that the marginal likelihood can be negatively correlated with generalisation, and can cause both underfitting and overfitting of hyperparameters. The link to PAC-Bayes bounds explains why: the bound holds only for a model fixed before seeing the data. A conditional marginal likelihood, which drops the first terms of the sequential decomposition, correlates with generalisation across data sizes and improves deep kernel learning on small data."

- Read the main text (Secs. 1-9); the appendices (neural architecture search, calibration, extra GP and DKL results, choice of $m$) were skimmed only through the main-text references.
- This is a Bayesian model-selection paper, not a feature-learning paper. It is placed under `bayes` with regime tag R6 as a judgement call, because no regime fits and the validator needs one. `high-dim` appears only in r1's override, where the data size $n$ is compared with the number of model parameters; this does not mean the paper takes a high-dimensional limit.
- The v3 text is the extended version of the ICML paper (v2); the title on arXiv is unchanged. `venue` is `preprint` because the arXiv record gives no journal reference and the first page states none.
- r1 and r2 point to figures that float into other sections in the HTML (Fig. 4 sits in the Sec. 5 container, Fig. 5 in the Sec. 6 container); the anchors are the figure elements, and the section numbers in `loc` are those of the discussing text.
- Sampling is used only for a supporting estimate of the conditional marginal likelihood with SGLD at $T=1$ (Fig. 7); no equilibrium claim rests on it, so `mixing: none`. Activations and parametrisation of the CIFAR architectures were not checked.
- Eq. (17) is a theorem of Germain et al. (2016) applied here; the $\log k$ cost is the paper's own union-bound argument.
