summary: "The paper studies a two-layer network trained, with all weights learned, on data from a teacher of the same architecture, in the Bayes-optimal setting. Using the interpolation method from spin glasses, it proves that the free entropy, the data-weight mutual information and the Bayes-optimal generalisation error equal those of a noisy generalised linear model, up to an explicit error bound. The equivalence holds when the number of samples is small compared with the hidden width and grows slower than the input dimension to the power 3/2. It gives rigorous fundamental limits for any learning procedure in this regime and confirms a replica-based conjecture."

- Read the v1 HTML for Secs. 1–2 and the statements and remarks of Secs. 3–5; the proofs by interpolation and the concentration lemma were not checked.
- Regime: only the combination in Eq. (19) is needed. Remark 2 says $n$ can grow as fast as $o(d^{3/2})$ but must be much smaller than $p$; so `p_vs_n` is `other` and the entry is under R6 and `high-dim`, as for the other Bayes-optimal teacher-student papers.
- The paper does not use the word 'feature learning'. It is recorded as T0 because the result says the nonlinear network is equivalent to a linear model with effective noise in this regime. The paper notes that for finite $p>1$ (committee machines) no equivalence is expected.
- The explicit GLM error formulas come from an earlier paper (Barbier et al. 2019), so Theorem 3 is only an equivalence; its grade is `A` because the GLM error depends on scalar overlaps only.
- Parametrisation recorded as `ntk` because of the $1/\sqrt p$, $1/\sqrt d$ factors in the forward map; the posterior is Bayes-optimal with matched prior.
- op-04 is listed since the entry gives Bayes-optimal error when the teacher is drawn from the prior (op-04's problem statement).
