one_line: "Bayesian networks with data augmentation built into the likelihood, exact on finite orbits and tightly bounded otherwise, still show a cold posterior effect."
summary: "The paper builds data augmentation into the generative model of Bayesian neural networks by averaging either logits or class probabilities over the augmentation distribution, which gives a valid likelihood. On a finite orbit of augmentations the likelihood is exact, and on the full orbit multi-sample Jensen bounds give tight lower bounds that improve with the number of samples. Gradient-guided Monte Carlo on ResNet-20 and CIFAR-10 shows that the cold posterior effect persists in these principled models, so it is not an artefact of an invalid likelihood. Without augmentation the effect is minimal. The authors suggest that curation or a misspecified prior explains the rest."

- Read the full main text; appendices (kinetic temperature checks, toy invariance posteriors) were skimmed.
- Regime tag R1 and `finite-proportional` are the closest schema group for a Bayes paper at finite width and data; the paper does not analyse proportional scaling. They are chosen only because Bayes entries need a regime tag.
- r3 is an SGD baseline, so its learning rule is overridden to GD with GD-only dials.
- The paper says the finite-orbit logit-averaging setup shows no cold posterior effect (Fig. 3 caption); I report this exception in r2 rather than a blanket statement.
- Relation to 2104.14421 (Izmailov et al.) is by the paper's own framing in Secs. 1 and 6.
- r4 is an expected-gradient argument (full-batch Langevin, App. A), hence `phys`. For a finite uniform orbit, $e^{L_{\rm loss}}=\prod_j p(y_i|a_j(x_i))^{1/K}$, the same stationary target as Kapoor et al. 2203.16481 Eq. (16), which reads it as a likelihood tempered at $T=K$.
- The BNN prior is inherited from the Fortuin et al. code and is not restated; `gaussian-iid` is not confirmed by the text. Sampling quality is checked only by the kinetic-temperature diagnostic (App. C, Fig. 5).
- Sec. 6 argues that the model with augmentation is the right one, so the absence of a cold posterior effect without augmentation is the artefact; Kapoor et al. (Sec. 7) dispute this reading.
