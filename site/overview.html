Who pays for interpolation?

Energy, entropy and feature learning for Bayesian posteriors and gradient descent, on one page. Citations link to the paper's entry in the map when it has one, otherwise to arXiv.

$W^\ell$ weights · $h^\ell$ hidden preactivations · $a$ readout · $N$ width · $L$ depth · $d$ input dimension · $P$ samples $(x_i,y_i)$ · $\varphi$ activation · $\mathcal M$ interpolating set · $\varepsilon_{\rm gen}$ generalisation error

■ entropy (hidden layers) ■ energy (readout) ■ exchange rate $\kappa$ ■ gradient descent

1Setup ▶ Animation

$$h^1=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt d}W^1x,\qquad h^\ell=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt N}W^\ell\varphi(h^{\ell-1}),\qquad f_\theta(x)=\tfrac{1}{\gamma\sqrt N}\,a^\top\varphi\big(h^L(x)\big),$$

prior $p_0$ iid $\mathcal N(0,1)$ on $\theta=(W^1,\dots,W^L,a)$; $\gamma=1$ is NTK/standard, $\gamma=\gamma_0\sqrt N$ is μP (Jacot et al. 2018; Yang & Hu 2021). Interpolating set and layer kernels:

$$\mathcal M=\{\theta:f_\theta(x_i)=y_i\ \forall i\le P\},\qquad K^\ell=\tfrac1N\,\varphi(h^\ell(X))\,\varphi(h^\ell(X))^\top\in\mathbb R^{P\times P},\quad K^0=\tfrac1d XX^\top .$$

2Two learning rules are conditioned priors ▶ Animation

$$p_B(\theta\mid D)=\frac{p_0(\theta)\,\mathbb 1_{\mathcal M}(\theta)}{Z_D(P)},\qquad Z_D(P)=p_0(\mathcal M)$$ $$\htmlClass{c-gd}{p_{GD}(\theta_\infty\mid D)=\int d\theta_0\;p_0(\theta_0)\,\mathbb 1_{\mathcal M}(\theta_\infty)\,\delta\big(\theta_\infty-\Phi_D(\theta_0)\big)}$$

Same prior, same constraint. Bayes weighs each interpolant by its prior volume; GD by the prior volume of its basin under the gradient-flow map $\Phi_D$. Gibbs / Langevin version ▸

Gibbs (noisy) and Langevin version

$p_\beta(\theta\mid D)=p_0(\theta)\,e^{-\beta L_D(\theta)}/Z_\beta$ with $L_D=\tfrac12\sum_i(f_\theta(x_i)-y_i)^2$; $\beta\to\infty$ recovers $p_B$. Langevin dynamics on $\beta L_D-\log p_0$ has stationary law $p_\beta$: taking $t\to\infty$ first and then $T=1/\beta\to0$ gives Bayes; setting $T=0$ first and then $t\to\infty$ gives GD.

$$p_\beta=\arg\min_q\big\{\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p_0)+\beta\,\mathbb E_q[L_D]\big\},\qquad \min=-\log Z_\beta .$$

At large $N$ the functional of §3 is unchanged except $K^L\to K^L+\eta I$ with $\eta=\gamma^2/\beta$: in μP a finite $\eta$ needs $\beta\propto N$, a likelihood scaled with width.

3One variational problem ▶ Animation

Bayes is an I-projection (Csiszár); its minimum is the log prior volume of the interpolants:

$$p_B=\arg\min_q\{\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p_0):q(\mathcal M)=1\},\qquad \min=-\log Z_D(P).$$

Neurons are exchangeable, so Sanov applies layer by layer (Papagiannouli et al. 2026; Andreis et al. 2025; Bordelon & Pehlevan 2022; Lauditi et al. 2025):

$$-\tfrac1N\log Z_D(P)\;\longrightarrow\;F_D=\min_{K^1,\dots,K^L}\ \htmlClass{c-ent}{\sum_{\ell=1}^L J_\ell(K^\ell\mid K^{\ell-1})}\;+\;\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa}\,\htmlClass{c-en}{\tfrac12\,y^\top (K^L)^{-1}y},\qquad \htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa=\tfrac{\gamma^2}{N}}$$

with the cost per neuron of reshaping layer $\ell$'s kernel

$$\htmlClass{c-ent}{J_\ell(K\mid K')}=\min\big\{\mathrm{KL}(\nu\Vert\mathcal N(0,K')):\ \mathbb E_\nu[\varphi(h)\varphi(h)^\top]=K\big\}.$$

Both rules fit one template on the neuron measure $\nu$ (prior $\nu_0$):

$$\nu^\star_R=\arg\min_{\nu:\ f_\nu(X)=y}\ C_R(\nu\mid\nu_0)$$ $$C_B=\mathrm{KL}(\nu\Vert\nu_0),\qquad \htmlClass{c-gd}{C_{GD}(\nu\mid\nu_0)=\text{cost of reaching }\nu\text{ by gradient flow from }\nu_0}$$

$C_{GD}$ is exact in three cases. Lazy: $\tfrac12\mathbb E\Vert\theta-\theta_0\Vert^2$ ($\approx W_2^2$, kernel frozen at the NTK; Chizat et al. 2019). Weight decay: $\mathbb E_\nu\Vert\theta\Vert^2$, giving variation and $R_L$ norms (Bach 2017; Ongie & Parhi 2026) and a different adaptive kernel from Bayes (Lauditi et al. 2025). Noise, then $t\to\infty$: KL, i.e. Bayes (Hu et al. 2019; Chizat 2022). Elsewhere schematic.

4The readout is energy, the kernels are entropy ▶ Animation

Given features $\Phi=\varphi(h^L(X))$ the readout is Gaussian, so its cost of interpolating is a quadratic form:

$$\min_m\Big\{\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathcal N(m,I)\Vert\mathcal N(0,I)\big):\tfrac{1}{\gamma\sqrt N}\Phi m=y\Big\}=\tfrac{\gamma^2}{2}\,\htmlClass{c-en}{y^\top (K^L)^{-1}y}=N\,\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa}\,\htmlClass{c-en}{E(K^L)},$$

the squared RKHS norm of the interpolant in the last-layer kernel: the cost of fitting with fixed features (lazy GD gives the same with $\tfrac12|a-a_0|^2$ and $y\to y-f_0$). Hidden layers are non-Gaussian after conditioning, so they pay a genuine entropy. The stationary point is a tilted single-site law,

$$\nu^\star_L(h)\propto\mathcal N(h;0,K^{L-1})\,e^{\frac{\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa}}{2}\,(v^\top\varphi(h))^2},\qquad v=(K^L)^{-1}y,$$

so neurons aligned with the residual direction $v$ are up-weighted and the kernel grows along the target. For small $\kappa$ (the adaptive-kernel corrections of Naveh & Ringel 2021; Seroussi et al. 2023):

$$\delta K^L\approx\tfrac{\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa}}{2}\,\mathrm{Cov}_0\big(\varphi\varphi^\top,(v^\top\varphi)^2\big).$$

5Feature learning needs an extensive $Z$ ▶ Animation

$$-\log Z_D(P)=N\,F_D+o(N):\qquad \text{features move by }O(1)\iff -\log Z_D(P)=\Theta(N).$$

The labels must be exponentially atypical under the prior at width $N$.

  • NTK, $P$ fixed: $-\log Z_D=\tfrac12y^\top K_0^{-1}y+O(P)=O(1)$, so $F_D\to0$ and the posterior is the NNGP posterior (T0; Hron et al. 2020).
  • μP: $\Theta(N)$ through $\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa=\gamma_0^2}$: the full tilt (T3; Lauditi et al. 2025).
  • NTK, $P\propto N$: $\Theta(N)$ through $\tfrac12\log\det K^L$: only the kernel scale moves (T1; Li & Sompolinsky 2021).
Animation: which scalings make −log Z extensive

6NTK ↔ μP: $\kappa$ is the exchange rate ▶ Animation

  • $\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa\to0}$ (NTK): moving a kernel costs $O(N)$, the whole readout bill is $O(1)$, so $\delta K=O(1/N)$, the perturbative $1/N$ corrections (Yaida 2020); the predictor is NNGP regression. All energy.
  • $\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa=\gamma_0^2}$ (μP): both bills are $O(N)$; interior optimum, the full tilt.
  • $\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa\to\infty}$ (large amplitude): hidden layers pay whatever makes $y$ cheap; the kernel aligns with $yy^\top$, the ReLU single-site law collapses onto atoms, and the cost becomes a variation or weight-decay norm (T4; Vogel 2024; Shang et al. 2026). Entropy buys the fit.
$$\mathcal E(J)=\min\big\{\htmlClass{c-en}{\tfrac12y^\top(K^L)^{-1}y}:\ \htmlClass{c-ent}{\textstyle\sum_\ell J_\ell}\le J\big\}$$

On this Pareto front the optimum sits where the slope is $-1/\kappa$: NTK is the $J=0$ corner, μP an interior point, $\kappa\to\infty$ the far end. The GD counterpart of this switch is the lazy-to-rich transition driven by output or initialisation scale (Woodworth et al. 2020).

7Generalisation is a variational quantity ▶ Animation

$\varepsilon_{\rm gen}(f)=\mathbb E_{x\sim\mu}\big[(f(x)-y(x))^2\big]$ and bad set $B_\varepsilon=\{\theta:\varepsilon_{\rm gen}(f_\theta)\ge\varepsilon\}$.

$$p_B(B_\varepsilon\mid D)=\frac{p_0(\mathcal M\cap B_\varepsilon)}{p_0(\mathcal M)}\asymp e^{-N\,\Gamma_D(\varepsilon)},$$ $$\Gamma_D(\varepsilon)=\inf\{\mathcal E_\kappa(f):f(X)=y,\ \varepsilon_{\rm gen}(f)\ge\varepsilon\}-\inf\{\mathcal E_\kappa(f):f(X)=y\},$$ $$\mathcal E_\kappa(f)=\min\big\{\htmlClass{c-ent}{\textstyle\sum_\ell J_\ell}+\htmlClass{c-kap}{\kappa}\,\htmlClass{c-en}{E(K^L)}:\ K^L(\cdot,X)(K^L)^{-1}y=f\big\}$$ $$\htmlClass{c-gd}{p_{GD}(B_\varepsilon\mid D)=p_0\big(\Phi_D^{-1}(B_\varepsilon)\big)\asymp e^{-N\,\Gamma^{GD}_D(\varepsilon)}}$$

The first line is the classical recipe of statistical learning theory (Seung, Sompolinsky & Tishby 1992; Haussler et al. 1996). $\Gamma_D$ is the bad-completion gap: the extra free energy a bad interpolant pays over the best one. The typical error is $\varepsilon_{\rm gen}(f^\star)$ at the minimiser, $f^\star=K^{L\star}(\cdot,X)(K^{L\star})^{-1}y$. $\Gamma^{GD}_D$ is the same with $C_{GD}$ in place of KL (schematic).

Bayes generalises by the volume of bad interpolants, GD by the volume of their basins. They agree iff $(\Phi_D)_\sharp\,p_0=p_0(\cdot\mid\mathcal M)$: true for lazy readout-only training, sample-then-optimise (He et al. 2020), false in the rich regime. How far apart they are is the volume question (Mingard et al. 2021; Peleg & Hein 2024).

Status: the Gibbs principle and the Gaussian-readout identity are standard; the large-$N$ rate is proved at kernel level for fixed $P$; the deep $J_\ell$ form is the μP Bayesian action; $C_{GD}$ is exact only in the three listed cases. $\Gamma_D$, the energy–entropy reading and the extensivity criterion are the map authors' own framing and not yet published.
