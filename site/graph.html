Most papers in this literature state the same few results in different notations. Each dot is one statement: a theorem or an established physics result, written once in the map's notation, with the papers that state it. Size is the number of papers; a filled dot is a collapse (the papers' results are one formula under a mapping of symbols), a ring a grouping (a shared object, not shown equal). Arrows join a statement to the one it becomes in a limit, labelled with that limit; red marks papers that disagree; diamonds are open problems in the gaps. Left to right: data per width, from fixed $P$ to $P\propto N$ to high-dimensional $P\sim D^k$. Bottom to top: how far the kernel moves, from lazy ($\kappa\to0$) through $1/N$ corrections to mean field and μP ($\kappa=O(1)$). Click a statement for its formula, conditions and member papers; click a paper for its results. The axes are schematic. The column on the right is the renormalisation-group layer: what each kind of “renormalisation” claim in the literature says, rated for maturity, with lines to the main statements it reproduces, extends or contradicts. Click a line's end to jump either way; every main statement's panel lists its “RG view”.
